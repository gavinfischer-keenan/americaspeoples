# Voyages, trade, North Atlantic communities and shipwrecks

The shared map connects settlement histories to movement by sea and land. Its clock ends in 1934. New future-atlas links are empty map previews; the evidence described here remains in the Americas atlas and its wider connections.

## Trade before 1200

Thirty-eight new source-reviewed observations cover California and Chaco, the Atlantic Southeast, the Caribbean, Mesoamerica, the Andes, Pacific islands, Australia, Africa and Eurasia. Thirty belong before 1200 CE; eight later observations describe Mexica tribute, merchants and Inka transport at their actual dates. The trade movie adds 28 chapters, including BCE exchange. A renewed source review enriches the existing Lake Huron obsidian record with its Oregon connection, keeping the two flakes as one finding and one chapter.

Stone sourcing, imported shells, plant remains and cargoes support movement between places. A connection joins the source area to the find context. It does not establish a direct trip, every intermediate stop, traffic volume or an uninterrupted operating period. Separate activity windows remain separate; state tribute and redistribution are identified in the record rather than treated as ordinary market purchases.

## Voyages and evidence gaps

Fourteen additional itineraries and 145 new dated evidence points expand the inventory, including Schouten and Le Maire’s Eendracht voyage, the separate Amsterdam return and Slocum’s Spray circumnavigation. The expanded expedition inventory also includes Orellana, Alarcón, Champlain, Chirikov, Hearne and Matonabbee, Rae, Bougainville, Dampier and Bellingshausen. Dated anchors and broad expedition activity are separate layers. An expedition can be happening without a recovered position for every day. Unsupported legs remain gaps rather than smooth invented tracks. Date-line ambiguity is retained where it affects the source chronology.

## Norse North Atlantic

The L’Anse aux Meadows guide places the workshops in their wider Iceland–Greenland–Newfoundland setting. The precisely dated 1021 evidence concerns cut wood; it is not a demonstrated first-arrival date. Newfoundland is distinguished from Labrador. Imported wood, butternuts and walrus ivory supply different kinds of movement evidence.

The Western Settlement’s approximate fourteenth-century end and the Eastern Settlement’s approximate fifteenth-century end are separate inferred abandonment horizons. The wedding at Hvalsey on 16 September 1408 is a dated written observation, not proof that the colony ended that day. Iceland remains inhabited through the atlas cutoff. Greenland’s Indigenous and later communities are shown through their own dated records.

Ari’s twelfth-century account of the papar is displayed as later textual testimony about an earlier time. It does not become a precisely dated Irish voyage or an excavated settlement.

## Whole-area maps and seasonal conditions

The Northwest Passage guide includes a 7,200-pixel Northern Canada map, a whole-Arctic IBCAO bathymetric poster and a historical Gjøa chart. The southern route guide includes a whole-Southern-Ocean IBCSO map, the historical Magellan survey and Beagle/Tierra del Fuego mapping. Downloads preserve their source resolution and licensing; enlarging a small archival image does not create detail. L’Anse aux Meadows has both modern terrain context and the published archaeological site plan.

Monthly station normals describe Resolute Bay and Punta Arenas. The Arctic wave table is explicitly a Beaufort Sea study statistic, not conditions in every passage. Southern Ocean wave statistics concern the open ocean, not narrow channels. Modern climatology, historical observations, sea-ice statistics, wave height and wind are labeled separately. No unsupported monthly wave values are filled in.

## Historical shipwreck inventory

The import uses the UK Hydrographic Office’s October 2026 export and public inventories from Ireland, Western Australia, Michigan, Wisconsin and NOAA’s Greater Farallones sanctuary. Additional NOAA Thunder Bay and coastal institutional records supply documented losses around six maritime graveyards. Four excavation-based records introduce Dokos, Uluburun, Cape Gelidonya and Kyrenia. Dokos is a very early lost-cargo assemblage; this is not a claim to have identified the oldest wreck everywhere.

Only a supported loss year, full date or archaeological date interval enters the movie. Discovery, detection, report, designation and survey years never substitute for a sinking date. Unknown dates, dates beyond 1934 and unusable locations remain in the audit rather than receiving invented dots. Rounded public positions may locate reported wreckage, a mooring or an approximate loss locality. Original position qualifiers and catalogue status are retained.

Aliases link overlapping catalogue entries to one loss marker. The source-specific audit counts eligible rows before reconciliation, so those totals must not be added to infer unique vessels. Different sources can disagree: Northern Light retains the unresolved 1880/1881 loss interval, while Mary Rose uses the museum’s contemporary-source chronology for 19 July 1545 and preserves the original catalogue entry separately.

At the loss date a marker brightens, then approaches a subtle background opacity over 25 years. That decay is an editorial display choice. Salvaged or removed historical losses remain visible; a surviving marker is not a claim that a physical hull remains. Wreck groups use anchor symbols and stay separate from settlement clusters.

The resulting coverage is large but incomplete. Hydrographic catalogues concentrate on particular surveyed waters and hazards. Unlocated losses, undated archaeology and sources without reusable locations remain substantial gaps. The retired US AWOIS mirror and monument designation dates were not treated as a complete dated wreck register.

## Ice interpolation

The ice layer blends signed-distance fields between the available reconstructed outlines. It preserves exact source snapshots, does not extrapolate outside their coverage and holds the previous outline while a new bracketing pair loads. The map labels the displayed outline as interpolation and identifies its bracket. This settles distracting jumps without presenting uncertain edges as an exact glacier simulation.

## Reproduction and reuse

`scripts/import-shipwrecks.py` normalizes preserved publisher downloads and produces an inclusion/exclusion audit with source hashes. `scripts/prepare-ancient-wrecks.py` prepares the four independently reviewed archaeological records. `scripts/maritime-trade-scenes.ts` regenerates the topic chapters from canonical evidence.
