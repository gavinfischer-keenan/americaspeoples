## Coverage comparison

### Northern Idaho — early Holocene

Targets the 10,000–6,000 BCE and 6,000–3,000 BCE collection gaps with named river-corridor sites. Wewukiyepuh and Kelly Forks are new locality leads; Cooper’s Ferry already has an Area B record and needs phase-level comparison rather than another counted place.

Review before integration: Separate BP from cal BP and distinct occupation episodes. Older Cooper’s Ferry component dates must not overwrite the existing Area B model. A long site sequence does not establish continuous residence.

### Northern Nevada and Idaho — named sites

Directly targets the northern Nevada 10,000–6,000 BCE gap, with Crypt Cave, Leonard Rockshelter, Grass Valley and Bonneville Estates leads; also complements Idaho. Later Holocene phases remain to be resolved individually.

Review before integration: Differentiate dated activity/cache evidence from habitation. Never use insect-cache or artifact counts as human population. Check revised Leonard Rockshelter chronology and explicit calibration.

### Aleutian occupation history

Provides early/middle Holocene through 1300 CE leads, complementing a broad Alaska row currently containing context but no dated locality IDs.

Review before integration: The report headline extends residence across 10,000 BCE–1300 CE, but its body describes approximately 9,000-year-old earliest sites. Do not assign the query start as an occupation date or equate historical cranial categories with discrete migrating peoples.

### Individual Aleutian archaeological sites

Anangula, Chaluka and Amaknak UNL-469 offer specific locality/phase leads; Islands of Four Mountains material may extend central-chain coverage.

Review before integration: Verify individual dating, location precision and continuity claims. The existing Alaska rectangle starts at 54°N and misses much of the chain; compare both sides of the dateline before measuring this gap.

### Kiribati settlement and contacts

Targets sparse later Holocene island sequences and contacts. Te Bakoa/Nikunau is already mapped but undated because the accessed age lacks calibration; this report is a lead to recovering its original chronology.

Review before integration: Do not apply one arrival date across all island groups. Island emergence is not human settlement, and broad Micronesian ancestry is not a dated local occupation. Current Marshall/Kiribati audit bounds omit islands east of the dateline.

### Nuku Hiva and nearby Marquesan islands

Ho‘oumi, Hakaea and Anaho leads can refine the 1001–1500 CE cell, which already has three dated locality IDs across a much wider region; later persistence requires its own evidence.

Review before integration: Retain dating disagreements and distinguish initial activity, settlement and sustained occupation. Check the reported Ho‘oumi 1129–1212 cal CE window against its original publication.

### Hawaiʻi settlement, trade and cultural development

Targets the 1001–1500 CE row currently dominated by context and the sparse later centuries. Trade, settlement and declining overseas contact need different record types.

Review before integration: Keep competing arrival chronologies and source-specific date bounds; do not infer a single origin island or a precise cessation of voyaging.

### Hawaiian raiding and kingdom consolidation

Adds leads for later precontact and historic warfare, competition and resource pressures rather than new settlement dots by default.

Review before integration: Resource stress is a proposed causal explanation, not automatically demonstrated. Repair missing-author citations and distinguish conquest, alliance and negotiated submission.

### Hawaiian political institutions and state formation

Targets 14th–16th-century institutional developments and later consolidation, supplementing settlement evidence with contextual political history.

Review before integration: Do not project later states backward or turn a conceptual pathway into a single dated event. Check temple, agricultural and warfare chronologies separately.

### Alaska, Yukon and northern Canadian Arctic

Targets Alaska’s sparse early dated-site coverage and Arctic pre-1000 CE gaps, plus revised timing of Thule expansion. Swan Point and Bluefish Caves are specific leads to reconcile.

Review before integration: Separate interior histories from coastal dispersals; show disputed early evidence and differing migration chronologies. Genetics, archaeological traditions and language affiliation need independent support.

### Inca report — exact repeat

Exact PDF duplicate of the archived round9 report. Retained for provenance; no additional gap reduction or independent corroboration.

Review before integration: Revisit only for an unresolved original-source lead; do not duplicate existing round9 records.
