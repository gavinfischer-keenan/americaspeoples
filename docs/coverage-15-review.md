## Earlier work and exclusions

- **Idaho — northern river corridors:** Round 10 Idaho leads were received but had not been verified as a regional history. v0.26 reviewed the separate southern/central bin.
- **Rapa Nui:** Pacific topic material and one late relocation case are narrower than an island-wide place review.
- **Montana — western mountains:** Listed as outstanding in Round 10. v0.26 focused on central/eastern Montana.
- **Southern Lesser Antilles:** The Caribbean synthesis and Garifuna movement topic contain relevant material but no coast-to-coast regional campaign.
- **Louisiana — northern / inland:** Earlier Louisiana/Gulf intake was broad. The recent expansion concentrated on the western coast. The Poverty Point guide covers that one complex within this bin, and its material is excluded from the new evidence focus.
- **Nevada — northwestern basins:** Round 10 received Northern Nevada leads pending verification. v0.26 focused on the central/eastern basins and southern desert.
- **Northern Lesser Antilles:** Earlier general Caribbean intake supplies leads; there is no comparable completed regional guide or production campaign.
- **Venezuela:** Two Round 7 Venezuela dossiers are intake leads. The focused interior-frontiers campaign targeted Amazonia, Guianas and Gran Chaco and did not provide a Venezuela review.
- **Fiji / Tonga / Samoa:** Fiji and Lapita studies and Round 6 Tongan precedence intake cover settlement and selected places; the later local cross-archipelago sequence lacks a dedicated regional review.
- **Northern Mexico / Baja:** Baja intake, mission histories and the Paquimé guide address narrower questions. Existing Paquimé study is outside the new guide’s evidence focus.
- **Societies / Cooks / Marquesas:** Society Islands intake and Pacific topic movies cover selected origins and evidence. The Pacific group guide does not provide this three-archipelago local sequence.
- **Aotearoa New Zealand:** The land/sovereignty and food/voyaging campaigns are targeted subject reviews with named Māori cases; avoid duplicating those cases and supply a regional place history.
- **Southeast / Tidewater:** Prior Southeastern and Virginia/Tidewater intake and broad culture guides are adjacent material. Existing Mississippian and eastern site guides are outside the new guide’s evidence focus.
- **Honduras / El Salvador / Nicaragua:** v0.17 reviewed El Gigante, and Copán has a dedicated site guide. General Central American research includes selected later entries. Emphasize unfinished El Salvador/Nicaragua place sequences.
- **Patagonia / Tierra del Fuego:** Patagonia intake, early continental peopling, voyages and southern-island work contain relevant material. No dedicated production review of the Patagonia/Tierra del Fuego local sequence was found.

The ten v0.26 regions, Canada/Aleutian campaigns, Four Corners, reviewed Brazil/Central American/Melanesian regions, the Falklands/Galápagos island campaign and the Guianas/Gran Chaco interior campaign are screened out. Fiji, Tonga and Samoa are counted as a union across the date line. Northern and southern Lesser Antilles remain separate geographic tasks, with overlapping islands reconciled in the added place identities.

## What filling the map means

New local observations enter the shared timeline with the source’s actual date precision. Guides provide connected histories; a guide-wide chronology is not copied into every site’s occupation interval. Earlier evidence remains legible through research gaps; continuity and departures require their own support. The review adds **70 shared observations at 70 distinct locality IDs**, including new phases at two existing map places, and **15 guides with 70 shared-clock chapters**.


The after column applies the original audit rules to the frozen baseline plus these 70 regional records. It omits additions from the expedition work in the same release. Rows overlap, so a new place can appear in more than one audit row. The observation column counts each guide’s records, including Potomac Creek at 38.35° N. Potomac Creek belongs in the Tidewater guide and shared map, north of that baseline rectangle’s 38° N cutoff. Werowocomoco and Haŋa Roa use existing atlas locality IDs. New dated records do not imply that all sites in a row were active at once.

## Source and date decisions

The review used original publications, historical documents, tribal and custodial sources. Access varies by record, from excavation tables and historic transcripts to author abstracts and institutional summaries. The underlying citations and passages are identified with each map observation. The review used no Consensus calls.

- **Northern Idaho’s rivers and gathering places.** The Ahsahka archaeological source does not establish whether the cited ages for the pit house are calendar or conventional radiocarbon ages. The guide describes the excavated context without entering a calendar date. Weippe Prairie records an encounter at an existing gathering landscape. Tolo Lake records the gathering on June 2, even though movements continued after that date.
- **Rapa Nui from Anakena to Haŋa Roa.** Use separate dates for early settlement, platform construction, quarry soil accumulation and ceremonial activity. The earlier archaeology of ‘Orongo can predate the first evidence that the authors attach to the Birdman cult. The 1888 item charts the agreement and proclamation. The texts and meanings differ across the Rapanui and Spanish versions. A rounded Haŋa Roa pin is an island political reference.
- **Crossing the mountains in western Montana.** MacHaffie’s published conventional radiocarbon dates remain outside the dated additions. The Big Belt evidence retains the authors’ reported combined calibrated envelope and a generalized reference for the named localities. Fort Owen’s date is for establishment of the post. The Hellgate Treaty records the parties’ negotiation and signatures; the Confederated Salish and Kootenai Tribes supply their own chronology.
- **Windward Islands and Barbados.** The Pearls shell dates a poorly described context. The Carriacou spans are rounded phase envelopes; keep each observation separate because assignment across changes of style does not supply a yearly sequence.
- **Caddo communities and inland Louisiana.** The Belcher phase range is based on archaeological comparisons. The French fort date is the year officials dispatched the construction party. The Spanish fort’s construction window is distinct from its later administrative closure and relocation.
- **Marshes and homelands of northwestern Nevada.** The Smithsonian bracket dates a particular decoy. Use a lake-scale reference for the 1860 conflict because the battlefield sites are restricted. Elizabeth Peabody’s 1886 school account dates a documented phase.
- **Leeward islands of the northern Lesser Antilles.** Use Brown’s approximate historic date for the Trants reference, and retain Upper Blake’s as an undated interpretive lead. The Anse à la Gourde span concerns excavated structures and deposits. Naval and fortification dates describe starts of works.
- **Communities of Venezuela’s rivers, llanos and islands.** Parmana records the published broad timing of changing diet at a settlement locality. La Tigra and the main Gaván site use an archaeological phase rather than a dated year of construction. Los Roques is a seasonal-use interpretation supported by research on excavated material.
- **Fiji, Tonga and Samoa after initial settlement.** Sigatoka’s relative ages are calibrated years BP, converted from 1950. Fua‘amotu is a modelled mound date, and Lapaha is evidence for reclamation. The Pulemelei date refers to the initial phase of a rebuilt mound.
- **Baja California and the Sonoran desert.** Use the INAH announcement as an institutional statement of the Bajamar age and find context. Its public wording does not give a confidence level. La Ciénega remains provisional; the construction dates at San Francisco Javier refer to the relocated stone church.
- **Society, Cook and Marquesas island histories.** The Fa‘ahia bracket follows the younger interpretation of archaeological activity, separate from older disputed samples. The broad marae bracket relates to the complex. Use the Cook Islands church’s community account for the Aitutaki milestone alongside the missionary archive.
- **Aotearoa’s coastal villages, pā and Māori political centers.** The Shag River date is an approximate village-era bracket. Akaroa dates a signing occasion on one copy. Ruapekapeka dates the occupation of the pā by allied forces and leaves disputed tactical interpretations open.
- **Indigenous town landscapes of the Virginia Tidewater.** The excavation date for Potomac Creek distinguishes the earlier palisaded site from nearby later contacts. Werowocomoco dates two landscape ditches. Paspahegh uses a broad period for its later component and keeps the protected location coarse.
- **Volcanic valleys and lakeshore communities in El Salvador and Nicaragua.** Use Ichikawa’s model for the specific ash-and-earth construction phase, with related eruption interpretations kept separate. Cerén’s date is a rounded burial-by-ash reference. Keep individual calibrated samples separate from site-duration claims.
- **Patagonia and Tierra del Fuego from the Atlantic to the Beagle Channel.** The four archaeological windows convert calibrated BP ages using 1950. Separate layers and sites retain their own dates. Dawson records the dated lease decree, with differences among institutional foundation and closure chronologies left open.

Ahsahka’s excavated structures are included in the guide. The brief available descriptions leave the calibration of their numerical ages unresolved. MacHaffie provides archaeological context in western Montana while its conventional radiocarbon dates await a separately sourced calendar bracket. The protected locations in the regional histories use generalized landscape or public locality references.
