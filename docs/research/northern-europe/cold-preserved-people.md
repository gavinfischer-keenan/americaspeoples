# Cold-preserved people and Andean summit burials

Three linked guides are available in the atlas guide data. Ötzi appears in Europe. The Northern Hemisphere comparison appears in Europe, Asia and the Pacific, and the Americas. The Andean guide appears in the Americas. Each guide has between 12 and 14 sections and closes with an evidence table. The museum and collection points identify modern custody or research. Ancient recovery areas, regional places and uncertain corridors retain their own labels.

## Research distinctions

- Ötzi’s Copper Age date is 3350–3120 calibrated BCE. The 1991 date belongs to discovery. Mosses and pollen support an ascent corridor through Val Senales, with uncertainty about the exact route and earlier changes in elevation. The taphonomic literature allows exposure and disturbance after death. Earlier studies proposed fatal haemorrhage from the documented arterial injury. Recent CT reconstruction leaves the speed of death and the contribution of other injuries open. [Ötzi genome and calibrated dating](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10504632/), [Moss and liverwort evidence](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0223752), [Find deposition study](https://bora.uib.no/bora-xmlui/bitstream/handle/11250/3054486/09596836221126133.pdf?sequence=1), [2025 forensic reassessment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12354122/).
- Qilakitsormiut is a Greenlandic museum account of Inuit people buried in sheltered graves, where cold and drying helped preserve them. Kwäday Dän Ts’ìnchi is a glacier recovery from a different landscape. The Champagne and Aishihik First Nations’ own reports establish their role in the project and its family research. A wide Tatshenshini-Alsek map anchor protects the distinction between the region and a recovery point. Ice in deliberately prepared Altai burial chambers represents a third preservation context. Pazyryk Barrow 5 and Ukok’s Ak-Alakha-3 are different burials. Many Indigenous Altai residents opposed displaying the Ukok woman. [Greenland National Museum and Archives](https://en.nka.gl/museum/exhibitions/new-people-the-thule-culture/qilakitsormiut/), [Champagne and Aishihik First Nations annual report](https://cafn.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2007-08_annualreport.pdf), [Altai tomb archaeology](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352226722000617), [Altai ethnographic research](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/725428).
- The Andean guide distinguishes the three individuals at Llullaillaco and dates the excavation to 1999. Published age estimates differ. It treats Ampato, El Plomo, Aconcagua, Chuscha, Misti and Pichu Pichu as distinct archaeological contexts. The Aconcagua discovery altitude was around 5,300 metres, below the summit anchor. Inca-period dates use broad historical context unless direct dates support greater precision. Hair biomarkers and isotope results describe sampled individuals and tissues. The guide leaves exact journeys, experience and causes of death open where the evidence does. [Llullaillaco museum collection](https://maam.gob.ar/colecciones_maam/llullaillaco/), [Llullaillaco biomarker study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3746857/), [Aconcagua discovery and genomic research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4642457/).
