# East Africa, the Horn and Malawi: evidence expansion

Research and access check: 19 November 2026. Local implementation only.

`data/world/africa-east.json` adds **48 evidence entries and 43 cited source records**. These are locality/phase observations, not population counts, settlements with continuous lifetimes, or an exhaustive inventory of every excavation. There are no invented population estimates or inferred migration paths.

## Scope and chronology

The requested cutoff is **15,000 BCE**, astronomical year **−14,999**, approximately **16,949 calendar years before 1950**. It is not 15,000 BP. All entries end before that cutoff. The latest included threshold is 17,000 calibrated years BP (astronomical −15,050) at Kuumbi and Chaminade II. Later archaeology is not pulled backward into this scope.

For compatibility with the foundation catalogue, years before present are represented as `1950 − age`. For geological ages expressed in millions of years, this is only an axis convention: it does not turn a rounded geological estimate into an exact calendar date. Published date labels and basis statements remain visible. Reported uncertainty is retained where available. Gademotta, Simbiro and Chaminade II explicitly distinguish minimum ages or boundary estimates from dated occupation events. The Mieso interval is tied to the associated tuff, not a direct date on every artifact.

Distinct phases at Melka Kunture, Konso, Mumba and Panga ya Saidi remain separate observations. An interval may describe dating uncertainty, repeated visits, deposition or archaeological phases; it is never a claim that a community lived continuously throughout it.

## Identity and evidence

Diagnostic fossil assignments follow the cited study or museum record, with qualifications for Bodo and the Malawi fossils. Archaeological industries are technological descriptions, not ethnicities, languages or biological species. Most archaeological entries use the shared `hominins-unassigned` category. Burtele, Buia and the Koobi Fora track surface also retain uncertainty rather than making a taxonomic identification from similarities alone.

The Lomekwi tool occurrence is distinct from the earlier Kenyanthropus fossil already in the catalogue. Bokol Dora is distinct from the older Ledi-Geraru mandible. Existing Nyayanga, Omo I, Herto, Lucy, Laetoli and other foundation observations are not repeated. The new Olduvai entries concern separate archaeological contexts, not additional copies of OH 5 or the habilis type material.

The Panga burial is interpreted from skeletal articulation and sedimentary context. Projectile interpretations at Gademotta, Aduma and Shinfa remain hypotheses about use and delivery, not preserved complete weapons or evidence of war. Kuumbi retains the reinvestigation’s caution about claims older than 20 ka and does not imply a sea crossing when Zanzibar was connected to the mainland.

## Mapping method

Points are rounded navigational locality anchors. They are not excavation GPS positions, sites to visit, boundaries of occupation, or ancient coastlines. Research-area anchors are deliberately broad for Ledi-Geraru, Konso, the Middle Awash, western Olduvai and the Chitimwe landscape. Multiple stratigraphic observations may share an anchor.

Location checks used the cited papers’ maps, published locality descriptions and these explicit cross-checks:

- Lokalalei 2C: the original paper gives 3°56′57.0″N, 35°46′43.7″E; rounded in the catalogue.
- Melka Kunture: the Gombore paper gives the research area at 8°42.284′N, 38°36.098′E; separate sites use a regional anchor.
- Panga ya Saidi: the [SeaLinks field report](https://www.sealinksproject.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/10/Helm-et-al.-2012-Preliminary-results-from-Kenya.pdf) locates the cave at 3°40′42″S, 39°44′10″E.
- Mumba: the [recent primary site analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10437-024-09582-9) gives 35°17′47″E, 3°32′26″S.
- Kisese II: the [primary burial study](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ajpa.24253) provides 4°29′30.47″S, 35°48′43.31″E.
- Fincha Habera: the locality description in the [Ethiopian faunistic research gazetteer](https://mapress.com/zt/article/download/zootaxa.5247.1.1/50027) gives 7.01°N, 39.72°E; the archaeology paper provides the site setting.
- Kuumbi: the [primary speleogenesis study](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_2243562_3/component/file_2247492/content) gives 6°21′40″S, 39°32′33″E.
- Uraha uses a rounded village anchor from [GeoNames](https://www.geonames.org/12182284/uraha.html), consistent with the fossil locality description, rather than claiming the precise U18 recovery location.
- Chaminade I and II use the same broad Karonga-area anchor. The Chitimwe entry represents a dated regional archaeological landscape, not a third excavation at Chaminade.

These coordinate checks are research provenance; the evidence bibliography below contains the sources cited by the playable entries.

## Coverage limits

This pass substantially expands Ethiopia, Kenya, Tanzania, Eritrea and Malawi. It does not establish that unrepresented parts of Somalia, Djibouti, Uganda, Rwanda or Burundi lacked occupation. Securely dated, geographically attributable entries should be added as primary material is checked. Broad industries, undated surface scatters and dates borrowed from neighboring sites were not used to fill empty map space. There is no claim of a complete census of early hominins or Stone Age communities.

The Malawi additions distinguish the Chiwondo fossil record from the far younger Chitimwe sedimentary system, plus the two separately studied Chaminade sites. Environmental-change hypotheses are not converted into population numbers or a first-arrival date.

## Validation

Cross-shard validation must run with the shared `hominins-unassigned` definition in the North/West Africa shard. The merged validator checks unique IDs, source and taxon references, geographic coordinates, chronological ordering and schema completeness. The task-specific audit additionally checks that every East/Horn/Malawi entry ends by astronomical −14,999 and that each source is used.

No new entry uses an existing foundation evidence ID. The intentional same-place observations are different fossil specimens, technologies or stratigraphic phases; they are counted as evidence entries rather than distinct settlement sites.

Latest verification after the focused literature follow-up: merged schema validation passed (264 evidence entries, including 156 Africa entries beginning by the cutoff at that checkpoint); all 43 East/Horn/Malawi sources are cited; all 48 East entries satisfy the cutoff and regional coordinate checks; no numeric population was added. The 24 tests in `world.test.ts`, `world-build.test.ts` and `world-guides.test.ts` passed. Later parallel regional additions may increase the merged count.

## Focused follow-up and remaining coverage

A private research review was used to identify leads, then checked against primary publications. Its raw report and extraction tables are not reproduced here. The seven additions come from three independently checked papers:

- [Braun et al., Namorotukunan](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-64244-x): three horizon records; the NMT2 age discrepancy is retained. The locality coordinate is published in the archaeological findings section.
- [Villmoare et al., Ledi-Geraru](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09390-4): LD 760 and AS 100 remain separate from the existing LD 350 jaw. The new uncertain Australopithecus category avoids forcing a named species. Figure 1 supplies rounded basin anchors, not findspot GPS.
- [Semaw et al., Gona](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7056306/): DAN5 and BSN12 use rounded positions read from Figure 1 of the original paper, inspected through the [CENIEH repository](https://cir.cenieh.es/handle/20.500.12136/1730). The plotted uncertainty does not imply continuous occupation.

The review's automatic extraction contains demonstrably incorrect cutoff flags, including million-year-old evidence described as crossing the 15,000 BCE cutoff. Those flags were discarded. Its final priorities section is truncated. Neither that review nor this catalogue establishes exhaustive coverage.

Important remaining East/Horn leads are the separately dated Kapthurin localities, the Halibee/Chai Baro/Faro Daba sequence, Abdur's coastal archaeology, and further Malawi open-air phases. These require original layer ages and defensible coordinates before export; no neighboring-site age has been substituted. The same applies to the underrepresented countries identified above.

Comparison across the three regional shards also leaves candidate gaps in Gabon, Equatorial Guinea, Cameroon and the Congo basin. Elarmékora, Campo, Matupi, Mukila and pre-Holocene Shum Laka are research leads, not newly verified map entries in this pass. In North Africa and Sudan, Wadi Lazalim, EDAR and Affad deserve further primary-source work; regional industries or full cave lifetimes must not replace dated phases. Older occupation at a site cannot carry younger burial or genetic findings across the cutoff.

One independently checked cross-regional caution concerns Twin Rivers: [Barham's 2012 correction](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22655215/) distinguishes separate passages whose dates had been conflated. [Baldreki et al. (2024)](https://reference-global.com/article/10.5334/oq.132) finds additional depositional complexity and evidence of reworking. A broad regional observation is not a secure continuous occupation sequence. This finding was referred to the southern-shard author; this pass changes only the East shard and this memo.
