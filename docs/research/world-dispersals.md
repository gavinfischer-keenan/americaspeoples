# World dispersal foundation: research decisions

Verified 18 November 2026. This shard supplies 27 evidence anchors, 11 schematic waves and 35 primary/institutional source records. It references the `homo-sapiens` taxon supplied by the human-family shard. IDs use `spread-`.

The evidence distribution is Africa 7, Europe 4 and Asia–Pacific 16. No Americas evidence is copied; the existing detailed collection remains authoritative there. Asia–Pacific guide links reuse `world-voyages-asia-pacific`, and selected entries link the human-family and Britain/Ireland Ice Age guides. Counts describe this deliberately small foundation, not research intensity or continent-wide population density.

## Access and citation scope

Every source was verified through browser retrieval or indexed primary/institutional text. Nature's cookie redirect and intermittent PMC challenges frequently prevented direct page retrieval; those records explicitly say indexed text/abstract, rather than claiming a full-paper reading. Some full-text pages were accessible, but no original sample dataset was reanalyzed. Article publication dates remain in source titles and locators; they never become occupation dates.

The catalogue has compact original paraphrases. Each evidence item identifies what the dating actually measures. It does not use a source count as a confidence vote.

## Dates and geometry

- Numeric years are astronomical integers: calibrated BP uses `1950 - age`; BCE uses `1 - year`. Rounded geological “years ago” are placed on the same 1950 display reference and remain approximate in labels.
- Error ranges are retained where retrieved: Jebel Irhoud 315 ± 34 ka; Tam Pà Ling TPL 7 modeled 77 ± 9 kyr; Ust’-Ishim 46,880–43,210 cal BP; Bacho Kiro 45,820–43,650 cal BP.
- Omo I has a minimum constraint, not a bounded fossil age. Its single display point uses the central age of the overlying ash, 233 ka. The label, basis and limits all say this; actual fossil age is older and unbounded by this record.
- Rounded single points (for example Blombos, Tianyuan and Yana) are dated observations, not year-exact events or continuous occupation.
- Wave windows are broad interpretive display envelopes. They are not fitted confidence intervals or inferred travel durations. The Bantu-associated wave's 1500 CE endpoint is explicitly a display cutoff.
- Coordinates are approximate locality or regional anchors. They are not survey coordinates, claims of territorial ownership, population centroids or genetic-admixture meeting places.
- A proposed northern-Eurasian line was omitted because the dated Ust’-Ishim and Yana observations alone do not establish a route connecting them. Their evidence remains available.
- The Sahul line illustrates one modeled southern possibility. Northern alternatives remain in the text, with Bird et al. (2019) and Gandini et al. (2025) supplying route-model context.
- The European Upper Paleolithic line is explicitly one proposed regional approach informed by the 2024 Southwest Asian staging model and Bacho Kiro genetics. It is not a path reconstructed from a fossil's birthplace.

## Interpretive safeguards

Misliya retains its discoverers' age and a citation to the published dating challenge. Early Asian fossils are not automatically made ancestors of living East Asians. Madjedbebe's chronology is labeled debated; the 2025 mitochondrial argument for a longer Sahul chronology is included without treating molecular dates as direct occupation dates.

Steppe movements west, east and later south are separate waves. The labels do not identify genetic ancestry with Indo-European languages, archaeological cultures or modern ethnicity. Northwestern Anatolian reference samples are separated from the Europe-facing farming movement.

Shum Laka's two burial cohorts are separate observations, explicitly not proof of continuous occupation and not substantial direct ancestors of sampled later Bantu-speaking populations. Bantu and Austronesian labels describe language-associated historical models; neither is a biological race or a uniform genetic population.

## Research gaps for the next pass

This is a breadth-first playable framework. Priority additions include African regional population history beyond these seven anchors; North African and Nile movements; eastern African pastoralism; South Asian archaeological sequences; more direct East Asian and mainland Southeast Asian early-farmer evidence; Japan, Korea and Tibet; northern Sahul route alternatives; and finer European postglacial/farmer/steppe local histories. Late Holocene and historical movements are highly selective.

The synthesis needs ongoing reconciliation with new ancient DNA and revised chronology, particularly for early Asia and Sahul. Subsidiary contested sites were omitted rather than assigned one definitive date. No inference of absence should be drawn from a missing marker. Local and Indigenous historical perspectives need fuller integration during regional deep dives.
