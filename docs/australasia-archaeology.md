# Australia and Aotearoa: dated places

This expansion adds eight observations at six named places. It supplements existing Madjedbebe, Warratyi, Boodie, Mungo, Budj Bim, Jiigurru, Wairau Bar and Triangle Flat records. This remains selected coverage, not an inventory of every known site.

| Place | What the map dates | Primary evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Riwi | Modeled early activity, 46.4–44.6 cal kBP (95.4%) | [Wood and colleagues 2016](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0160123), abstract and discussion |
| Karnatukul | Separate initial and Last Glacial Maximum occupation phases | [McDonald and colleagues 2018](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0202511), occupational sequence and conclusions |
| Nawarla Gabarnmang | Published early occupation estimate, 45,180 ± 910 cal BP | [David and colleagues 2011](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/nawarla-gabarnmang-a-45180-910-cal-bp-site-in-jawoyn-country-sout/), article title and institutional bibliographic record |
| Cloggs Cave | Two built fireplace installations with separate modeled ranges | [David, Mullett and colleagues 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-01912-w), chronology and Fig. 2 |
| Pūrākaunui | Construction/use of seasonal kūmara stores, 1430–1460 CE (95%) | [Barber and Higham 2021](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0247643), discussion and conclusion |
| Otāhau Pā | Palisade construction around 1768 ± 4 CE (95%) | [Hogg and colleagues 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jasrep.2017.01.032), discussion and in-situ timber analysis |

Calibrated BP dates use 1950 CE. The internal astronomical year is 1950 minus the calibrated age. Uncalibrated radiocarbon ages are not converted into calendar dates by subtraction. The possible date of an early hearth or palisade is not a continuous occupation interval. The map retains earlier evidence without labeling a dating gap as abandonment.

Pūrākaunui's constructed stores also appear in the sweet-potato movie. They support storage; they do not by themselves prove local cultivation. Cloggs Cave is represented as a place of ritual activity, not a residential town. Archaeology and community knowledge are considered together, with the specific interpretation and source visible.

Public locality anchors have explicit precision and provenance in each record. Karnatukul and Nawarla Gabarnmang use deliberately generalized regional references because source projects explicitly withhold their precise locations. These pins are labeled as regional references, not excavation coordinates. Other places use checked named localities; the Pūrākaunui site is beside the inlet north of Dunedin, not the similarly named locality in the Catlins.

The forced-relocation movie separately adds Wybalenna, Putalina, Cootamundra, Kinchela, Palm Island and Parihaka. Their institutional dates are not the beginning of Aboriginal or Māori history. Māori schools are not automatically classified as child-removal institutions without case-specific evidence.

Further coverage can grow within the completed movies and shared atlas: Tasmania beyond the historical removals, Australian regional sequences, more dated Māori pā, and local construction and rebuilding phases. Original summaries and links are published; source articles, figures and Consensus reports are not redistributed in the atlas.
