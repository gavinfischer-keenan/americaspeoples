# Cities, encounters and topic dives · v0.11

The ordinary Watch and Explore maps still share one historical world. The new **Topic dives** navigation is a deliberate exception: each topic has its own clock and shows only its evidence. Returning to the shared atlas restores its date, expedition and camera; Explore also retains its search. Reading a topic's sources and returning preserves the topic position. “Show on map” from a topic review stays within that topic.

## Four subjects to watch

- **Horses:** Caribbean landings, horses in Mexico, an archaeological horse at Puerto Real, early Indigenous horse networks in the northern Rockies, and Aónikenk/Tehuelche horse use in Patagonia. Specimen dates, arrival accounts and modeled early phases remain distinct.
- **Maize:** early Balsas microfossils, Peruvian coastal remains, dietary change in Belize, Southwestern cultivation and northeastern cooking residues. These are different observations, not interchangeable first-arrival dates.
- **Trade networks:** Lake Huron obsidian, Poverty Point copper, Chaco macaws, Mexica obsidian, an Ecuadorian sailing raft, silver and merchandise on two oceans, enslaved transport, Hawaiian island exchange and ocean commerce. The commentary separates direct trade, possible intermediaries, transport mechanisms and claims about social or religious consequences.
- **Languages:** a selected family forest, dated documentary encounters, a qualified Austronesian root model, Portuguese language policy, and a “View 1799” control. Spanish, Portuguese, French, English, Dutch and Russian contact appears alongside Indigenous language evidence. The map does not turn political borders into language territories. Earlier documents remain labeled with their dates, rather than masquerading as a complete 1799 census.

The language diagram distinguishes descent from contact and borrowing. Several family roots remain separate; there is no invented universal American root. Classification does not date every split or identify the speech of an archaeological culture. Modern Glottolog coordinates are not used as historical distributions. Hawaiian, Tahitian and Rapanui share ancestry; Hawaiʻi Creole has a contact history, not a claim that Hawaiian turned into English.

The next endorsed subjects remain a research backlog: sweet potatoes, dogs, boats, pottery, obsidian exchange and copper working. They are not advertised as finished movies.

## A fuller inhabited landscape

Forty-one additional city and local-phase records cover Inka/Wari/Cañari settings and multiple Mesoamerican traditions. A city can have several phases rather than one “built, then gone” interval. The three Machu Picchu records now share an archaeological-city anchor derived from a public Cusco plan, replacing the sanctuary proxy.

There are 401 additional locality encounter markers. The largest inventory is the NPS Civil War study-area collection, supplemented by independently sourced events in Canada, Mexico, Central America, the Caribbean and South America. Names and dates lead to claims about the participants and source-supported outcome. Points do not reconstruct tactical positions or imply year-long fighting.

**Peoples of the North** is an ordinary contextual story. Early Paleo-Inuit open-water travel, Dorset seasonal settlement, Thule Inuit expansion, whaling settlements and regional chronological overlap are distinct chapters. Neither demographic change nor the end of a site's sampled dates establishes a massacre. The included evidence does not support a continent-wide Inuit extermination event or an earlier universally boatless population.

Every regular story chapter now has expandable explanation, claim-level evidence and uncertainty. Boat descriptions distinguish evidence for later Pacific canoe traditions from very early crossings whose actual vessels have not survived. The map retains its surrounding historical context while the narrative focuses attention.

## Reading the symbols

A new dated-evidence history type lets an archaeological find or language document remain as **earlier evidence** after its source date. It does not assert continuous occupation, first arrival, foundation or the beginning of a language. Hollow historical markers and full source reviews preserve that distinction. Topic-only material stays out of ordinary playback, while pre-existing crop and exchange evidence remains in the shared atlas.

Coverage remains selected. The [coverage ledger](cities-conflicts-coverage.html) lists the source-defined scope, held inventory rows and remaining gaps. All evidence records and revisions are included in the editable researched workbook and the self-host package; private generated research reports are excluded.
