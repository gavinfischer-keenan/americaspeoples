# Pacific and violence expansion: source decisions

Reviewed 2026-11-15. This batch adds **32 Pacific records** and **25 violence contexts**, with **50 distinct supporting source URLs**. It contains 11 habitation histories, three exchange connections and one migration connection. No new Consensus calls were made. Both completed Deep reports in the sibling `pacific-research` directory were used as leads and left unchanged; their narrative wording was not copied into these collections.

Every row contains a claim, locator, source access note and geographic uncertainty. Publication abstracts, accessible full texts, institutional histories and original archival documents are distinguished in access notes. DOI metadata was checked against publisher records/Crossref where available. The initial ID comparison found no collisions with the existing compiled dataset. The root integration still needs to validate the combined dataset.

## Chronology and map conventions

- Historical BCE is stored as `1 - BCE`; calibrated BP uses the 1950 baseline. Optical ages and rounded institutional age estimates are labeled separately. No dates were recalibrated here.
- Modeled onset ranges are occurrences, not assertions that a settlement was active throughout every candidate onset year. Activity/deposit envelopes are identified as such.
- Most coordinates are honest editorial anchors for a named island, valley or district, with a numeric kilometer uncertainty. They are not recovered excavation coordinates. Kuk uses rounded public UNESCO coordinates. Modern coastline anchors do not reconstruct former shorelines.
- No mainland Asian or European city inventories were added. Eastern Taiwan and northern Philippines are regional island endpoints. Makassar denotes the historical voyaging region, not a newly mapped city history.
- No human population counts were inferred from site area, sample size or trauma frequency.

## Pacific decisions

| Record or group | Decision |
|---|---|
| Madjedbebe | Retain debated status and the optical model's large systematic uncertainty; do not present an exact Sahul landing date. |
| Warratyi and Boodie | Separate lower-unit dating, modeled first occupation and later residential use. Barrow's modern island shape cannot establish an ancient crossing. |
| Lake Mungo | Mortuary evidence is neither a founding event nor a violence diagnosis. |
| Ivane Valley | Keep the early title interval distinct from the paper's wider archaeological sequence. |
| Kuk and Jiigurru | Distinguish earlier occupation/cultivation from later ditches or pottery. Technological similarity alone creates no voyage arrow. |
| Budj Bim | The only `continuity: ongoing` entry is a living cultural landscape supported through the 1934 cutoff. Its ancient start is rounded and does not date continuous use of every structure. |
| Mussau, greater Bismarcks, Marianas | Use the same explicitly probabilistic regional model; do not mix its bounds with older headline chronologies. |
| Toaga and Teouma | Identify differing model coverage and uncertainty levels; a midden ending does not date island abandonment. |
| Bourewa | Prefer the revised publisher abstract consistently. Abstract and PDF bounds differ by one year; the record discloses that discrepancy. |
| Babeldaob and Palau Rock Islands | Earthwork construction is not uniform village residence. Rock Island departures are local relocations; descendants' continuing connection is retained. |
| Emily Bay and Mount Bates | Leave Emily Bay's terminal boundary as the end of known site evidence. Mount Bates uses the calibrated table rather than mechanically converting the headline age. |
| Raoul | Two horizons and a volcanic burial are not evidence of uninterrupted occupation or permanent final abandonment. |
| Enderby | The final review broadened the ending to the published phase model, replacing an over-tight late-fourteenth-century interpretation. Upper-layer mixing and wider uncertainty remain explicit. |
| Henderson and Pitcairn | Local habitation can outlast material exchange. Henderson has a broad inferred departure bracket; no invented Pitcairn departure date is supplied. No Rapa Nui abandonment is asserted. |
| Hooumi and Vatcha | Later use is retained separately from earlier disruption or lower activity. Phase change is not cultural disappearance. |
| Fergusson to Caution Bay | Correct source region is Fergusson Island. Repeated imports and younger possible recycled/redeposited pieces are distinguished. Procurement cessation is inferred, not proof that all contact ended. |
| Raoul to Norfolk | Forward direction means sourced material transfer. It supplies neither a direct sailing course, recurrence rate nor return voyage. |
| Makassan voyages | Documentary outward and return travel supports both directions. The last licensed season, ban and subsequent checking voyage have separate notes; ending an industry does not end cultural relationships. |

Key independently inspected sources include [Anderson and colleagues on Enderby](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/arco.5337), [Jorgensen and colleagues on Mount Bates](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/arco.5307), [Molle and Hermann on Pitcairn](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/n4467/html/ch02.xhtml), [Mialanes and colleagues on Caution Bay imports](https://doi.org/10.1080/03122417.2016.1252079), and the [National Museum voyage account](https://www.nma.gov.au/audio/behind-the-scenes-australian-journeys-series/transcripts/from-makassar-to-marege-to-the-museum). Full source metadata and narrower locators are attached to the corresponding records.

## Violence decisions and balance

The 25 additions include six Australian contexts; six other Pacific contexts (New Guinea, Taumako, Tonga, New Zealand and Samoa); four Caribbean contexts; and nine mainland American contexts. These supplement existing Sand Creek, Bear River, Wounded Knee, Crow Creek, Southwest village and Andean trauma records without duplicating their events.

| Evidence class | Added contexts | Interpretive boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Australian historical killings | Myall Creek, Waterloo Creek, Pinjarra, Coniston | Named historical actors where supported; broad campaigns are not rendered as one mass grave. Conflicting totals are not forced into an exact count. |
| Individual skeletal violence | Kaakutja, Narrabeen | Perimortem evidence can support violent death without identifying an attacker. Weapon and motive hypotheses remain hypotheses. |
| Aggregate or equivocal trauma | Nebira, Taumako, Tonga | Healed injury is distinct from lethal injury. Samples are not single massacres. Tonga's possible ritual/sporting injuries remain date-unplaced because this review did not extract one defensible sample-wide interval. |
| Pacific historical conflict | Wairau, Rangiaowhia, Apia Black Saturday | Distinguish firing, surrender killings, burning and disputed casualty accounts. |
| Caribbean coercion and protest | Morant Bay, Fireburn, Samuel Sharpe's execution, Les Cayes | Execution, uprising and later repression are different events. Colonial accounts are attributed as interested primary sources, including self-defense claims. |
| American archaeological violence | Sacred Ridge, Pot Creek, Huanchaquito, Witzna, Cancuen | Context ranges are not continuous wars. Ritual interpretation and warfare attribution require their stated evidence; neither trauma nor fire alone identifies ethnicity or motive. |
| American historical attacks | Mystic, Gnadenhutten, Washita, Camp Grant | Historical circumstances are preserved without a universal battle/massacre label or collective responsibility assigned to modern communities. Old Camp Grant is geographically distinct from later Fort Grant. |

For [Nebira and Taumako](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajpa.21250), the sample distinction is central. The [Tonga study](https://doi.org/10.1086/676477) expressly tests and challenges a warfare expectation. The [Les Cayes telegram](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1929v03/d199) is evidence that firing occurred, not independent verification of its justification or final toll. [Pot Creek's primary article](https://doi.org/10.7275/R5SF2T36) concerns a small group of individuals rather than a community-wide massacre.

## Existing records to review in integration

These are recommendations, not unreviewed edits to existing records:

1. `history-mangareva-pitcairn-henderson`: audit whether `direction: both` is established by its own sources. Material origin and destination alone do not establish a return sailing. A 1450 material endpoint must not automatically end Henderson habitation.
2. `history-lapita-vanuatu-fiji`: keep regional movement estimates separate from the revised site-specific Bourewa model; do not silently replace a regional arrival with one site's onset.
3. `history-samoa-continuity` and `history-american-samoa-regional-occupation`: compare any old broad founding dates with the Toaga revision, while preserving independent Mulifanua and other island evidence. One site's revision cannot redetermine an entire island group.
4. Existing `r10v-hooumi-layeriv` disturbance evidence should remain separate from the later raised-house entry.
5. Existing `r9v-sw-pot-*` occupation, construction and decommissioning evidence should remain separate from the new Pot Creek skeletal sample.

## Gaps retained

Marshall Islands, Kiribati, Tuvalu and several smaller Micronesian groups still need site-level source verification. No convenient date was invented to fill those gaps. Direct voyage direction and precise local endpoints are often unrecoverable. Archaeological violence coverage remains dependent on preservation and research intensity; historic documentary coverage is not a proxy for actual relative violence rates. Archaeological Caribbean trauma studies require further verification beyond this batch's four historical cases.

The raw research responses are preserved outside these interpretive decisions. Intermediate local drafting helpers were placed beside those reports, not in tracked source code.

## Validation

`node --import tsx --test tests/pacific-expansion.test.ts` passes six focused tests. They validate complete records and source links, shared metadata consistency, date conversion, local endings versus missing evidence, the 1934 continuity horizon, direction and cessation distinctions, and violence/unplaced-date semantics. The final metadata and chronology corrections were included before the final test run.
