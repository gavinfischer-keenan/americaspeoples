# Expedition chronology and source decisions

The atlas adds 64 dated itineraries or expedition phases to the earlier catalog. The expansion contributes 204 source-linked waypoint records and 130 catalog sources. The separate activity index includes 59 year- or month-level entries. The coverage inventory records 35 held, partial and mapped subjects. Coverage favors major oceanic, river, overland and polar expeditions whose dates can be grounded in surviving records. These selections cover documented episodes from the late fifteenth century through the atlas’s 1934 endpoint.

## How to read the evidence

The itinerary records observations at named places, departures and documented stays. Day dates use the convention of the cited edition or institution. Early modern sources sometimes disagree on which calendar date an event falls on. The note identifies those alternatives. A single dated observation carries no assumption about the duration of a stay. A documented interval, such as time spent aboard a beset ship or at a winter base, has explicit bounds.

Positions identify approximate places, reported historical fixes, or the broader region of an offshore observation. When the route has enough evidence to connect adjacent anchors, the displayed line is schematic. Where intermediate positions, seasonal occupations or route changes are unresolved, the documented anchors are separated by a gap. The contemporaneous list keeps the expedition visible while the route position is unspecified.

The activity index shows the calendar year, month or range in which the documented activity falls. It gives a region in text without asserting a location on any particular day. Some year ranges encompass several field seasons, and the notes explain those breaks. The index separates Cabot’s two voyages, the French Antarctic expeditions, the Cook and Peary journeys, and the three earlier Endurance rescue attempts. The inventory retains unresolved or disputed dates and places with direct source links. Cabot’s June 1497 landfall is unresolved along the eastern North American coast. Cook and Peary’s disputed claims have no North Pole waypoint. [Parks Canada on Cabot](https://www.pc.gc.ca/apps/dfhd/page_nhs_eng.aspx?id=1036), [Library of Congress polar chronology](https://guides.loc.gov/polar-exploration/timeline)

## Shackleton and the Antarctic voyages

Endurance’s ship track and its crew’s subsequent drift diverge on 27 October 1915. The abandoned ship’s track ends at the wreck on 21 November. The people on the pack, the lifeboat and Elephant Island phases, the James Caird, the overland crossing of South Georgia, and Yelcho’s rescue retain separate itinerary identities. This permits the waiting party and the boat expedition to appear at the same time. The Chilean officer Luis Pardo commanded Yelcho, which reached the stranded party on 30 August 1916. The Ross Sea party’s deployment and the surviving members’ later return to Cape Evans are distinct from the Weddell Sea party. [Royal Geographical Society chronology](https://www.rgs.org/media/wu2hsebf/wse-shackleton-expedition-timeline.pdf), [Canterbury Museum navigation reconstruction](https://cms.canterburymuseum.com/assets/Navigation-of-the-Shackleton-Expedition-on-the-Weddell-Sea-pack-ice.pdf), [Armada de Chile rescue account](https://www.armada.cl/a-110-anos-de-un-rescate-imposible-capitulo-5-el-rescate-en-isla), [Antarctic Heritage Trust Ross Sea history](https://nzaht.org/conserve/explorer-bases/scotts-hut-cape-evans/history-of-other-expeditions/)

Long passages such as England to Aotearoa in Nimrod and Terra Nova appear as gaps when their intermediate oceanic waypoints are outside the selection. They cannot place a ship on a straight great-circle crossing of Eurasia. The cruise supporting Framheim, Amundsen’s sledge journey, and the expedition vessel’s later return are distinct. Discovery’s outward sledging trip, Terra Nova’s polar party, and the Belgica sea-ice winter use separate phases. This selection uses air mode for the 1925 N24/N25 expedition, the 1926 Norge crossing, and Byrd’s 1929 South Pole flight. Byrd’s 1934 winter at Advance Base appears in the dated rows. The first completed Ellsworth transcontinental flight occurred in 1935 beyond the atlas’s endpoint. [Antarctic Heritage Trust on Nimrod](https://nzaht.org/conserve/explorer-bases/shackletons-hut/history-of-shackletons-expedition/), [Fram Museum](https://frammuseum.no/en/polar-history/expeditions/the-third-fram-expedition-1910-1914/), [Ohio State University Libraries Byrd archives](https://library.osu.edu/richard-e.-byrd-chronology), [Ellsworth archive](https://natlib.govt.nz/records/40915674)

## Americas and Arctic coverage

Cabral’s observed Brazilian coast and the Porto Seguro dispatch, Verrazzano’s Atlantic crossing and regional harbor evidence, Cartier’s three voyages and Hudson’s distinct river and bay expeditions, Jolliet and Marquette’s Mississippi expedition, and Mackenzie’s two northern routes extend the dated coverage. Older voyages retain the colonization, diplomacy, Indigenous guidance and violence described by the evidence, without using a visit as an occupation claim. Inuit testimony and Inuit contributions to travel and survival have direct relevance to the Franklin, Gjøa, Polaris and Thule expedition accounts. The activity index includes the movement of Qitdlarssuaq and Inuit families across the eastern Arctic at the precision of the cited community and historical records. [Biblioteca Nacional do Brasil](https://objdigital.bn.br/objdigital2/acervo_digital/div_obrasraras/or26616/or26616.pdf), [Canadian Museum of History on Cartier](https://www.historymuseum.ca/virtual-museum-of-new-france/the-explorers/jacques-cartier-1534-1542/), [Parks Canada on Inuit testimony](https://parks.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/nu/epaveswrecks/culture/inuit/qaujimajatuqangit), [Qikiqtani Truth Commission community histories](https://www.qtcommission.ca/en/file-download/download/public/78)

The NSIDC trackline supplies selected fixes for Nansen’s Fram drift. The published participant dispatch supplies the freezing-in position and the museum chronology dates the ship’s emergence. The sources disagree on the date and position of the northernmost point. Gjøa’s King Point anchor records the September 1905 halt and leaves the following winter and disputed resumption dates as a gap with a separate month-level context band. Cartier’s Stadacona winter and Hudson’s James Bay winter also have separate bands. Their anchors do not interpolate voyages across winter intervals. [NSIDC trackline](https://doi.org/10.1594/PANGAEA.926633), [Fram Museum on Gjøa](https://frammuseum.no/en/polar-history/expeditions/the-gjoa-expedition-1903-1906/), [Library of Congress on Gjøa](https://www.loc.gov/resource/gdc.08035750v1/?st=gallery), [Royal Museums Greenwich on Hudson](https://www.rmg.co.uk/stories/maritime-history/henry-hudson-north-west-passage-expedition-1610-11)

## Data and limitations

- [Dated itinerary catalog](https://americaspeoples.11mosswood.us/voyages.json) contains voyage dates, travel modes, positions, citations, route gaps and links to evidence records.
- [Voyage evidence collection](https://americaspeoples.11mosswood.us/voyage-expansion-records.json) contains full cited evidence records. Dates known to the day appear in the event-date field. Conflicting day dates remain in the commentary and reference chronology without a declared exact event date.
- [Expedition activity index](https://americaspeoples.11mosswood.us/expedition-activity.json) contains year- and month-precision entries with a self-contained source catalog and optional links to related dated routes.
- [Coverage inventory](https://americaspeoples.11mosswood.us/voyage-coverage-inventory.json) preserves missing days, disputed claims and unverified route phases. Its entries are research decisions, with named and linked evidence where available.

Many coastal surveys, trade voyages, rescues and inland expeditions remain incompletely mapped. Further work should trace their separate routes from the underlying logs and community records. The inventory includes leads for the American coastal and Iberian voyages and the remaining Antarctic phases. A broad band documents the period at the precision stated and point records document only their selected observations.
