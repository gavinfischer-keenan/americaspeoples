# Where the map has gaps

The audit found both missing research and evidence that still needs better dates or locations. It does **not** find that blank regions were empty of people. The current campaign adds **70 shared observations** supporting the final twelve dedicated studies. The previous release added 61 shared observations for the first thirteen. See [the campaign and source decisions](coming-soon-campaign.html). The prior v0.17 campaign added 37 early-continuity observations. The prior v0.16 campaign added 35 interior observations. The prior v0.15 campaign added 43 regional observations; those remain part of the baseline. The prior v0.14 release added 16 other gap observations and 8 Australia/Aotearoa archaeological observations; those are retained, not counted as new here.

## What the numbers measure

Shared dated locality/point observations in overlapping rectangular regions; connections with named origin points count at that origin, while route-only and focused topic records are excluded. Intersecting source windows count, not historical-memory display. Counts are evidence records, not towns, populations, continuous occupancy or research effort. Empty cells are not classified as human absence. Undated records remain separate.

The immutable baseline is the completed v0.18 atlas: 2,143 total records and 2,078 shared records, before the second dedicated-study release. The completed v0.14 and earlier working baselines are retained separately. Cells show **before → now** when changed; unchanged counts appear once. ## When a gap could be real

Evidence of a local departure can be shown when a source supports it: Popo’s shift and the interpreted later departure from Illutalialuk are labeled with uncertain windows. That is different from the absence of a record in a selected year. Aotearoa’s very early empty bins precede the currently supported settlement chronology; they should not trigger invented towns merely to fill a matrix. See the existing [early Māori settlement evidence](../index.html#review/context-triangle-flat) and the [Australia/Aotearoa source guide](australasia-archaeology.html).

No broad region is classified as unoccupied solely from this audit. Counts are especially biased toward documented U.S. Civil War encounters, colonial foundations and sites with readily accessible dates. Routes and long-lived historical markers also make the visible map different from this count of dated observations.

## Remaining priorities

- Northwest Coast villages in the earlier Holocene; date and locate existing Ts’ishaa, Huu7ii and Xwe’etay evidence before requesting duplicate research.
- Arctic local phases and mobility beyond a single Dorset-to-Thule narrative; community-reviewed accounts and evidence of any violence require case-specific support.
- Atlantic Brazilian built landscapes, sambaquis and Jê communities across the first and second millennia CE.
- Central America, the Andes and Amazonia in 1700–1934: Indigenous towns, labor, local conflicts, displacement and continuing institutions, beyond capital cities and independence battles.
- New Guinea, Solomons and other Melanesian islands after Lapita: local villages, gardens, ritual centers and changing exchange windows.
- Australia’s later Holocene local sequences and Aotearoa’s many pā, seasonal places and community histories.
- Geographic refinement where public site coordinates are available. A town name with a broad regional pin is useful orientation, but does not support building-scale precision.

All 25 formerly planned dedicated studies are now available, alongside the earlier content. The atlas has 26 independent topic movies. The six older place-guide expansions remain separate future work. These priorities are **continuing research gaps**, not a queue of missing movies. Full Consensus reports remain private; original factual records cite the underlying sources.
