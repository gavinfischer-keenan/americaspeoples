There are 85 distinct source URLs across the records. These include community institutions, archaeological research, heritage nominations, national archives, municipal histories, and government heritage inventories. Access notes distinguish relevant page text or indexed passages from any claim to have independently reviewed a complete excavation archive.

The full Consensus settlement, voyage and Latin America reports were treated as research leads and preserved unchanged elsewhere. This file records implementation decisions rather than rewriting those reports. No additional Consensus calls were made by this batch.

## Time and event decisions

- First European mention is not a founding event. Taos starts with the broad 1000–1450 construction estimate for the present adobe community, has a conservative certain bound at 1450, and includes the 1540 attestation in its claim. Acoma, Hopi villages, Picuris and Ohkay Owingeh likewise precede colonial accounts. Community institutions support continued habitation. [Taos community FAQ](https://taospueblo-nsn.gov/faq/), [Acoma interpretation](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/acoma-pueblo-ancient-city-in-the-sky.htm), [Hopi community account](https://www.hopieducationfund.org/about-hopi).
- Walpi is explicitly a settlement lineage across local moves. Its early date does not date the present mesa buildings. The anchor is regional. Oraibi uses a conservative mid-twelfth-century phase rather than an unsupported exact first household.
- Cholula begins approximately 500 BCE, stored as astronomical year **-499**. The interval concerns urban habitation rather than unchanged institutions. [INAH archaeological history](https://revistas.inah.gob.mx/index.php/antropologia/article/view/2941).
- Mexico-Tenochtitlan uses conventional 1325 with status **under discussion**, since retrospective accounts and archaeological inception are not identical. The continuing urban locality is distinct from continuing Mexica sovereignty. It links the existing colonial-transition and archaeological/population records. [Templo Mayor account](https://www.templomayor.inah.gob.mx/historia/mito-de-la-peregrinacion), [Mexico City heritage account](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/412/).
- Cusco begins at a securely supported fifteenth-century urban-development phase; this is explicitly not its earliest occupation. Earlier existing Cusco records remain linked.
- En Bas Saline, Maima, Caguana, Salt River Bay, Iximché and Upper Xingu retain finite evidence intervals without automatic abandonment metadata. Maima's earliest value is the early bound of a calibrated radiocarbon result, not an exact founding year. Upper Xingu is a regional settlement-network anchor; it cannot be read as a single surveyed city footprint.
- Pecos has a separately supported 1838 final local departure. Hawikuh's source says abandonment **after** 1680, so the selected occupation interval ends at 1680 without inventing a precise abandonment event.
- La Isabela's late-1493 construction and early-1494 formal establishment are represented as a bounded event. La Vega Vieja uses 1495–1496 for its on-site town/fort, separating the 1494 fort about a kilometre away. [La Vega primary layout study](https://ufdcimages.uflib.ufl.edu/AA/00/06/19/61/01105/29_Kulstad.pdf).
- Puerto Real's 1578 order is distinct from actual forced evacuation/destruction in 1579. The ending note does not claim every subsequent squatter was absent. [Excavation project history](https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/histarch/research/haiti/puerto-real/history/).
- Sevilla la Nueva's 1534 capital transfer is not treated as total abandonment: the heritage submission reports later inhabitants. Port Royal's 1692 earthquake likewise does not erase subsequent town history.
- Sancti Spíritus (1522) and Camagüey (1528) date the present-site relocation, with earlier town lineages stated in their claims.
- São Luís begins with the French settlement of 1612, not the subsequent Portuguese conquest. Paramaribo's 1613 Dutch trading post is explicitly distinct from the later municipal city and from prior Indigenous Parmubo.
- Georgetown dates French urban works in 1782, with prior Indigenous trade, the 1748 guard station and 1781 British works acknowledged. Stabroek (1784) and Georgetown (1812) are later names. A government anniversary article giving 1791 for French establishment was rejected in favor of the detailed National Trust chronology. [National Trust account](https://ntg.gov.gy/historic-georgetown/).
- Montevideo uses the 1724–1726 fortification/settlement/layout process, not the 1730 cabildo. Porto Alegre's approximate 1732 settlers precede 1752 immigration and 1772 parish organization. These are inhabited nuclei, not backdated municipal status.
- Belo Horizonte dates the 1897 planned-capital opening while acknowledging the older locality. Londrina dates the 1929 initial company settlement, not the 1934 municipality. Saint-Laurent-du-Maroni distinguishes prisoner installation in 1857 from inauguration/naming in 1858 and records older Arawak and Kali'na habitation.
- Colonial founding events remain events; they are not converted to ongoing habitation merely because a modern city exists. Their later visibility is governed by the atlas history layer.

## California mission completeness and relocations

The [California State Parks institution chronology](https://www.parks.ca.gov/?page_id=22679) is the common list check. Individual institutional and heritage sources supply local moves and distinctions. Foundation dates describe institutions, not the surviving church masonry. Public mission points are not evidence that every city named San José, San Rafael or San Francisco originated as a mission.

Key local corrections:

- San Diego: presidio foundation 1769; Mission Valley move 1774.
- San Carlos: Monterey foundation 1770; Carmel move 1771.
- San Antonio: original valley establishment 1771; upstream operating complex 1773.
- San Gabriel: Whittier Narrows 1771; current locality 1775 after flooding.
- Santa Clara: original 1777; interim church in use 1779; third church in use **1784**, not its 1781 cornerstone; temporary fourth church in use **1819** after the 1818 earthquake; fifth church in use **1825**, not its 1822 construction start. [Detailed university chronology](https://www.scu.edu/scujesuits/aboutjes/missionhistory/), [mapped site sequence](https://dh.scu.edu/exhibits/neatline/show/sites-of-mission-santa-clara). A short university overview collapsing the site sequence was not used in place of the detailed sequence. The first-site marker uses the university-supported January 12 date rather than a contradictory January 17 in one state landmark entry.
- La Purísima: 1787 initial locality; earthquake December 1812; replacement site actually established April 23, **1813**. [State Parks collection guide](https://www.parks.ca.gov/pages/1080/files/fa_513_001.pdf).
- Santa Cruz: initial institution 1791; hilltop church cornerstone 1793; church dedicated/in use **1794**. This site-phase record does not pretend all functions moved at one instant.
- San José is placed at the Oroysom locality in **Fremont**, not downtown San José. [State landmark record](https://ohp.parks.ca.gov/ListedResources/Detail/334).
- San Francisco Solano (1823) is a **Mexican-period** foundation. Earlier institutions began under Spain. Santa Clara's fifth church phase in 1825 likewise uses Mexico.
- Institutional secularization and closure are not treated as abandonment of all nearby Indigenous or later settlements.
- Each Alta foundation includes an explicitly system-level Indigenous subordination context supported by the [California Museum interpretation](https://californiamuseum.org/exhibitions/signature/california-missions/). San Gabriel and San José have additional local Indigenous place context. No fabricated local coercion episode or tribal population is assigned by analogy.

## Geography and evidence limits

Coordinates are editorial orientation anchors for the named public locality. Most towns and mission complexes use approximately 1–2 km resolution, with explicitly broader anchors for Walpi's lineage, early Córdoba, En Bas Saline, Maima, early Santa Clara and San Antonio. Upper Xingu is deliberately a roughly 50 km regional anchor. These points are not cadastral boundaries, excavated-house coordinates, surveyed original chapel footprints or population surfaces. The record's precision and provenance fields disclose this.

Bayamo and Paramaribo are openly labeled town-lineage anchors where the earliest footprint is unresolved; they must not be presented as surveyed original structures. Closely spaced Santa Clara site points represent different named public sites, but the stated precision exceeds their separation; the dates and captions carry the distinction.

The sources substantiate settlement history, and do not imply permission to copy institutional maps or photographs. No source imagery or downloaded GIS geometry is incorporated in these collections.

## Remaining coverage gaps

| Area | Delivered | Remaining |
|---|---|---|
| Caribbean | Twenty colonial town/site records, plus four Indigenous settlement sequences | El Cabo, El Chorro de Maíta and additional Lesser Antillean communities need location/phase-specific review; Nassau and many smaller colonial towns remain absent |
| Pueblo Southwest | Taos, Acoma, Walpi, Oraibi, Ohkay Owingeh, Picuris, Pecos, Hawikuh | Other Rio Grande pueblos; current Zuni town and its relation to earlier villages; more precise relocation histories |
| Mesoamerica | Cholula, Tenochtitlan/Mexico City, Tzintzuntzan, Iximché and Central American colonial relocations | More Maya and Nahua community continuities; San Salvador/Ciudad Vieja, Granada, Cartago and Tegucigalpa |
| Andes and southern cone | Cusco, Sucre, Arequipa, Cuenca, Santiago, Buenos Aires, Córdoba, Asunción, Montevideo, Colonia | Quito, Cajamarca, La Paz, Potosí, Guayaquil and Concepción/Penco require separate local-phase work |
| Brazil and Guianas | Eight Brazil records, Paramaribo, Georgetown and Saint-Laurent-du-Maroni | Manaus, Recife and São Paulo earlier phases, Cayenne, additional Indigenous and Maroon towns, broader nineteenth-century urban/industrial growth |
| Amazonia | One deliberately coarse Upper Xingu settlement-network phase | Individual town positions, local hiatuses and ongoing descendant-community connections need site-specific sources |
| Missions | All 21 Alta California foundations; major relocation/site phases; five Baja examples | Full Baja chain, asistencias, recruitment trajectories and individual communities' accounts remain incomplete |

This collection adds meaningful chronology before and during 1700–1934 without an artificial 1700 onset or claiming every town is covered. Political geometry, routes, transport, population counts and application rendering are separate integration responsibilities.
