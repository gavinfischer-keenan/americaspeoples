# Falklands and Galápagos: people, finds and settlement

The new master-map material separates disputed early visits, dated historical encounters and actual construction. It adds 17 records and revises the two earlier research assessments. Existing Beagle stops remain linked to their own journey records rather than being copied.

## Falklands / Islas Malvinas

At New Island, Hamley and colleagues reported sea-lion bone deposits dated to 1275–1420 CE. Their proposed human explanation also draws on charcoal and a surface stone point. The atlas now displays that published deposition window, with a disputed interpretation attached to the regional feature. It does not treat the window as continuous residence. The bones had no identified cut marks, the stone point is not securely dated, and natural formation and fire are discussed in the later critique. The warrah is not used as proof of people or of a particular voyage.

- [Hamley et al. 2021: full primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8550247/)
- [Zangrando and Borrero 2022: critical review](https://doi.org/10.1080/15564894.2022.2077484)

Historical additions identify Port Saint-Louis in 1764, Port Egmont's built settlement in 1766, the Spanish takeover of Puerto Soledad, armed intervention and withdrawals, Vernet's settlement, the Lexington attack and Stanley's foundation. A claim made during a survey is not silently substituted for a construction date. Foundations retain explicit closure information where the cited history supports it.

## Galápagos

The old pre-Columbian interpretation rested substantially on plain pottery. Later fieldwork retained glass, metal and other ceramics alongside it; dating and their shared distribution favour historical camps. The assessment remains available without creating a prehistoric settlement or an inferred Inca or Polynesian voyage.

- [Anderson et al. 2016: repository record](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/159894/)

The dated additions cover Berlanga's recorded arrival, Watkins's food cultivation and exchanges with ships, the mail station attested by Porter, construction at Asilo de la Paz, Wreck Bay, San Cristóbal's highland farms and sugar industry, and the revolt against Cobos. The exact landing beach in 1535 and Watkins's dwelling are not established here, so these use regional references. Public modern locality coordinates locate the other named places without pretending to locate every historical building.
