# Ten sparse areas: communities, connections and changing places

Empty parts of the atlas held many histories that had not yet been entered. This campaign adds named places and dated activity across six western U.S. areas, coastal Louisiana and the Gilbert, Phoenix and Line islands. It brings in archaeological buildings and camps, enduring Indigenous communities, colonial labor, commerce, warfare and documented local departures.

This update adds **68 shared map records**. Each can be found on the master map at its supported date, alongside events elsewhere. Archaeological samples, villages, trading posts and journeys keep their distinct meanings.

## People between the dates

A place can have people before and after a poorly documented interval. The map keeps its earlier evidence visible rather than turning that gap into a disappearance. Where community histories support continuing habitation, the inhabited-place record spans the interval. Where they do not, the marker stays as earlier evidence and the later activity remains separately dated. A dated sample is not the date everybody arrived or left. Explicitly documented closure or relocation receives a separate cue: for example, the U.S. Army leaving Fort Phil Kearny in 1868, or Kiritimati’s 1905–1912 occupation gap. Neither a government incorporation date nor the atlas endpoint means the first or last human presence.

## Idaho — southern / central

Owl Cave records bison processing near the end of the Ice Age. Much later, Bison Rockshelter preserves a Shoshonean tool tradition. Fort Hall connects two different places: a fur-trade post on the Snake River and the later Shoshone–Bannock reservation community. The post’s closure did not end the people’s history.

### Owl Cave (near Idaho Falls)

Fluted Folsom points and bison bones were recovered from the lava-tube cave. Direct dating and reanalysis indicate the mammoth bones are older than the Folsom finds. Newly dated bison remains make a human-bison association plausible.

The youngest bison bones in the deeper finds overlap the age of Folsom technology. The report presents an estimate for bison processing and the later analysis considers the association plausible.

**When:** roughly 12,500 cal BP

**Where:** Owl Cave (near Idaho Falls). Idaho Falls area, approximately city-to-surrounding-plain scale; public city reference point east of the restricted cave site

### Old Fort Hall trading post

Shoshone and Bannock people traded at the Snake River post. The Hudson’s Bay Company bought it in 1837. The fur business declined as the site became an emigrant supply stop, and the company closed the post in 1856.

Local Indigenous traders used the post while wagon-based commerce increased along the Snake River.

**When:** 1834–1856 CE

**Where:** Old Fort Hall trading post. Rounded historic site on the Snake River, on tribal land

## Kiribati — Gilbert Islands

At Buota, plant remains and garden soils connect people with swamp-taro cultivation. Later accounts locate villages, chiefly courts, churches and trading stores at Koinawa, Binoinano and Butaritari. The Alexa’s service connects Butaritari’s copra economy with supplies and passengers from Australia.

### Buota, North Tarawa — early taro-pit landscape

Charcoal beneath and beside swamp-taro pit spoil marks early activity on North Tarawa. The excavated Buota landscape includes pits and mounds as well as plant remains.

Buried soil horizons at the site include charcoal consistent with clearance before gardening. Charcoal can be older than the burning episode.

**When:** Third–seventh centuries CE, as reported for North Tarawa charcoal in taro-pit mounds

**Where:** Buota swamp-taro pits, North Tarawa. Approximate public islet location.

**Where:** Koinawa, Abaiang. Approximate public village location.

## Kiribati — Line Islands

Kiritimati’s archaeological complexes and Tabuaeran’s imported Marquesan adze show earlier island connections. Nineteenth-century plantations, harbor settlements and the cable station bring workers and families into view. Kiritimati’s documented 1905–1912 gap is retained, followed by the renewed estate from 1913; it is not treated like an unexplored archaeological interval.

### Kiritimati — Manulu Lagoon ceremonial and domestic complex

Along Manulu Lagoon’s northwest shore, Polynesian people used stone pavements and ovens. Excavators interpreted several adjacent features as one domestic and ceremonial complex.

The published synthesis links KKI-03, KKI-14, KKI-15 and KKI-18. Charcoal determinations differ by feature. The anomalously old shell sample from KKI-18 could reflect reused shell.

**When:** Thirteenth–fifteenth centuries in the excavation synthesis, with activity most likely around 1400

**Where:** Manulu Lagoon archaeological complex, Kiritimati. Approximate public lagoon location.

## Louisiana — western coastal plain

On coastal ridges, Veazey’s pottery and Morgan’s mound-top building reveal organized places in a marsh landscape. Chitimacha continuity at Charenton sits beside the colonial growth of Nueva Iberia and St. Martinville. ### Morgan Mounds — a house and pottery work above the marsh

People built a circular wooden building on Morgan’s largest mound, overlooking the coastal marsh. Hearths, replaced posts and pottery-making materials show a building used and repaired over time.

**When:** Tenth century CE, the published interpretation of radiocarbon dates for the summit structure.

**Where:** Morgan Mounds, near Pecan Island, Louisiana. Public site-area reference, approximately 1 km; no individual building footprint.

## Wyoming — eastern plains

Powars II was a source of red pigment. Hell Gap and Vore preserve different forms of recurring activity: camp life and collective bison hunting. Fort Phil Kearny and the Fort Laramie treaty place the struggle over the Bozeman Trail on the same shared clock.

The excavations found ancient quarrying debris, pigment-bearing rock and tools left during the extraction of red ochre. Early deposits also record tool repair. Stone sourced far from the site connects the quarry users with wider Plains networks.

## Places still needing more work

- **Celebration Park (Warick site):** The excavated site has Middle and Late Archaic diagnostics. The cited park report does not establish secure absolute bounds for the layers, and visitor-page petroglyph estimates cannot substitute. [Source 1](https://www.lib.uidaho.edu/Idaho_Archaeologist/IdahoArchaeologist-Vol.39-No.2.pdf) · [Source 2](https://www.blm.gov/visit/celebration-park)

This source does not resolve site-specific calibrated ages for the separate phases. [Source 1](https://data.visitmt.com/data/site/11052)
- **Pictograph Cave:** The park reports an approximately 9,000-year-old projectile point, a 2,145-year-old pigment measurement and much later painted guns, but the available park account does not show calibrated ages or uncertainty. Scott, Davis and Adovasio (2014), doi:10.1179/2052546X13Y.0000000001, directly dated four objects to the Late Prehistoric period. Exact lab windows remain to be verified. [Source 1](https://fwp.mt.gov/binaries/content/assets/fwp/montana-outdoors/2010/pictographcave.pdf) · [Source 2](https://fwp.mt.gov/stateparks/pictograph-cave/)
- **Obsidian Cliff quarry:** NPS places first obsidian quarrying more than 11,000 years ago. An exact earliest window cannot be inferred from the lower bound. Ohio Hopewell obsidian provenance is valuable evidence for a later mapped exchange endpoint if directly dated contexts are available. [Source 1](https://www.nps.gov/yell/learn/historyculture/obsidiancliff.htm?fullweb=1) · [Source 2](https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NRHP/96000973_text)
- **Mummy Cave (North Fork Shoshone):** Original abstract says the record begins more than 9,000 years ago. Seek dated stratigraphic components and calibrate legacy radiocarbon assays before mapping numeric windows. [Source 1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17740426/) · [Source 2](https://www.nps.gov/articles/archeology-archeological-significance-yellowstone-lake.htm)
- **Medicine Lodge Creek archaeological site:** State archaeological synthesis reports habitation dating back about 10,000 years, without giving a specific archaeological sample or diagnostic age. Hold for detailed chronology. Rock-art dates must be independent of tool dates.
