# Islands, connected histories and readable guides

Version 0.21 adds 77 records: 74 shared observations and three explanations specific to the stone-money topic. The atlas now contains 2,394 records, including 2,321 shared records and 73 focused topic records. Five contextual movies add 41 chapters; the independent rai movie adds 12.

## Seven new guides

- Borneo and Brunei: Niah’s early forest occupation, Santubong and Kota Batu, river ports, colonial interventions and the beginnings of oil exports.
- Cebu and neighbouring Philippine islands: archaeological finds, Butuan’s plank-built boats, an existing maritime trading world, Mactan, colonial building and port growth.
- Timor: early fishing evidence, hilltop communities, Dutch and Portuguese footholds, local alliances and resistance.
- New Guinea and its islands: early crossings, highland crafts, cultivation, changing pottery connections and later colonial encounters.
- Palau: early occupation, cemeteries, inhabited earthworks, quarrying and maritime relationships.
- Micronesian island networks: changing connections between islands, documented voyages and the arrival of colonial administrations.
- Yap’s rai: stone selection and manufacture, working and provisioning in Palau, transport, remembered ownership and obligations, commercial shipping and colonial pressure.

The regional movies keep the master map’s shared clock and surrounding history. The rai topic has its own timeline; named communities, archaeological finds and documented voyages remain available in the shared atlas. The movie and illustrated guide share one Subjects card.

## What the evidence changed

Butuan boat 1 uses the published AMS result of 777–988 CE for the sampled timber, rather than the frequently repeated older 320 CE attribution. The timber date does not give a launch date. Early occupation and burial at Palauan quarry sites are kept separate from the much later stone-money industry. Metuker’s early-to-middle eighteenth-century onset is an archaeological interpretation; other quarries without defensible dates remain available as undated evidence rather than receiving invented origins.

Mactan’s fuller account of Lapulapu, Humabon and Magellan enriches the existing expedition record. No second battle marker was added. The Falklands/Islas Malvinas and Galápagos additions distinguish debated early presence from dated settlements and historical visits; see [the island evidence notes](island-history-evidence.md).

Coordinates identify public named places and, where the source permits, archaeological sites. Regional locality references are labeled accordingly. Approximate coordinates are not building plans. The six new place guides have labeled modern terrain maps; the rai subject guide has modern and historical photographs with individual credits.

## Writing, pictures and sources

All 43 earlier guides receive additional original, source-linked narrative and verified Wikipedia reading links. Six individually licensed photographs appear across 12 guides. Images retain their own licenses; the atlas’s noncommercial license does not restrict public-domain or CC BY material.

The [Sources, attributions & licenses register](../sites/sources-attributions.html) brings together canonical record citations, guide sources, further reading, public provenance manifests, and complete third-party notices. It distinguishes references from reproduced material and identifies unverified reuse rights. Wikipedia articles are linked; their prose is not copied under the atlas’s noncommercial license.

## Research and remaining coverage

Two Consensus Deep reviews supported source discovery. Their original responses remain private; public guides link independently checked scholarly and institutional sources. This release used nine Consensus tool calls, including status retrievals, from the newly authorized allowance of 75; 66 remain. This is a conservative tool-call ledger, not an estimate of account credits.

These guides deepen selected island histories; they do not inventory every community. Local settlement sequences, smaller islands, archaeological road and field systems, and changes between dated observations remain priorities for further evidence. No blank interval is interpreted as abandonment without evidence.
