# A shared historical clock

The atlas opens with Watch a story set to Americas: all histories, centered on the Americas, automatically playing from the earliest included evidence to 1934, with one date governing all evidence everywhere. A story or expedition changes emphasis and camera, never temporal eligibility or the surrounding population. The same rule applies to every timeline. Zooming out always reveals contemporaneous places, routes, conflict, losses and political context. Existing user layer preferences remain explicit and visible; returning to the whole atlas restores the complete view.

Keep focused stories and expedition controls. Highlight a selected itinerary while animating every contemporaneous itinerary on the shared date. Gate visits by their actual arrival dates, not merely the year. Retain persistent earlier-place and wreck semantics without asserting continuous occupation. Show a concise multi-region context list with evidence links; avoid implying a complete historical census.

The broad frame covers 95°E eastward across the Pacific through the Americas to 30°W, 60°S–80°N. Expedition playback omits portions beyond that scope, including European/African port holds; preserve the complete itinerary in source details. Scope crossings are interpolated schematic positions, not newly documented dates. A changing viewport must never change the global clock or erase other regions.

Smart movie speed must allow documented journeys and historical events to be seen while traversing long prehistoric intervals efficiently. Whole-atlas date controls remain easy to reach. Political boundaries use their documented date semantics and historical names where established; separate source geographic labels from verified contemporary titles.

Add independently source-checked Hawaiʻi arrival/exchange and earlier western Pacific sea-crossing evidence, preserving competing chronologies, unresolved transport dates and unknown cessation. Audit Beagle-era South American context and add selected source-linked omissions. No inferred uninterrupted traffic, invented exact ancient tracks, or fabricated absence of conflict. Consensus Deep calls, including polls, remain under the standing total of 100. Raw research reports stay private.

Acceptance: at 1492 Columbus appears in the same temporal world as Taos; every selected voyage retains communities and other events; a story's zoom-out retains other regions; off-scope voyage time is skipped; Hawaiʻi connections and early offshore evidence can be found on the timeline; desktop/mobile layouts remain usable.

## Additional user requirements

- Chaco opens at settlement scale, with distinct documented great houses and communities. Separate uncertainty about a regional interpretation from the known locations of buildings.
- Expand actual mapped places across Four Corners (Hovenweep, Mesa Verde, Canyons of the Ancients), mound-building regions, Mesoamerica and the Andes. Regional influence polygons are context, not substitutes for settlements. Preserve accurate source coordinates and display building plans where verifiable geometry is available.
- Use a topographic basemap; include detailed terrain for local Four Corners scenes. Keep georeferencing correct when panning, recentering and crossing the dateline.

- Close zoom exposes source dates for local construction and occupation phases, while offscreen rendering is culled without changing chronology. No inferred continuous growth in town area.
- Hawaiian close views include selected known places and named inter-island conflicts, preserving uncertain dates, regional event positions and disputed responsibility separately.
