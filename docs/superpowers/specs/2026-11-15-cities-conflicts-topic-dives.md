# Cities, conflicts, northern peoples and topic dives

The next atlas revision expands actual mapped settlements and conflict events while preserving the shared world in ordinary stories and expeditions. It adds an explicitly separate Topic dives mode, approved by the user, with its own clock and only its subject on the map. First topics: horses, maize, trade networks and languages. Endorsed later subjects: sweet potatoes, dogs, boats, pottery, obsidian exchange and copper working.

## Coverage and meaning

Audit Machu Picchu and Inka centers, Aztec/Mexica and other Nahua towns, Maya and other American city traditions against named primary inventories. Map independently supported places and phases, not an empire-sized circle in place of cities. Publish a coverage ledger naming what was included, already present, held for missing evidence and still outside the surveyed sources. Never claim every known city from a selective expansion. Public heritage locators are acceptable when clearly distinguished from buildings and surveyed footprints; replace sanctuary-wide proxies with better public archaeological anchors where verified. Administrative directory placeholder dates must never become ancient foundation dates.

Add dated locality-level conflict markers spanning French/British/Indigenous conflicts in Canada, the French and Indian War, War of 1812, U.S. Civil War, Texas and Mexican-American conflicts, and substantial Caribbean, Central and South American coverage. Name the participants/alliances and event type; distinguish battles, massacres and raids. Regional wars do not substitute for local events. Do not invent tactical positions, casualty precision, perpetrators or outcomes.

A contextual Peoples of the North story follows settlement, seasonal mobility, adaptation, sea ice and watercraft. Distinguish archaeological cultures, descendant peoples, languages and genetic evidence. Test the violent-displacement hypothesis against sources; do not encode a cultural disappearance as proof of extermination, or limited Dorset boat evidence as established absence of boats.

## Topic mode and explanatory depth

Regular Watch, Explore and expeditions retain a single global date. Topic mode is reachable from navigation/Library and visibly labeled as focused and independent. Entering it pauses and saves the regular clock and viewing state. Leaving restores that state. Topic changes, manual dates, playback, source review navigation and browser back/forward must not leak topic filters or time into the regular atlas. Direct topic links work after a fresh page load. Only relevant topic records and geographic reference terrain are shown; unrelated voyages, hazards, rails, borders and events do not flood the map.

Horses follow dated evidence for introduction, Indigenous adoption and spread rather than an invented smooth front. Maize distinguishes domestication, arrival and later dietary dependence. Trade explains partners, goods, transport and supported versus inferred consequences for people, technologies and beliefs. Language history uses historical attestation/distribution and a companion family tree: ancestry, borrowing and contact are distinct; modern coordinates cannot be backdated to 1799; colonial borders do not imply universal European-language use; disputed macrofamilies are not established common roots.

Every regular story step gains substantive, source-linked explanatory material: travel/craft where known, subsistence/building or exchange mechanisms, and concrete limits. Unknown boat design is stated instead of illustrated as a documented craft. The original summary remains readable, with additional detail accessible on desktop and mobile.

## Approach and constraints

Reuse the existing TypeScript/SVG atlas and JSON evidence model. Source-defined imports and curated original factual records are preferable to unsourced bulk gazetteers or decorative density. A separate topic controller and topic registry keep focused playback out of the shared movie state. Reuse story transport/record inspection, extending scene detail rather than building a second map engine. Existing licenses apply; preserve third-party terms and keep raw Consensus reports private. Consensus Deep only, at most 100 cumulative calls including polling. End the work with a tested reviewable PR; merging/deploying this revision requires a later release instruction.
