# Pacific crops, dogs, boats and shared context

Build three additional separate-topic movies: sweet potatoes, boats and voyaging, and dogs. The user explicitly approved all three. Use the existing independent topic clock and map; ordinary Watch and Explore remain one shared historical world. Deliver a tested PR, not an unrequested deployment.

Sweet potatoes lead with South American origins and the timing and mechanisms of movement into the Pacific. Separate dated remains, contextual dates, crop-genetic models, historical specimens, linguistic comparisons and independent human-contact evidence. A lineage divergence is not a crop-arrival date; human admixture does not identify a cargo, landing beach, direction of travel or repeated voyages. Explain competing interpretations and the strongest supported conclusions without presenting all hypotheses as equally well supported. Include later routes and island adaptations where evidence permits.

Boats distinguish actual surviving craft and parts, contemporary observations, experiments and inferred water crossings. A crossing can demonstrate watercraft use without revealing hull or sail design. Cover Asian departure and Pacific island connections as well as relevant American and Arctic craft, within the atlas's existing extent and 1934 ceiling. Modern experiments may explain feasibility in the narrative but must not be backdated as archaeological finds.

Dogs distinguish dated individual animals, population/ancestry models, human transport and local roles. Trace American and Pacific histories, including supported island arrivals, losses and colonial changes. Dog genes are not a direct map of human ethnicity or language. Empty samples do not establish absence or extinction.
