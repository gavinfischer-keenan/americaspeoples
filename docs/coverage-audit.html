First priority: local sites in thinly covered mainland regions

Colombia, Venezuela, Guianas, Ecuador, inland Brazil, northern Mexico and the Central American isthmus have broad summaries but few individually encoded dated places. Seek named sites, sample/phase chronology and public geographic precision; do not convert a continental synthesis into a settlement.

Middle Holocene continuity

Across North America, South America and the Caribbean, arrival / early evidence and late centers are better collected than intermediate periods. Target10,000–1,000BCE transitions region by region; query repeated-use, gaps, environmental change and survey limits separately.

Island histories after arrival

Many Pacific islands have first-settlement evidence with thin subsequent histories, especially500–1500CE and1501–1934CE. Seek site-level habitation, exchange, language-history alternatives and dated abandonment/continuity evidence. Early human absence assessments are evidence about surveys, not automatically population absence.

North American late and colonial history

Great Lakes, prairie, northern Canada / Arctic and Northeast need more local sequences and1500–1934CE evidence. Existing Four Corners detail is not a substitute for those regions. Community identity, language and genetic ancestry must remain distinct.

Pending latest batch

Latest Guianas, Cuba, Lesser Antilles, central South America, Venezuela, Amazonia, Brazil and Andean syntheses identify research leads. They are not all integrated facts yet. A wrong Saint Lucia citation and mismatched generated claims are quarantined; verify original studies before filling cells.

Climate / disaster completeness

New NOAA ENSO subset starts1525CE; earlier ENSO remains an independent proxy-reconstruction research gap. Smithsonian covers Holocene eruptions, not all Pleistocene events. NOAA tsunami records are historically incomplete. Ice snapshots are North America25–1.5ka; Greenland, Pacific sea ice, ancient shorelines and navigable water need separate data. Drought/heat/cold layers currently use sourced regional findings, not continuous gridded reconstructions.
