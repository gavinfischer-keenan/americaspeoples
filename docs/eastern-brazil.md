# Eastern Brazil: villages, exchange and conflict evidence, 501–1500 CE

Eastern Brazil’s missing centuries now contain named villages, camps and burial places. Wooden house piles, pottery, hearths and human remains establish local episodes; graphite and a carved green-stone pendant reveal connections beyond the places where they were found. The 30 map records comprise 29 evidence entries and one linked route view of the Canaã pottery evidence. No new battle marker is assigned: the reviewed bone modifications and suspected injuries do not establish a securely dated conflict in this campaign.

This release adds **30 shared map records** at **28 distinct named localities** within the study rectangle. The master map now has 3,419 shared observations and 73 focused topic records.

## Settlements and activity

### Morro Grande — charcoal in a burial urn

At Morro Grande, people used large pots and painted bowls in a burial ceremony. Charcoal recovered from inside an urn has a calibrated age range of 130–560 CE.

The urn lay in the part of the site where excavators also found much older hearths. Later funerary material came from another area of the settlement, where people returned to use the land.

**When:** Beta-84333: 1820–1390 cal BP at 2σ in Macario et al. 2009; 1817–1394 cal BP in Buarque et al. 2020.

**Where:** Morro Grande, Araruama. Site area, approximately 0.5 km.

### Morro Grande — a hearth beside a later burial

People returned to a different part of Morro Grande and lit a fire near a burial. The charcoal dates the fire within a broad range that includes the centuries before and after Portuguese arrival.

The dated charcoal came from the same funerary arrangement as pottery dated independently by thermoluminescence. Differences in the vessels from this sector led the team to consider whether people returned to the site after a long interval.

### Bananeiras — a woman buried beside the Araruama lagoon

At Bananeiras, mourners placed a young woman in a large clay urn. Painted bowls and two pendants made from marine shells accompanied her.

The burial lay near the Araruama lagoon. The natural shell layers helped preserve her bones. The dug area was small because buildings already surrounded it, so this burial provides a much more precise picture than the settlement layout. A separate radiocarbon study of pottery residues at Bananeiras found evidence of sixteenth-century activity.

**When:** Beta-171160: 450–269 cal BP at 2σ in Emenes et al. 2026. The earlier Buarque et al. 2020 calibration reports 1420–1510 CE and 1600–1620 CE as separate possibilities.

**Where:** Bananeiras. Archaeological locality rounded to 0.001°.

### Ilha do Cabo Frio — a cremation near Una pottery

On Ilha do Cabo Frio, excavators found a cremation close to pottery of the Una tradition. Elsewhere at the site, they recorded pierced shark teeth and cut marine shells.

The island's archaeological deposits document fishing and distinct ways of preparing the dead. The investigators considered contact with people making Una pottery as one possible explanation for the new burial practice.

**When:** Beta-222741: 1610–1420 cal BP.

**Where:** Ilha do Cabo Frio, Arraial do Cabo. Island reference point, within a few kilometers of the site shown on the field map.

### Zé Espinho — a later burial in the Guaratiba shell mounds

On the tidal plain at Guaratiba, people buried their dead among shell deposits. A dated context in the Zé Espinho complex places one burial between 600 and 1222 CE.

The nearby mangroves, channels and coastal forests offered fishing and gathering grounds. The deposits preserve the traces of repeated activity in that setting, including a layer with ceramic sherds and several burial contexts.

**When:** Burial 3B / individual 2061: 600–1222 cal CE at 2σ from context.

**Where:** Zé Espinho. Rounded published shell-mound complex location.

### Mata dos Bentes — fired pottery on a Rio Novo hill

Residents of a hill near present-day Rio Novo shaped and fired clay vessels. Excavators recovered corrugated and painted pottery alongside worked stone.

The collection preserves differently finished vessels, including pieces with white and red slips and surfaces marked by fingertips. Later finds are evidence of another period of use.

**When:** 604–804 CE (TL, Corrêa Table 14).

**Where:** Mata dos Bentes. Rounded published archaeological-site reference, approximately 0.5 km.

### Primavera — pottery and stone on a hill above the Rio Novo drainage

### Poça — painted and textured pottery in the hills

At Poça, people used pots with corrugated ridges, incised marks, and red or white surface coatings. Three pieces of fired pottery provide dates between the twelfth and fifteenth centuries.

Only a small area was excavated. Its finds help show how people used several nearby hills in the Zona da Mata.

**When:** 1164–1344, 1294–1454 and 1339–1479 CE (three TL estimates).

### Emílio Barão — household pottery and worked stone

### Córrego do Maranhão — dated hearths in a Carangola settlement

On a ridge near Carangola, people left fired pots and charcoal in patches where they worked and used hearths. Charcoal and pottery were found together in a lens exposed along a road cut.

Archaeologists analyzed thousands of fragments from concentrations distributed over the ridge. Some pots had corrugated or incised surfaces, while others carried red and white finishes. The spatial distribution and chronology suggest the ridge was used in different episodes.

**When:** Mageste 2017 Table 10, 2σ: Beta-245433 670–550, Beta-290691 660–520, Beta-290692 550–460, Beta-290693 550–470, Beta-290694 and -290695 510–300 cal BP.

### Santa Marina — household traces in the Paraíba Valley

Dark patches of soil at Santa Marina marked places where people had worked or lived. Excavators recovered fragments of clay vessels and worked stone, and two charcoal samples date activity to 1400–1475 CE.

The excavations recovered materials from household activity. The larger distribution of dark patches may reflect houses and other work areas that shifted as people reused the settlement.

### Rio Preto Oeste — pottery, cooking and a reused urn

On a sandy terrace near São Mateus, people made clay vessels and cooked over fires. Excavators found an urn cut or broken before it was used for burial, and pottery fragments bearing soot and marks left by fingers.

The excavators found only scattered stone artifacts alongside abundant pottery. Many vessels fit the regional Aratu tradition, while some surface treatments resemble those used by Tupiguarani potters. Those parallels led the team to propose contact among nearby communities.

**When:** Excavator's concluding archaeological assignment to the fourteenth century CE, Caldarelli 2005, pp. 135–136.

**Where:** Rio Preto Oeste 1 (RPO-1), São Mateus. Excavated settlement area rounded to the nearest 0.001°.

### Arapuan — a burial in the top of an older shell mound

People buried an individual in the upper part of an older shell mound near Guanabara Bay. Bone chemistry suggests this person's diet included a greater share of land-based food than a much older individual buried deeper in the mound.

Arapuan contains burials from widely separated periods. The later individual’s date and diet provide evidence of changing uses of the mound and different ways of obtaining food around the bay.

**When:** Burial 18: 477–312 cal BP (2σ) in Cunha et al. 2022, Table 1.

**Where:** Arapuan, Guapimirim. Rounded survey location, approximately 0.5 km.

### Boca do Rio — a village on wooden piles

In the Turiaçu River, people built on a long central framework of posts with smaller clusters around it. Archaeologists recorded 1,071 surviving piles, with figurines and more elaborate pottery concentrated near the middle.

Cooking vessels cluster in the areas interpreted as houses, while fine pottery and figurines occur near the likely gathering space. A carved greenstone pendant from the central area carries evidence of contact beyond the Maranhão wetlands. Its stone was identified by X-ray diffraction and Raman spectroscopy, while the deposit that supplied it is still unknown.

**When:** Beta-406836, wooden pile: 889–1015 cal CE (2023 Table 2). Navarro’s earlier Table 1 gives 885–995 cal CE for the same sample.

**Where:** Boca do Rio. Rounded plan-derived site-area reference, approximately 0.2 km.

### Cabeludo — a large village on the Paruá River

More than a thousand timber piles survive at Cabeludo. A long central cluster and seven smaller groups suggest separate places for gathering and living. A line of posts may have carried a walkway between two groups.

The village stood in the Paruá, a tributary of the Turiaçu. Cooking pottery was common in the smaller groups, while figurines and more elaborate pottery concentrated in the larger space.

**When:** Wood Beta-458479 773–987, Beta-430864 885–1013, Beta-458480 990–1148, Beta-515390 1046–1222 cal CE; vessel soot Beta-515391 892–1020 and Beta-492361 995–1151 cal CE.

**Where:** Cabeludo. Rounded plan-derived site-area reference, approximately 0.2 km.

### Caboclo — three clusters of stilt-house posts

Surveyors mapped 161 wooden piles in three groups in the Turiaçu channel. The groups are similar enough in scale that the excavator interprets them as possible separate houses.

At Caboclo, the clusters could each have accommodated domestic and communal activities. Boca do Rio and Cabeludo have substantially larger central clusters, showing how neighboring settlements could organize their spaces differently.

**When:** Beta-406835, wooden pile: 893–1023 cal CE (2023 Table 2).

**Where:** Caboclo stilt village. Rounded plan-derived site-area reference, approximately 0.2 km.

[Map and full evidence](../index.html#review/eb-ne-caboclo) · [Alexandre Guida Navarro: New evidence for late first-millennium AD stilt-house settlements in Eastern Amazonia](https://www.fapema.br/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/Artigo-na-Antiquity.pdf) · [Christopher Davis and Alexandre Guida Navarro: Plan of prehistoric stilt village in Maranhão Brazil may resemble the Pleiades](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jasrep.2023.104123)

### Armíndio — groups of houses in the Turiaçu

The piles at Armíndio are spread among small clusters in two groups. Similar-sized clusters led the survey team to interpret them as the supports of separate houses.

The settlement occupied the Turiaçu channel within the seasonally flooded Maranhão wetlands. Its two sets of posts differ from the large central arrangement at Boca do Rio.

**When:** Beta-404757, wooden pile: 1046–1222 cal CE (2023 Table 2).

**Where:** Armíndio. Rounded plan-derived site-area reference, approximately 0.2 km.

[Map and full evidence](../index.html#review/eb-ne-armindio) · [Alexandre Guida Navarro: New evidence for late first-millennium AD stilt-house settlements in Eastern Amazonia](https://www.fapema.br/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/Artigo-na-Antiquity.pdf) · [Christopher Davis and Alexandre Guida Navarro: Plan of prehistoric stilt village in Maranhão Brazil may resemble the Pleiades](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jasrep.2023.104123)

### Encantado — wooden piles in the Pericumã basin

People also placed timbers in the seasonally inundated Pericumã basin near present-day Pinheiro, one river basin east of the Turiaçu villages. Two wooden posts date to the late first millennium CE.

Encantado gives the settlement pattern geographic depth across neighboring wetlands. The dated timbers overlap in age with posts at some Turiaçu sites. Similar use of flooded waterways is visible in both basins.

**When:** Wood Beta-406837 770–966 and Beta-492359 771–969 cal CE (2023 Table 2).

**Where:** Encantado stilt village. Approximate river and lake locality read from the published regional graticule, about 2 km.

[Map and full evidence](../index.html#review/eb-ne-encantado) · [Alexandre Guida Navarro: New evidence for late first-millennium AD stilt-house settlements in Eastern Amazonia](https://www.fapema.br/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/Artigo-na-Antiquity.pdf) · [Christopher Davis and Alexandre Guida Navarro: Plan of prehistoric stilt village in Maranhão Brazil may resemble the Pleiades](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jasrep.2023.104123)

### Formoso — wooden piles and pottery in a seasonal lake

Above a seasonally flooded lake in Maranhão, the people of Formoso built structures on wooden posts. Pottery from the site includes incised and punctured decoration, painted vessels and modeled figures.

The ages come from timber posts and a carbon deposit on pottery. The dates bring Formoso into view earlier than the best-known settlements to the north. Its local pottery brings another set of designs into the history of Maranhão’s wetland settlements.

**When:** POZ-11300 wood 441–637 cal CE. Later wood: Beta-576497 664–842, Beta-576496 and -576498 680–876, Beta-576500 771–985, Beta-512409 and -576499 772–990. Vessel soot Beta-512408 892–1020 cal CE.

**Where:** Formoso stilt village. Rounded surveyed site-area coordinates, approximately 0.2 km.

[Map and full evidence](../index.html#review/eb-ne-formoso) · [Christopher Davis and Alexandre Guida Navarro: Plan of prehistoric stilt village in Maranhão Brazil may resemble the Pleiades](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jasrep.2023.104123)

### Praça de Piragiba — clay urns beside the Ribeirão Santana

People living beside the Ribeirão Santana buried their dead in clay urns with ceramic lids. The smooth, pear-shaped vessels were often grouped in small clusters. A date on bone from one burial places this use of Piragiba in 1040–1257 CE.

Repeated excavations and survey have traced the village deposits beneath the modern settlement and across the plain. Knapped stone blades and production debris also occur across the locality. Technological study found a mixture that includes older material, so the urns provide the clearest link to the dated community.

**When:** Femur from burial Un1Ur2, 1040–1257 cal CE, published preliminary calibration. Original burial inventory reports GIF-10999 (870 ± 50 radiocarbon BP).

**Where:** Praça de Piragiba. Rounded survey reference, approximately 1 km across the recorded village area.

### Serra do Evaristo I — pottery and burials above Baturité

On the slopes above Baturité, people made large pottery vessels and used some of them to bury individuals. Excavators recovered eighteen vessels and identified human bones in six.

The excavated deposits included both adults and children, and the excavators identified several primary burials. The shapes of the vessels link the site to the pottery archaeologists call Aratu.

**When:** UE011, Beta-328350 charcoal: 653–555 cal BP, printed as 1395–1297 cal CE.

**Where:** Serra do Evaristo I. Rounded published site-area coordinate, approximately 0.2 km.

### Serra da Barriga — a hilltop village in Alagoas

Indigenous potters on the Serra da Barriga made vessels for cooking and other daily use, along with forms used in burial. Excavation exposed a living surface with pottery from the village on the ridge.

The surviving ceramics include graphite-finished pieces, smooth everyday vessels, and large containers. Excavated areas give a view of an Indigenous village built centuries before the period in which the Serra da Barriga became central to Palmares.

**When:** Site ALUDPSB1 pottery, 1050 ± 67 CE, published thermoluminescence result.

**Where:** Serra da Barriga (SB1). Rounded ridge-area reference, approximately 2 km across the protected locality.

### Canaã — graphite in everyday pottery beside the Rio Colônia

At Canaã, potters mixed graphite into some clay vessels and coated others with it. Near broken pots, archaeologists found stone tools and bones of armadillos and other animals, suggesting an area where people prepared food.

One graphite-coated sherd came from the deepest excavated sample. Earlier craft practice can therefore be dated apart from the site's later materials. Elemental tests on other sherds found similarities with graphite from the Jequié area.

**When:** Graphite-coated sherd C-06-1 (DAT-5426): 620 ± 95 years by thermoluminescence in the report signed February 2021, approximately 1306–1496 CE.

**Where:** Canaã, Rio Colônia. Rounded reservoir-area reference, approximately 5 km.

[Map and full evidence](../index.html#review/eb-ne-canaa-pottery) · [Morgana Cavalcante Ribeiro: História indígena de longa duração no Vale do Rio Colônia: o estudo de caso do sítio arqueológico Canaã](https://www.univasf.edu.br/~tcc/00001f/00001f1f.pdf) · [Morgana Cavalcante Ribeiro, Rodrigo Lessa Costa, Maria Conceição Soares Meneses Lage, and Benedito Batista Farias Filho: O uso do grafite enquanto escolha técnica em cerâmicas da tradição Aratu no sul da Bahia](https://revista.sabnet.org/ojs/index.php/sab/article/download/1005/855) · [Prefeitura Municipal de Itabuna and Fundação Instituto de Pesquisas Econômicas: Plano Municipal de Saneamento Básico de Itabuna: Produto 2, Diagnóstico](https://itabuna.ba.gov.br/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/6039-Itabuna-Saneamento-Produto-2-Diagnostico-v7-logo-itabuna.pdf)

### Canaã — possible graphite supply from the Jequié area

The strongest match among the tested deposits points toward the Jequié area, roughly 130 km from Canaã. Potters used graphite as a coating and mixed it into clay.

A portable X-ray fluorescence study compared four Canaã fragments with samples from the Bahia–Minas graphite province. Similar titanium and barium signals favored the Jequié-area sample among the materials tested. People could have procured graphite themselves or obtained it through intermediaries. The line links a candidate source region to the find area.

**When:** Graphite-coated sherd C-06-1 (DAT-5426): 620 ± 95 years by thermoluminescence in the report signed February 2021, approximately 1306–1496 CE.

**Where:** Jequié graphite source region. Municipal reference for the candidate source area, on the order of tens of kilometres.

### Jericoacoara 1 — fires and tools between coastal dunes

In the dunes of western Ceará, people lit hearths, chipped stone tools and used pottery. Some tools were retouched or reused for other tasks, and the excavated layers held shell as well as charcoal.

People collected suitable pebbles and stone in the coastal plain. The tool assemblages include flakes, hammerstones and grinders. The archaeologists relate the range of activities to a coastal economy that combined collecting, fishing and hunting.

**When:** C1 charcoal Beta-504658 1526–1367 cal BP (424–583 CE), C1 shell Beta-504659 1487–1285 cal BP (463–665 CE), C2 and C5 charcoal Beta-478976 and -478975 1267–1070 cal BP (683–880 CE), and C1 charcoal Beta-290953 1055–924 cal BP (895–1026 CE). Earlier C2 sample omitted.

**Where:** Jericoacoara 1. Rounded placement near excavation Concentrations 1 and 2, site spread about 1 km.

### Baixio dos Lopes — painted pottery in a secondary burial

Near Brejo Santo, mourners placed an adult’s bones in a large vessel painted on the inside and covered the vessel with a ceramic lid. The vessel belongs to the polychrome pottery archaeologists call Tupiguarani.

Stone flakes and tools occurred with the burial. The placement of selected remains in a painted serving vessel is evidence of how a useful pot acquired a new role in a ceremony.

**When:** Beta-447236, carbonate from two teeth of one adult, 1280–1320 or 1350–1385 cal CE.

**Where:** Baixio dos Lopes. Rounded site-area reference from the published survey, approximately 0.2 km.

[Map and full evidence](../index.html#review/eb-ne-baixio-dos-lopes) · [Ana Solari, Daniela Cisneiros, Anne-Marie Pessis, Gabriela Martin, and Elisabeth Medeiros: Os remanescentes humanos das urnas funerárias Tupi-Guarani no sítio Baixio dos Lopes, Brejo Santo – CE (720 ± 30 AP)](https://periodicos.ufpe.br/revistas/clioarqueologica/article/view/254543)

### Pedra do Cachorro — mortuary use of a Catimbau rock shelter

In the sandstone shelter of Pedra do Cachorro, people laid an adult man to rest after handling and rearranging his remains. Cuts and fractures on the bones helped archaeologists reconstruct this treatment.

The site's excavators identified cut marks from disarticulation and percussion marks on fresh bone. These observations help distinguish the way the body was prepared from the site's earlier primary interments.

**When:** Human long bone PCe050hbsS01, Sepultamento 01 / Beta-424624: 1265–1305 and 1365–1375 cal CE (2σ), Quadro 1.

**Where:** Pedra do Cachorro, Buíque. Rounded published site-area reference, approximately 0.2 km.

### Alcobaça — a dated burial in a Catimbau shelter

At Alcobaça, excavators found human burials among traces of pottery-making, basketry and other activity. The directly dated burial dates to 992–1152 CE.

Several earlier deposits included burned remains and woven plant fibres, while the bones in the dated burial showed no signs of burning. Bone chemistry also differed from individuals studied at two neighboring shelters.

**When:** AL.55-1 / Beta-474452, adult rib, 958–798 cal BP (992–1152 CE).

**Where:** Alcobaça. Rounded published site coordinate, approximately 0.2 km.

## Evidence still needing work

- **Violence at Zé Espinho:** The possible ray-spine injury and disputed fracture context belong to a different individual from the dated burial. They cannot supply a dated conflict marker. [Source 1](https://www.colibri.udelar.edu.uy/jspui/bitstream/20.500.12008/4520/1/FHCE-l%C3%B3pez_mazz_pdf.pdf)
- **Pedra do Cachorro: cut and broken bones:** The excavators attribute the marks to treatment of the dead. The dated burial is included, but cause of death and participants are unknown. [Source 1](https://doi.org/10.20891/clio.v31i1p105-135)
- **Boca do Rio pendant source:** The pendant is included with the village. Mineral analysis supports a nonlocal material, but no specific quarry, seller, journey or direct Caribbean route is established. [Source 1](https://www.scielo.br/j/bgoeldi/a/F8n4zYkjbChqMFctJCzWrhC/?lang=pt)
- **Canaã graphite:** The proposed source connection is included as tentative. Jequié is the closest of the tested comparisons, while other deposits remain untested. A separate coated sherd supplies the approximate date context. [Source 1](https://revista.sabnet.org/ojs/index.php/sab/article/download/1005/855) · [Source 2](https://www.univasf.edu.br/~tcc/00001f/00001f1f.pdf)
- **Rio Preto Oeste 1 chronology:** The actual hearths, pottery and urn are included under the excavators’ provisional fourteenth-century interpretation. Internally inconsistent sample ages and possible contamination prevent a more precise chronology. [Source 1](https://www.academia.edu/36033086/S%C3%ADtioArqueol%C3%B3gico_RPO1_ES_SalvamentoArqueol%C3%B3gico_Relat%C3%B3rioFinal_2005)
- **Bananeiras chronology:** The bone record uses the modern reported 1500–1681 CE calibration. The older 1420–1510 and 1600–1620 alternatives remain in its review. - **Peruaçu and Abrigo do Malhador:** Maize and hearth studies offer promising local histories.
