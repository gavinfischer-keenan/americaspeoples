# Current source verification

Version **0.29.0** contains **11,201 records: 11,128 shared observations and 73 focused topic records**. It includes 175 dated voyage itineraries, 126 expedition activity periods, 90 guides, 84 contextual stories and 28 independent topic movies. The guide collection contains 127 terrain maps, 26 additional source-map sheets and 39 individually credited photographs or other media.

The four destinations are Americas, Africa & human origins, Europe & Mediterranean, and Asia & Pacific. The last three contain only a coastline basemap and **COMING SOON**. They have no evidence records or historical overlays. One repository and shared engine serve separate atlas manifests. Keyboard navigation, direct links and phone widths from 320 to 800 pixels were checked; returning to Americas preserves its date, camera, selection and filters. The [repository plan](multi-atlas-plan.md) defines future data ownership and cross-atlas references.

The new research adds 38 trade observations and enriches the existing Lake Huron obsidian finding, retaining one canonical record for its two flakes. Thirty additions belong before 1200 CE; eight describe later Mexica and Inka networks. The trade movie adds 28 chapters. Shared assemblages at Plum Piece and Uluburun are cross-linked instead of presented as independent confirmations. Source dates, material provenance and schematic connections remain distinct from direct journeys or continuous traffic.

Fourteen added itineraries and 145 new dated voyage records expand American and polar coverage. The shared clock ends on 31 December 1934; Hawaiʻi’s separate political topic retains its later dates. A 12-scene Norse North Atlantic story separates Greenland settlement arrivals and inferred abandonment, Iceland continuity, the 1408 Hvalsey wedding and the precisely dated 1021 cut wood at L’Anse aux Meadows. Papar testimony remains a later written account, not a fabricated exact voyage.

The shipwreck topic contains 7,512 loss records from selected public inventories and archaeological sources. Only supported loss dates or intervals enter the timeline; discovery and survey dates do not replace them. Catalogue aliases, unresolved date ranges, coordinate qualifications and original included source fields remain inspectable. A fresh loss brightens and fades toward a subtle persistent marker. This display does not assert that a hull survives or that the collection is a complete worldwide census. Six maritime graveyard guides accompany Northwest Passage, southern-ocean routes and L’Anse aux Meadows. Large survey, historical and bathymetric sheets retain their source resolution, credits and licences; modern weather and wave climatology is explicitly distinguished from historical conditions. See the [research expansion and limits](maritime-trade-expansion.md).

Place previews describe actual places and findings. Interpolation, scenario and source dates remain labeled; no extrapolation beyond source coverage is added.
