# Editorial evidence policy

Include archaeological evidence and verified historical sources evaluated to academic standards. Neither publication alone nor repeated assertion establishes truth. Prefer peer-reviewed research, scholarly monographs, formal excavation reports, and authenticated archival material with source criticism. Popular videos and derivative web assertions do not provide independent corroboration.

Each occupation, chronological assignment, cultural identification, movement and causal explanation is its own evidentiary claim. A source establishing human presence does not automatically establish cultural identity, origin, migration destination, or disappearance. Keep unknown values explicit.

Credible academic disagreement belongs in the dataset, visible by default. Mark the particular disputed claim with a subtle asterisk, state the competing interpretations, map each to sources and explain what remains unresolved. Temporal precision, scholarly disagreement and proposal review status are separate dimensions. Do not fabricate confidence percentages or assume alternative claims have equal support.

Source verification means checking the relevant text and metadata at the access level documented. Abstract-only checks must be described as such. Do not assert inspection of inaccessible full text or original archives known only through a scholarly synthesis. Record source locators, methods, calibration context and access limits. Bibliography and prose must be present offline; publication links can require the internet.

The starter corpus underwent an editorial source check, not an independent archaeological peer review. It contains no population estimates, migration arrows, paleo-coastlines, or claims of racial categories. White Sands is represented by an explicitly labeled public locator; data precision must never be silently upgraded. A missing or ended marker never establishes human absence, extinction, or a causal account of cultural change.

Only maintainers can promote a submitted proposal into a released dataset after scholarly review. Structural validation verifies data integrity, not scientific credibility. Keep the proposal's original, rationale and source trail available during review, and retain the record's revision history when publishing an updated corpus.

## Literature-intake policy

Abstract-only sources can support claims explicitly stated in the abstract, with access scope attached. Reviews and supplied syntheses are not independent replications of their underlying studies. Retain opposing interpretations, regional limits and original chronology. Generic environmental correlations do not establish individual motives or one universal causal story. Animal population-genetics papers are not evidence for human migrations. Asterisk strength follows explicit claim status, not vote counts, citation metrics or a computed confidence scale.
