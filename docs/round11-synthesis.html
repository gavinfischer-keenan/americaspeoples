Interpretive limits

Genetic affinity does not identify a language. Artifact sourcing does not establish a direct voyage or the identity of traders. Separate occupation phases do not establish continuous habitation. Regional counts of platforms, structures and samples are not settlement populations. Uncalibrated dates are not silently converted to calendar years. Named sites without reliable coordinates remain in the evidence catalog; regional display anchors are explicitly identified. Locality pins are unsuitable for navigation.

This release preserves critiques of Upano chronology, Southern California contact proposals, and ancient language attribution. Later ceramic continuity, modern genomes and generic regional arrival dates cannot supply missing early dates for named sites. A gap in dates is not proof of human absence.
