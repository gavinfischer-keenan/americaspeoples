# Where the atlas needs its next evidence

Reviewed against the live v0.16.0 atlas on 16 November 2026, after PR #12 deployment. The owner selected **early Central/South America and Pacific continuity first**. Australian pastoral/wool and mining movies remain in the [research queue](roadmap.md), linked to forced Indigenous relocations. This is a coverage assessment and research proposal, not newly integrated evidence.

## What was checked

- The existing 65-region, 12-period detailed coverage matrix and all 2,038 published records (1,980 shared).
- Forty-two live movie snapshots: 21 dates from the opening 68,751 BCE to 1934 CE in both the Americas and Asia-Pacific overviews, plus short playback samples starting at 6000 BCE, 500 CE and 1800 CE. This was a sampled sweep, not a frame-by-frame viewing of the entire movie. No browser errors occurred.
- The 12 existing site/culture guides, 11 contextual stories, 20 independent topics, and individual records associated with candidate archaeological complexes.

Matrix counts are distinct locality IDs with dated non-overlay site/activity records in each period. A second construction phase does not count as a second town. Regional circles, routes, undated records and earlier-history display are different measures. Rectangular regions overlap; counts are not additive and are not a census of ancient people or modern archaeological surveys. A source window may date an uncertain event rather than continuous occupation.

| 2 | Australia, 10,000 BCE–1700 CE | Only two or three localities in each of the four BCE bins; two in 1–500 CE and one in each following bin through 1700. | Add regional sequences and engineering/food-production landscapes, plus later phases at already represented places. The existing early-arrival evidence is not a substitute. |
| 2 | Guianas, before 1500 CE | No dated local-place records in the early/medieval bins; the new collection is much stronger after 1700. | Earlier Indigenous village and landscape histories, ceramic sequences, waterways and coastal/interior connections. |

These are missing collection sequences, not claims of abandonment. The Melanesian row must also be split into individual islands during research: older settlement in Near Oceania cannot establish the arrival date of communities in Remote Oceania. Similarly, pre-settlement bins in Aotearoa, Hawaiʻi and other remote islands are not automatic targets for filling. The existing audit's coarse first-arrival screen is only a triage aid; it cannot settle habitability or arrival chronology.

## What the movie makes noticeable

The early global view is sparse: the Asia-Pacific overview shows 29 current records at 8000 BCE, 19 at 6000 BCE and 16 at 4000 BCE. These counts include different evidence types and are not site or population totals. The scarcity is especially visible across Central America, much of South America and Australia.

Later the screen becomes busy with accumulated history, while evidence of activity at the selected date becomes thinner:

| Sample year | Visible records, including earlier history | Records marked current | Active connection records in view |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1250 CE | 222 | 129 | 4 |
| 1800 CE | 416 | 67 | 8 |
| 1851 CE | 436 | 29 | 3 |
| 1900 CE | 466 | 26 | 2 |
| 1934 CE | 485 | 24 | 0 |

This does not demonstrate a historical decline in settlement or trade. Foundations and older observations remain visible; many later phases and operating route windows have not been added. End-of-timeline coverage, including continuing trade and town development, deserves a dedicated pass after the first priority campaign. Route-only evidence is excluded from the locality matrix, so route coverage must be audited separately.

There is also a display issue: the bottom “established places” counter counts `history.kind === settlement`, excluding other habitation/occupation evidence. It can show zero while actual habitation records are present. Review that wording/count before using it to explain how many places existed. A useful future side panel would distinguish evidence at this date, earlier evidence with unknown continuation, and documented departure or relocation. It must not silently extend occupation to fill visual gaps.

Budj Bim and Kuk are large worked landscapes, not equivalents of a stone-built capital. Hopewell and the Colombian funerary complexes also require explicit separation of ceremonial/burial areas from residential settlement. Their value is showing how varied organized human places could be.

Existing dedicated guides cover Chaco, Hovenweep, Mesa Verde, Canyons of the Ancients, Machu Picchu/Inca heartland, and Cahokia. Those are **expansion candidates**, not missing guides. Maya centres and Pacific complexes currently have group guides; their individual centres still merit deeper treatment. Further candidates already in the older site queue include Wari, north-coast Moche centres, Tenochtitlan/Basin of Mexico, Hohokam canal communities, Marajó, Santarém and Upper Xingu networks. They need the same site-by-site inventory before selection.

## Proposed research approach

1. Start with the owner-selected early Americas and Pacific continuity. Review the existing undated and regionally placed evidence first; many useful improvements may need better source extraction rather than another broad literature search.
2. Divide each chosen region into specific places and successive time windows. Ask “what evidence follows the earlier phase?” rather than assume the same population remained, or that a settlement's disappearance means its people vanished.
3. Keep four distinct results: before supported arrival; independently supported continuing/changing activity; documented relocation or local abandonment; and later history still unknown. A quiet map is not enough to choose among them.
4. Use a small first group of Consensus Deep dossiers for early Central America, early Andean/coastal South America, and post-settlement New Guinea/Melanesia. Count every request and poll against the remaining 34 calls; preserve capacity for follow-up and source retrieval. No new Consensus calls were used for this audit: the cumulative total remains 66/100.
5. Admit original-source-supported places, construction phases, trade, violence and relocations into the shared map with dates, public geographic precision and reciprocal topic links. Add approximate observations when supported; do not demand a named ruler or exact year to map a known inhabited place.
6. Repeat the same movie-date sweep and regional audit. Evaluate whether inhabited regions now show successive activity and whether late trade remains visible, not merely whether the total number of pins increased.

My first site-dive recommendations alongside this programme are **Caral/Supe, Kuk, and the Upano centres**: they can directly improve early settlement and landscape narratives. Nan Madol and Budj Bim are strong subsequent choices for Pacific complexity and Australian continuity. These are recommendations for discussion, not newly completed movies.
