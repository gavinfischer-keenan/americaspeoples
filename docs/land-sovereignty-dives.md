# Land, sovereignty and forced movement

These three independent movies connect named places with coercion, political action, resistance and continuing communities. Their earlier human events also appear on the shared atlas. A separate topic clock lets a viewer inspect one thread without changing the date or place they left on the main map.

## What is included

- **Forced Indigenous relocations:** Garifuna deportation; Choctaw and Cherokee removal; Wybalenna and Putalina / Oyster Cove; the Diné Long Walk, confinement and return; Martín García; Carlisle; Parihaka imprisonment and invasion; Yaqui resistance at Sakakal; Cootamundra; Haŋa Roa; Palm Island; Kinchela; and Napalpí. These are selected cases across the Americas, Australia and Aotearoa New Zealand, not a complete inventory.
- **Hawaiʻi:** Lahaina, the built ʻIolani Palace, constitutional coercion, Liliʻuokalani’s accession and overthrow, U.S. involvement, trial and confinement, the Kūʻē petitions, annexation, territorial government, Kalamaʻula homesteads, wartime martial law and the 1959 statehood process.
- **Territorial purchases:** Louisiana, Florida, Gadsden, Alaska and the Danish West Indies. Treaty signing, local ceremony, ratification, payment and infrastructure are different observations. Florida is a treaty-and-claims comparison, not a simple cash sale.

## Time and location decisions

The shared atlas and ordinary topics end in **1934**. Only the Hawaiʻi topic continues through **1959**. Its four later records have explicit topic-only membership: the 1941 martial-law declaration, its 1944 ending, the June 1959 ballot and August admission. Review links can open earlier shared context at its own date; they cannot move the master map into 1959. Schools whose records stop at 1934 did not necessarily close then: Cootamundra and Kinchela continued later, as their evidence panels explain.

Dates preserve what the source actually dates. The Alaska treaty was signed on 30 March 1867, ratifications exchanged on 20 June, the Sitka ceremony held on 18 October, and the payment check issued on 1 August 1868. The 1803 Cabildo marker is the transfer of lower Louisiana in New Orleans, not the signing in Paris. Gadsden’s first agreement and revised terms are separate. Existing boundary datasets may use a different administrative milestone; the topic creates no new unsourced polygons.

Public locality points are rounded orientation anchors, not building surveys. Wybalenna, the schools, Honolulu, Sitka and Parihaka have named localities. The Sakakal evidence is only placed at a **regional Yucatán anchor with about 100 km uncertainty**, pending a verified estate location. Napalpí uses a **regional Quitilipi-area anchor with about 30 km uncertainty**, not an invented precise killing ground. Route lines join documented endpoints schematically. The Parihaka–Dunedin line does not skip the existence of intermediate custody or imply that all prisoners went to Dunedin.

## Reading the evidence

The [Aboriginal Land Council of Tasmania](https://alctas.org.au/project/wybalenna/), [Coota Girls Aboriginal Corporation](https://www.cootagirls.org.au/), [Kinchela Boys Home Aboriginal Corporation](https://kinchelaboyshome.org.au/kinchela-boys-home/history-of-kinchela-boys-home/) and [Parihaka community](https://parihaka.maori.nz/history/) supply community histories. Their accounts are not reduced to institutional founding dates. Survivor histories, official documents and archaeological observations answer different questions. Cootamundra’s survivor organization uses 1912; some administrative records use 1911. That difference remains visible. Māori education is not automatically treated as equivalent to Australian child-removal institutions.

For Hawaiʻi, the University of Hawaiʻi’s [Kūʻē petition collection](https://guides.library.manoa.hawaii.edu/kuepetitions), the queen’s own writings and [ʻIolani Palace interpretation](https://www.iolanipalace.org/sacred-palace/the-palace/imprisonment-room/) keep political agency visible. [Cleveland’s December 1893 message](https://millercenter.org/the-presidency/presidential-speeches/december-18-1893-message-regarding-hawaiian-annexation) is identified as his finding after the Blount investigation, rather than a claim that all contemporary American inquiries agreed. The [1959 Admission Act, section 7](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-73/pdf/STATUTE-73-Pg4.pdf) sets out the ballot propositions; the [admission proclamation](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/proclamation-3309-admission-the-state-hawaii-into-the-union) supplies election and admission dates. A statehood vote is not retrospective consent to overthrow or annexation.

[Nagy and Papazian’s archival study](https://doi.org/10.4000/corpusarchivos.1176) includes transcribed naval documents concerning Martín García. [Padilla Ramos’s article](https://revistas.inah.gob.mx/index.php/noroestedemexico/article/view/17178) supports the limited Sakakal episode through the inspected publisher abstract and indexed p. 94 extract; full PDF access was unavailable and is not claimed. The [Napalpí archive](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/node/317245) links primary documents used in the truth trial. Arriagada Poblete, Milin and Tuki’s [Rapa Nui study](https://doi.org/10.7764/aisth.74.15) contextualizes the 1914 photographic record; no photographs are reproduced here.

Each marker’s **Full evidence & sources** panel contains its specific claim, limitations, source link and locator. Existing canonical Garifuna, removal, Louisiana, Lahaina and railway records are reused, with reciprocal links where new evidence relates to them. Research-provider output is kept outside the public application and distribution packages. The atlas contains original summaries and citations; third-party sources retain their own rights.

## Further coverage

This release does not claim every forced relocation, reservation change, residential school or acquisition. Canadian residential schools, many Central and South American cases, Western Australian and Northern Territory institutions, local land-rights consequences and detailed historical boundaries need further source-specific work. A blank area is a coverage gap, not evidence that nobody lived there or resisted. Later events outside the stated time scope remain linked reading rather than silently expanding the master timeline.
