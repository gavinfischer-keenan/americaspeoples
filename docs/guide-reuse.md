# Guide writing, sources and image reuse

The guide narratives are original Americas Peoples writing. Their factual claims are supported by the sources cited with each paragraph; the additional introductory narratives use the evidence already cited in the corresponding guide. Wikipedia links are provided for further reading. No Wikipedia essays, institutional essays, copyrighted research reports or raw Consensus responses were copied into this enrichment.

## Different materials keep different terms

The new photograph selection uses CC BY 2.0, CC0 1.0 and public-domain material. The files are self-hosted; readers do not contact an external image service. Wikimedia-generated previews are identified as such. No local cropping, retouching, generated reconstruction or color alteration was applied. Modern photographs and the historical Palau quarry photograph are clearly distinguished in their captions.

## Wikipedia and Commons

[Wikimedia’s Terms of Use](https://foundation.wikimedia.org/wiki/Policy:Terms_of_Use) describe text attribution and licensing obligations. Wikipedia’s CC BY-SA material cannot simply be relabeled CC BY-NC-SA. The [CC BY-SA 4.0 terms](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/) require compatible terms for adaptations and prohibit additional restrictions. This project therefore links to verified Wikipedia articles without copying their prose into its noncommercial text.

Commons is a hosting service, not a single license. The [Commons reuse guidance](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Reusing_content_outside_Wikimedia) requires checking the individual file. For these six photographs, the source page, named creator and license declaration were checked on 17 November 2026. The item-level notices remain applicable independently of the surrounding page. See the [CC BY 2.0 terms](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/) and [CC0 dedication](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/).

## Government and institutional websites

The [National Park Service disclaimer](https://www.nps.gov/aboutus/disclaimer.htm) distinguishes employee-created government works from third-party content, trademarks and other protected material. An NPS hostname alone is not sufficient evidence that every photograph or paragraph is public domain. The same item-level approach applies to other institutions. UNESCO descriptions, museum photographs and downloaded scholarly reports are not assumed to be freely reusable merely because they can be read online.

Non-English official sources are linked in their original languages, including Spanish heritage sources from INAH, Peru’s Ministry of Culture, MINCETUR and ICANH. Their language is labeled where curated for further reading. No translation of an institutional essay is presented as newly owned atlas text.

## Traceability and maintenance

The source register combines canonical evidence-record citations, guide sources and paragraph citations, curated reading links, public data metadata and manifests, and the complete project notices. It groups exact URLs and DOI aliases while preserving the supplied addresses and the records or files that use them. Reference-only entries explicitly say that their reuse license has not been verified. Reused data and photographs retain their separate notices; the full third-party notices preserve copyright statements for incorporated software and datasets.

The build verifies each photograph against its recorded byte count and SHA-256 before publishing it. Photographs are bounded to two million bytes per stored file. Tests require sourced narrative for every enriched guide, verify image provenance, exercise safe rendering and new-guide reading fallbacks, and check that every canonical record and guide citation appears in the register. Links can change after verification; access failures do not create permission to redistribute the source.
