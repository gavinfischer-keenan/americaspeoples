Does trade-route connectivity correlate with language shift after conflict
events?
Yes, trade network connectivity accelerates post-conflict language shift and
structural assimilation.

Literature in contact linguistics, historical sociolinguistics, and spatial demography confirms that trade networks
and transport corridors strongly correlate with post-conflict language shift (De Souza Barreto, 2025; Galandarova,
2025; Mussa-Juane et al., 2019). Rather than operating as isolated forces, warfare destabilizes existing
ethnolinguistic boundaries while exchange networks provide the sustained communicative frequency necessary for
long-term language replacement (Korbozerova & Obruchnikova, 2024; Arias et al., 2022; Jin et al., 2025).

Trade Corridors as Drivers of Post-Conflict Replacement
Interregional corridors channel both military movement and subsequent commercial integration, directly
accelerating language shifts (Galandarova, 2025; Korbozerova & Obruchnikova, 2024; Jin et al., 2025). In the
Gansu-Qinghai region along the eastern Silk Road, persistent multidimensional interactions along trade conduits
drove extensive language contact and structural admixture among Sinitic, Mongolic, Turkic, and Tibetan language
communities (Jin et al., 2025). Quantitative trait-sharing frameworks reveal that major transport corridors generate
convergence zones where contact-induced phonological and morphosyntactic shifts take place (Jin et al., 2025).
Similarly, in the lower Amazon and the mouth of the Amazon River, socio-political changes after AD 1000 coincided
with heightened intergroup conflict, demographic expansions, and regional exchange networks that together
provoked ethnogenetic processes and linguistic assimilation (De Souza Barreto, 2025).
Bayesian spatial mixture models demonstrate that geographic contact areas often trace historic long-distance
exchange zones, such as the Marañón River trade networks in South America, where inter-dialectal contact and
shifting occurred (Ranacher et al., 2021).

In the Balkans, empirical Bayesian analyses highlight that imperial conquest by the Byzantine and Ottoman empires
fostered contact areas along major administrative and trade routes, facilitating structural convergence across
Slavic, Albanian, and Romance varieties (Ranacher et al., 2021).
Mechanisms of Structural Shift and Contact
Language conflict is inextricably bound to contact, emerging predictably when purposeful interactions occur
between groups within a competitive socioeconomic setting (Škifić & Pintarić, 2024; Nelde, 1987).
•
Warfare and colonization impose severe power asymmetries, shifting vocabulary, syntax, and communicative
norms through forced administrative and commercial contact (Galandarova, 2025; Korbozerova &
Obruchnikova, 2024; Arias et al., 2022).
•
Physical terrain acts as a mediator: impassable mountain barriers preserve phylogenetic diversity, whereas open
transport corridors facilitate intense demographic overlays and structural convergence (Sevilla, 2026).
•
Sustained interaction frequency within connected networks governs lexical adoption, whereas isolated
communities reach linguistic steady states along different developmental trajectories (Saddour, 2026; Mussa-
Juane et al., 2019).

Mathematical models of sociolinguistic networks indicate that regional connectivity parameters dictate the pace of
language replacement, where all-to-all connectivity introduces systemic heterogeneity across urban and rural
nodes (Mussa-Juane et al., 2019). When conquered populations are integrated into commercial networks,
prestigious lingua francas and dominant varieties diffuse rapidly through urban hubs, leading to bilingual code-
switching, grammatical convergence, and eventual language extinction (Hickey, 2020; Korbozerova &
Obruchnikova, 2024; Backus, 2005). Conversely, geographic isolation from primary exchange routes preserves
ancestral languages despite regional conflict, confirming that trade-route connectivity acts as a primary vehicle
through which post-conflict shifts materialize (Sevilla, 2026; Prochazka & Vogl, 2022).
