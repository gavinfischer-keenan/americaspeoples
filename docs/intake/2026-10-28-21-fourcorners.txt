    Four Corners Archaeology from Incipient Agriculture to 1500 CE

    Archaeological research across the Four Corners documents a multi-millennial trajectory spanning early agricultural emergence, the rise and regionalization of Chaco Canyon, and terminal depopulations leading into post-1300 CE dispersals Reed2008.

    This sequence tracks three primary dimensions: early agricultural village formation, the fluorescence and restructuring of the Chacoan regional system, and the late thirteenth-century exodus toward the Rio Grande and southern borderlands Reed2008Mills2018Kemp2017.

    The trajectory of human settlement across the northern Southwest reveals continuous adaptations to ecological pressure and social reorganization across four distinct archaeological periods Reed2008Brown2013.

    Regional Reorganization and Post-1300 Exodus

    After AD 1140, the primary administrative seat shifted northward away from Chaco Canyon without an immediate regional abandonment Brown2013Lekson1995. Post-Chaco populations congregated along the Middle San Juan, Elk Ridge, and Mesa Verde uplands before complete regional depopulation occurred in the late thirteenth century Brown2013Burrillo2017Crown2018
