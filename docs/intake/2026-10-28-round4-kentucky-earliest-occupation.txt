    Paleoindian Fluted Points Mark Earliest Kentucky Occupation

    The earliest recorded human occupation in Kentucky dates to the Paleoindian period, documented by diagnostic fluted projectile points and Pleistocene open-air habitation sites Anderson2015Potter2021.
    Paleoindian and Early Archaic Chronology

    Archaeological surveys demonstrate widespread early human occupation across the southeastern United States, including Kentucky, where fluted projectile point surveys have recorded hundreds of artifacts dating to the late Pleistocene Anderson2015. One of the classic early excavations occurred at the Parrish Village site in Hopkins County, which established an early baseline for Kentucky's terminal Pleistocene and transitional lithic sequence Anderson2015. Broad synthesis of early American sites places this Paleoindian dispersal after roughly 16,000 cal yr BP, with well-dated Southeast contexts confirming early human-megafauna coexistence by ~14,550 cal yr BP Halligan2016
    Potter2021.

    Following the initial Paleoindian horizon, Kentucky saw continuous occupation across the Early and Middle Archaic periods, spanning 10,000 to 3000 BP Jefferies2005Nance1986. At the Hart site (15LA183) in Lawrence County, spatial analyses identified Early Archaic Bifurcate horizon hearth features that reflect redundant, short-duration domestic encampments Bradbury2016. Similarly, in the lower Ohio River valley, Kirk Cluster populations engaged in macroband aggregations at locations like the Butterfield site along the Green River, demonstrating that major waterways facilitated regional mobility rather than serving as cultural barriers Jennings2023.
