    Early Archaic Lower Ohio Sites

    In the lower Ohio River valley, Early Archaic sites document mobile hunter-gatherer bands who adapted to post-glacial landscapes between 10,000 and 8,000 BP Jefferies2009Hill2018
    . These occupations are characterized by specialized lithic technologies, riverine and upland land-use strategies, and seasonal aggregation behaviors Jennings2023
    Jefferies2009.
    Landscape Use and Aggregation

    Early Archaic foraging groups structured their mobility around major drainage systems and raw material outcrops Jefferies2009Hill2018
    . Foraging bands established seasonal, short-term camps aligned with localized food resources and well-trodden hunting trails Jefferies2009Hill2018
    .
        In northern Indiana and southwestern Kentucky, intensively occupied sites typically sit in near-upland settings near chert sources, sinkholes, and higher-order streams Jennings2023
        .
        The Butterfield site in central Kentucky served as a macroband aggregation camp situated in the lowlands along the Green River Jennings2023
        .
        Morphometric and spatial models show that populations from Indiana frequently visited Butterfield, demonstrating that the Ohio River did not form a barrier to interaction Jennings2023
        .
        In contrast, contemporaneous groups in southwestern Kentucky maintained distinct settlement ranges and participated only minimally in aggregations at Butterfield Jennings2023
        .

    Geomorphic Preservation and Archaeological Visibility

    Assessing the Early Archaic record in the lower Ohio River valley is complicated by fluvial geomorphology Stafford2004. Broad tracts of Holocene alluvium form compound point bars and bank-side bars, frequently burying early sites beneath meters of sediment Stafford2004Carlson2025. Archaeological visibility is strongly mediated by soil order; older Early Holocene deposits correlate with weathered Alfisols, whereas late-Holocene landforms are dominated by Entisols or Inceptisols Stafford2004. The uneven preservation and distribution of early alluvium explains why the documented Early Archaic record appears far denser below the Falls of the Ohio compared to upstream reaches Stafford2004.

    These early hunting-and-gathering lifeways established persistent anchor points on the regional landscape Jefferies2009Carlson2025. Although foraging served as the primary subsistence strategy for millennia, the geographic knowledge and settlement nodes established by Early Archaic bands laid the spatial groundwork for later Middle and Late Archaic riverine adaptations, including intensive shellfishing and eventual plant cultivation Jefferies2009Hadden2013Hill2018
    .
