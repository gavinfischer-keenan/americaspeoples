Initial Arrival and Pleistocene Colonization

Initial human expansion into Central America is securely dated to the late Pleistocene, driven by southward dispersals moving through the Isthmus of Panama into South America between 16,000 and 13,000 calibrated years ago Sutter2020Llamas2016
Potter2021. Genomic assessments of ancient mitochondrial DNA indicate a founder population entered via Pacific coastal corridors around 16.0 ka, traversing the Americas to reach southern South America rapidly Llamas2016
. While contested claims suggest pre-Last Glacial Maximum occupations prior to 20,000 to 30,000 years ago, systematic reviews view pre-16.0 ka sites as empirically unsubstantiated, favoring the Paleoindian dispersal framework Zeitlin2008Gruhn2023Potter2021.

    The Panamanian land bridge served as a direct transit conduit for northeast Asian broad-spectrum foragers entering South America between 13,000 and 12,000 cal BC Cooke2005Sutter2020Sutter2020.
    Early foragers actively modified tropical vegetation through burning as early as 11,000 years ago, demonstrating early ecological management Cooke2005Sutter2020.
    Whole-genome transects from Belize rock-shelters identify an early Holocene lineage dating from 9,600 to 7,300 cal BP that represents an early-splitting branch distinct from present-day Mesoamericans Kennett2022

Agricultural Transitions and Early Village Formations

The transition from mobile foraging to sedentary life occurred during the Archaic and Preceramic periods, transforming local landscapes Cooke2005Zeitlin2008Roca-Rada2020
. Genome-wide analyses reveal that a major south-to-north migration originating from Chibchan-related groups in lower Central America and Colombia entered the Maya region around 5,600 cal BP, contributing over 50% of later indigenous ancestry and directly coinciding with forest clearing and maize horticulture Kennett2022
.

    Cultigens including maize and manioc were introduced into the Central American land bridge between 7,000 and 4,500 BP, gradually establishing agrarian economies Cooke2005Larosa2018Sutter2020.
    Northern Honduras developed sophisticated pottery and nucleated village life before 1600 BC, integrating into macroregional trade routes linked to Guatemala and Mexico by 1100 BC Joyce2001.
    The Preclassic site of San Isidro in El Salvador constructed more than 50 monumental earth mounds around 400 BC, establishing trade connections that exchanged jade and figurines across Mesoamerican and Isthmo-Colombian spheres Szymański2024
    .
