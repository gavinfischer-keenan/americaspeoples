    The research trajectory shows a significant shift from mid-twentieth-century claims of pre-Columbian visitation to robust chronometric refutations and modern historical ecology of post-contact industrial landscapes Anderson2016Stahl2020Stahl2020.
    Mid-Twentieth-Century Pre-Columbian Hypotheses

    Early archaeological fieldwork in the archipelago was characterized by claims that Indigenous South Americans frequented the islands prior to European arrival Anderson2016Rydén1958. In the 1950s, Thor Heyerdahl and Arne Skjølsvold investigated five coastal sites—including James Bay, Buccaneer Bay, Whale Bay, and Black Beach—recovering 1,961 unglazed earthenware sherds Anderson2016Anderson2016Anderson2016. They argued that these campsites represented multi-component palimpsests formed by repeated fishing voyages launched from pre-Columbian Ecuador and Peru Anderson2016Anderson2016Heyerdahl1956.

    However, contemporary critics immediately noted that these ceramic collections were overwhelmingly plain wares lacking diagnostic decorative styles, painted finishes, or distinctive Inca vessel forms Rydén1958Rydén1958Rydén1958. Furthermore, supposed stone scrapers closely matched European gunflints, and the indigenous-style ceramics were consistently found alongside post-Columbian Spanish artifacts and European items Gifford1953Rydén1958Rydén1958+1 more.
    Scientific Re-Evaluation and Refutation

    Subsequent systematic excavations and chronometric testing refuted the palimpsest model of pre-Columbian occupation Anderson2016Froyd2010.
        Re-excavation of all five original sites confirmed that putatively aboriginal redware shared the exact same spatial and stratigraphic distributions as historical glass, metal, and glazed ceramics Anderson2016Anderson2016Anderson2016.
        Optically stimulated luminescence (OSL) dating of the pottery and radiocarbon analyses of coastal sites confirmed that occupation began strictly after the sixteenth century Anderson2016Rydén1958.
        Radiocarbon dating of fuel wood charcoal from campsite horizons confirmed that all hearths and collecting activities date to the post-European discovery era Froyd2010Froyd2010.
        Sedimentary cores and fossil deposits from Barn Owl Cave demonstrate that vertebrate extirpations and environmental disturbances are restricted to recent centuries Anderson2016Steadman1999Steadman1999+1 more.
    Historical Archaeology of the Nineteenth and Twentieth Centuries

    Following legal annexation by Ecuador in 1832, archaeological investigations shifted toward early colonial encounters, whaler campsites, penal colonies, and nineteenth-century agro-industrial infrastructure Stahl2020Astudillo2017Astudillo2017+1 more. Research on San Cristóbal Island centers on the moist highlands of Hacienda El Progreso, a major sugar plantation founded in the 1860s that created the first permanent human community on the island Stahl2020Stahl2020Astudillo2023+1 more.

    Excavations and archaeobotanical macro-remains at El Progreso document native timber use, extensive forest clearance, and the introduction of exotic cultivars and weeds that initiated novel island ecosystems Stahl2020Astudillo2017Astudillo2018+2 more. Additionally, historical and anthropological work reveals the prominent presence of Andean Indigenous settlers who permanently inhabited the archipelago from the 1860s onward Sánchez Voelkl2022Sánchez Voelkl2022. Modern public archaeology projects now focus on integrating these historical ecological datasets into community identity, heritage preservation, and ecotourism frameworks Conrad2020Astudillo2023Astudillo2023+1 more.

    This archaeological progression establishes that human interaction with the Galapagos is entirely confined to historic encounters beginning after 1535, with the most pronounced material footprint stemming from nineteenth-century industrialization Anderson2016Stahl2020Astudillo2017+1 more.
