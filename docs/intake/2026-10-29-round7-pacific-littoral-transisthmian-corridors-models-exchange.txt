    The environmental conditions of the Pacific coastline offered resilient marine and estuarine ecosystems that sustained prolonged human settlement Lesnek2018Fladmark1979Lesnek2023.         Archaic shell mounds across the Soconusco region of Chiapas reflect intensive aquatic harvesting and sustained seasonal settlement prior to agriculture Lesnek2023Davis2020.

        Coastal corridors facilitated rapid regional interactions, linking Pacific maritime communities with emerging networks throughout Central America Llamas2016Davis2020.

    Mid-Holocene shoreline stabilization and sea-level adjustments further transformed these littoral environments, creating fertile estuarine wetlands that supported the transition to settled village life Rosenswig2020Lesnek2023.
    Trans-Isthmian Passages and Inland Exchange

    The Pacific corridor served as a launching point into the interior highland valleys of Oaxaca and Guatemala, as well as the Atlantic lowlands of Veracruz Rosenswig2020Anderson2000. River canyons slicing through the Sierra Madre provided natural breaches that channeled human groups inland from littoral base camps Anderson2000Rosenswig2020. These transverse routes allowed early foragers to settle intermontane basins and exploit varied ecological zones at diverse altitudes Ardelean2020Anderson2000.

    Topographic modeling demonstrates that populations utilized least-cost paths through the Isthmus of Tehuantepec to connect the Gulf Coast plain of southern Veracruz with the Pacific littoral of Oaxaca and Chiapas Rosenswig2020. During the Formative period, these established dispersal conduits evolved into major regional trade arteries connecting early centers like San Lorenzo with Pacific coastal societies Rosenswig2020Davis2020. Over time, routes expanded into the Chiapas and Guatemalan highlands, formalizing long-distance exchange in obsidian, jade, and ceramic styles along ancient dispersal paths Rosenswig2020Davis2020.
