Interior Foraging Models and Lithic Organization

During the Early Holocene, hunter-gatherer lifeways across the interior Southeast were structured around drainage networks, seasonal resource availability, and toolstone distributions Halligan2023Moore2013Hollenbach2023. Three foundational archaeological models frame these patterns: seasonal band movements along major river drainages, band-macroband interactions, and central-place foraging tethered directly to high-quality lithic sources Miller2023Halligan2023Hollenbach2023. In the North Carolina Sandhills, spatial modeling shows that interriverine trails and watershed divides operated as primary conveyance corridors, linking high-quality chert sources in the Piedmont to rich coastal plain resource blooms Moore2013. Over time, Early Archaic groups in these interior zones underwent a shift from wide-ranging logistical mobility to residential infilling Moore2013.

In the karst regions of northern Florida, Early Holocene Bolen point makers organized settlement around reliable surface water basins and localized toolstone outcrops Halligan2023. Petrographic analyses of Bolen bifaces demonstrate that rising post-glacial sea levels and environmental changes compressed available foraging territories, reorganizing social networks and establishing structured lithic conveyance zones across the interior Halligan2023Burke2026. Rather than following a uniform mobility template, Early Archaic groups systematically adapted their foraging decisions to regional ecotones within 10 to 30 km of central residential base camps Hollenbach2023.
