    The earliest archaeological indications of human presence in the archipelagos comprising the Republic of Kiribati point to initial settlement in the Gilbert Islands during the late first millennium BCE Di Piazza1999Thomas2009.
    Archaeological Findings on Nikunau

    Excavations at the Te Bakoa site on the raised coral limestone island of Nikunau uncovered two ancient earth ovens that yielded the oldest known radiocarbon dates for Kiribati Di Piazza1999. Radiocarbon dating of preserved pandanus keys associated with these cooking features established human exploitation and the presence of cultivated plants on the island around 2000 BP (~50 BCE) Di Piazza1999.

    Beyond the Gilbert chain, the northern Line Islands of Kiribati—including Kiritimati and Tabuaeran—fit the regional model of Pacific mystery islands, which contain prehistoric archaeological traces despite being uninhabited at European contact Di Piazza2001. Excavations demonstrate that Tabuaeran supported more permanent agricultural settlement centered on reliable freshwater marshes, whereas Kiritimati functioned as an intermittent staging post and resource refuge for marine fauna Di Piazza2001.

    Across the wider Pacific, these settlement horizons fit into broader human expansions enabled by specialized voyaging technology and interisland mobility networks Thomas2009Petersen2019. Hydroclimate reconstructions derived from Kiribati and surrounding equatorial islands demonstrate that prolonged drought shifts and changing wind belts frequently coincided with these major exploratory voyages across East Polynesia and Micronesia Sear2020Sear2020.
