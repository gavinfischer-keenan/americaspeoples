    Figure 1 Scientific consensus regarding pre-5000 cal BP archaeological deposits in Jamaica

    Archaeological excavations confirm that Jamaica possesses no pre-5000 cal BP deposits, with initial human settlement dating after AD 600 Keegan2016Callaghan2008Rampersad2009.
    Absence of Archaic Occupations

    Extensive surveys and excavations across the island have documented no evidence of preceramic or Archaic Age occupations Keegan2016Callaghan2008Rampersad2009. While neighbouring islands in the Greater Antilles such as Cuba, Hispaniola, and Puerto Rico contain Archaic sites reaching back to the fourth or fifth millennium BC, systematic attempts to locate equivalent early sites on Jamaica have consistently found none Keegan2016Fitzpatrick2015Callaghan2008. No archaeological evidence of human habitation on Jamaica exists before approximately 500 BC, and securely documented settlements begin even later Keegan2016Callaghan2008Rampersad2009.

    The earliest confirmed human occupations across Jamaica date to shortly after AD 600 during the Ostionoid period Callaghan2008Rampersad2009. This initial occupation is associated with the Ostionan Ostionoid culture, informally termed "Redware," which predated later Meillacan populations by roughly two centuries Rampersad2009. Radiocarbon dates place this early cultural sequence between approximately AD 650 and AD 850, leaving Jamaica with one of the most delayed colonization records in the Caribbean archipelago Callaghan2008Rampersad2009.
    Environmental and Navigational Barriers

    Computer simulations and paleogeographic models indicate that rough sea conditions and adverse oceanic currents around Jamaica discouraged early seafaring groups Callaghan2008. Maritime crossings directly into Jamaica remained hazardous until socio-political shifts in the northern Caribbean favoured the construction of large ocean-going canoes Keegan2016Callaghan2008. Furthermore, relative sea-level rise over the middle Holocene may have inundated early coastal shorelines, although inland terrestrial surveys have likewise failed to identify preceramic lithic tool horizons Hackenberger2021
