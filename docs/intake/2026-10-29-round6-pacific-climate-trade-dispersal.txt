    Trade Networks and Secondary Dispersals

    Human trade and social interaction spheres dictated the subsequent mobility and integration of populations once islands were colonized Nägele2025
    . Rather than representing isolated colonization events, the archaeological record reveals that long-distance trade links periodically contracted and expanded Nägele2025
    . Along coastal Papua New Guinea, an interruption in ceramic exchange networks known as the "Ceramic Hiccup" between ~1200 and 500 BP coincided with medieval climate variability and increased El Niño occurrences, prompting settlements to abandon coastal fringes, fortify communities, and retreat inland Nägele2025

    Following this disruption, the resurgence of specialized maritime trade systems—such as the Kula ring and hiri voyages around 500 BP—spurred renewed population mobility and genetic exchange across coastal and island groups Nägele2025
    . Similar processes governed early migrations; during the Lapita expansion, voyagers used selective range expansions and inter-island exchange networks to transport domesticates, obsidian, and ceramics across thousands of kilometers Cochrane2018Nägele2025
