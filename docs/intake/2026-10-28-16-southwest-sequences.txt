    California Horizons and Coastal Schemes

    Archaeologists initially organized Central California into a classic tripartite framework termed the Early, Middle, and Late Horizons based on excavations in the lower Sacramento Valley and San Francisco Bay Beardsley1948Heizer1939Rosenthal1996. Stratigraphic layers at stratified mound sites revealed distinct burial customs and grave goods separating these successive stages Belous1953Heizer1939Fredrickson1974. Radiocarbon assessments later revised this unilineal scheme, demonstrating that the Windmiller culture of the Early Horizon dates between 2400 and 545 BC rather than preceding 4,450 BP Jackson1981, with transitions occurring asynchronously across subregions Fredrickson1974. Regional phase definitions in Solano County further refined these sequences into precise local temporal subdivisions Rosenthal1996.

    Southern California coastal sequences established four primary horizons, moving from early lithic milling assemblages to late protohistoric cultures Wallace1955Wallace1962. Radiocarbon records from San Diego County indicate that early and middle Archaic occupations were substantially larger than previously recognized, with artifact typologies reflecting functional tasks rather than uniform temporal phases Scharlotta2015. Inland, southern desert successions track four cultural horizons beginning with terminal Pleistocene hunting complexes at ancient Lake Mohave Wallace1962. These were followed by Pinto Basin foraging assemblages, the less-defined Amargosa period, and late phases marked by arrow points, brownware ceramics, and intensive seed grinding Wallace1962.

    Great Basin and Mojave Climatic Sequences

    Human occupation sequences in the western Great Basin and northern Mojave Desert reveal sharp oscillations tied to Holocene climate shifts Goebel2021Allen2011Gardner2015.
        Centennial paleoclimate tracking at Bonneville Estates Rockshelter, Nevada, across 247 radiocarbon assays shows high occupational intensity during cool, mesic intervals and complete abandonment during severe mid-Holocene droughts Goebel2021.
        Continuous 12,000-year records at Gold Butte, Nevada, preserve a trans-Holocene transition from Archaic foragers into Virgin Branch Puebloan farmers occupying river bottomlands, terminating with Southern Paiute and Hualapai occupations Gardner2015.
        Environmental perturbations during the Younger Dryas and Medieval Climatic Anomaly forced punctuated demographic reorganizations across arid interior basins and coastal estuaries alike Acebo2015Allen2011.

    These empirical chronologies confirm that cultural boundaries in Far Western and Southwestern archaeology reflect adaptive ecological reorganizations rather than static, unilineal stages Arnold2004Scharlotta2015Goebel2021+1 more.
