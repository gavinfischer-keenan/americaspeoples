    Tupi Expansion and the Colonial Transition

    Beginning around 2,400 to 2,000 cal BP, Tupi-speaking peoples expanded outward from Amazonia, rapidly colonizing the eastern Atlantic seaboard and riverine systems Silva2023
    De Souza2020
    . Archaeological inventories with more than 660 georeferenced dates document this dispersals into two principal branches: the Guaraní moving south along the Paraná drainage to the Río de la Plata estuary, and the Tupinambá spreading along the Atlantic coastline Silva2023
    Bonomo2025De Souza2020
    . Ancient DNA confirms this demic transition: at the Sambaqui do Limão in Espírito Santo, individuals dating to 3,200 BP showed Jê genetic affinities, whereas an individual dating to 500 BP displayed close genetic similarity to Tupi speakers Silva2023
    .

    By the time of Portuguese arrival in 1500, when Brazil's native population was in the millions, coastal groups including the Tupiniquim, Temiminõ, and Tupinambá (Tamoio) controlled the eastern seaboard Noelli2023
