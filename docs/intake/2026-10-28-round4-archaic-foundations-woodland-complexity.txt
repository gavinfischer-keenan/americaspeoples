    Archaic Foundations of Late Woodland Complexity

    Archaic developments shaped Late Woodland social complexity across three interrelated facets: settlement permanence, subsistence intensification, and incipient plant domestication Wright2021Hollenbach2022Bender1985.
    Sedentism and Landscape Packing

    Archaic populations established persistent settlement hubs, transitioning from seasonal foraging rounds to fixed landscape anchors that concentrated subsequent Late Woodland communities Anderson2025Benison1997

    Subsistence Intensification and Household Inequality

    Archaic broad-spectrum foraging established economic feedbacks that directly fueled institutionalized social differentiation in later Woodland groups White2013Bender1985. Modeling of prehistoric economies indicates that mass-harvesting low-quality Archaic foods lowered the productive age of children, relaxing reproductive constraints and enabling larger, wealthier households White2013. Structural data from more than 800 residential dwellings reveal right-tailed wealth distributions originating in the Late Archaic, which provided the institutional foundation for emergent inequality, lineage debt, and hereditary status White2013Bender1985.

    Indigenous Horticulture and Landscape Management

    Late Archaic foraging bands independently domesticated a suite of small-seeded annual crops 5,000 to 3,500 years ago, creating anthropogenic landscapes that facilitated later agricultural regimes Benison1997
    Hollenbach2022. Controlled low-energy burning used by Archaic and Early Woodland peoples cleared forest canopies and replenished soils, serving as a critical pre-adaptation for intensive maize farming Benison1997
    . The transition of storage pits from communal contexts to individual domestic structures mirrored the rise of private property and localized control of arable fields Hollenbach2022Quinn2022.
