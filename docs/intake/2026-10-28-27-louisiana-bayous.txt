    Chronology of Occupations on Bayou Levees

    Settlements directly situated along coastal Louisiana bayous were constrained by the physical evolution of the Holocene Mississippi River Delta Chamberlain2020Springer1980
    . Modern marine delta lobes developed between 8500 and 6500 BP, but geomorphic data indicate that communities could only construct stable settlements after several centuries of natural overbank deposition and crevasse splay aggradation elevated the natural levees above seasonal flood levels Chamberlain2020
    . Consequently, the earliest secure occupations situated directly on coastal bayous date to the Middle and Late Woodland stages rather than the Paleoindian era Springer1980Kerr2023.

    At the Bruly St. Martin site (Iv-6), positioned on a well-drained natural levee of an abandoned bayou that broke off Bayou Lafourche, four radiocarbon dates securely place the village occupation between AD 600 and 750 Springer1980. The stratified midden deposits and platform mound contain ceramics diagnostic of the Troyville and Coles Creek cultures Springer1980. Farther upstream along the 18-mile corridor of Bayou Manchac, systematic surveys and excavations at the Hillman site (16EBR60) revealed a pre-contact village established during the Marksville culture (AD 1–400) that persisted through the subsequent Troyville and Coles Creek periods Kerr2023
