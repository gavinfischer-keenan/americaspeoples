    Early anthropogenic impacts appear in microscopic charcoal particle stratigraphies, showing steep increases in fire activity in leeward Kauaʻi by cal AD 1050–1280 Burney2003. Polynesians simultaneously transformed native environments through the introduction of domesticates, including candlenut, breadfruit, mountain apple, pigs, and dogs Kirch2023. Extensive stonework features, such as the Alekoko Fishpond on Kauaʻi, were established relatively early in the sequence, dating to AD 1305–1420 Burney2003.
    Material Evidence of Conflict and Warfare

    Unlike other Pacific societies that constructed prominent earthwork hillforts, ancient Hawaiian warfare left a distinct, state-level footprint centered around ritualized political conquest Kolb2002. Pre-contact Hawaiian polities competed intensely for fertile agricultural land and political hegemony, but institutional structures shaped conflict differently than in regions characterized by long-term defensive refuges Kolb2002:
