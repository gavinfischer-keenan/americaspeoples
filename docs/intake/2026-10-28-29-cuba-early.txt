    Figure 1 Scientific consensus on pre-5000 cal BP human occupation in Cuba

    Excavations across the Greater Antilles confirm that preceramic hunter-gatherer populations colonized Cuba well before 5000 cal BP (~3000 BC), reaching the island during the middle Holocene Dickau2015Keegan2016.
    Chronological Evidence for Early Settlement

    Archaeological assessments identify the initial colonization of Cuba and the Greater Antilles occurring around 5000 BC (~7000 cal BP) Dickau2015Keegan2016Keegan2017. In western Cuba, archaic assemblages featuring flaked-stone tools and ground-stone blades date to ca. 5000 BC, reflecting technological parallels with contemporary mainland populations in Central America Dickau2015Keegan2016Keegan2017. These tools show strong similarities to the early microlithic stone technologies documented in Belize and the Yucatan Peninsula, pointing toward early maritime crossings eastward across the Yucatan Channel Keegan2016. This antiquity places the earliest Antillean settlements squarely within the regional preceramic period, coeval with early insular occupations on Trinidad and Curaçao Keegan2016Kraan2024.

    The radiocarbon record of Cuba has expanded through targeted AMS programs, confirming that indigenous groups were actively exploring and utilizing coastal ecosystems prior to 5000 cal BP Cooper2007Cooper2012
    . On Cayo Caimán Mata del Coco in the Jardines del Rey archipelago of northern Cuba, directly dated marine shell artifacts from stratified surface deposits yielded calibrated dates between 2754 and 2478 cal BC (~4700–4400 cal BP) Cooper2012
    . When integrated into the broader database of Cuban pre-Columbian sites, these findings verify long-term offshore marine resource exploitation spanning more than four millennia Cooper2007Cooper2012
    . Absolute chronologies continue to demonstrate that first colonization occurred ca. 5000 BP or earlier, establishing sustained maritime networks across adjacent island channels Cooper2007Cooper2012
