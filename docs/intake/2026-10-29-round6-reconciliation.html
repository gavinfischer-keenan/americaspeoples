All19 complete supplied syntheses are preserved. This release adds32 scoped records and7 context updates. Existing related records supply corroborating context. The uploads are secondary research leads; automated headlines, unrelated papers and uncertain inferences are not promoted to facts.

Published additions

  • Te Bakoa, Nikunau — dated earth ovens — Do not automatically convert an unspecified radiocarbon BP age to 50 BCE. This was the oldest known Kiribati evidence reported in 1999, not a timeless archipelago-wide first-arrival claim.
  • Utrōk — oldest cultural date — Approximate atoll reference, not an individual hearth coordinate. Islet development around 2750 calBP is not human occupation.
  • Maloelap — oldest cultural date — Approximate atoll reference. Geomorphic ages and cultural dates must remain distinct.
  • Majuro — settlement following emergence — A source-rounded onset, not a one-year occupation. This local sequence does not prove synchronous settlement of all Marshall atolls.
  • Mwoakilloa — early occupation horizon — An onset window, not occupation ending at 1560 calBP. Whether use was continuous requires further investigation. This is a Caroline atoll, not in the Marshalls.
  • Nukuleka — coral-file founder horizon — Dates a proposed initial-landfall horizon. It is not the late 29th centuryBCE. Exact lab intervals and model probabilities are not interchangeable.
  • Haʻapai — modeled Lapita beginning — Island-group model from sampled sites; keep distinct from exact site dates or full occupation. A modeled 70–90 year lag does not specify a canoe itinerary.
  • Vavaʻu — modeled Lapita beginning — Few accepted dates constrain this group. A strict Haʻapai-then-Vavaʻu sequence remains a hypothesis, not a resolved ordering.
  • Tonga — proposed early northward dispersal — Schematic connection between island groups; no exact route, travel time or compulsory founding genealogy is inferred.
  • Nukuleka — cultivation-pit infilling — This interval dates infilling, not first planting or continuous cultivation. Proposed moisture/nutrient roles of ceramic sherds are interpretations.
  • Ureia, Aitutaki — revised occupation chronology — A site chronology, not first arrival on the entire island or an exact settlement-duration guarantee.
  • Tangatatau — early coral tool — A dated tool context, not continuous settlement. The checked 2020 paper synthesizes the original dating study.
  • Tangatatau — later coral tool — Separate from the earlier tool; no temporal support is invented between the two dated intervals.
  • Bapot-1, Saipan — revised arrival estimate — Site-specific revision; do not apply it automatically to all Mariana settlements or erase older competing models. Approximate bay-area placement.
  • Alamagan — Latte-period settlement — The broad display envelope intentionally avoids invented decade bounds. Fourteen structures are not fourteen people or a contemporaneous census. No Wake settlement is inferred.
  • Nan Madol — construction and relative sea level — A monument-phase context, not initial island settlement. Lower sea level informs the interpretation of later inundation.
  • Leluh — construction and relative sea level — A monument context rather than first occupation. Original canal/islet interpretation is reassessed by this model.
  • Pohnpei and Kosrae — submerged-record bias — A preservation-bias mechanism, not discovery of an unlocated submerged settlement or proof of earlier arrival.
  • Micronesia — five inferred ancestry streams — Model-specific ancestry sources are not five exact routes, five languages or five synchronous colonizations.
  • Central Micronesia — inferred male-mediated ancestry input — Human genetic inference differs from rat, pig and chicken pathways. No exact route or date of first island settlement is claimed.
  • Anakena — late-colonization evidence — Rounded date of an onset interpretation, not one-year occupation or proof that earlier visits were impossible.
  • Rapa Nui — modeled American ancestry admixture — The sampled individuals date 1670–1950 CE. Admixture estimates do not determine a voyage direction, number of contacts or exact American landfall.
  • Rapa Nui — severe seventeenth-century bottleneck rejected — Effective population-size inference is not a census and does not establish absence of environmental or social disruption.
  • Rapa Nui — prolonged rainfall deficit — Proxy reconstruction with model uncertainty. Cultural reorganization is a proposed response, not proof of warfare or demographic collapse.
  • Southern Cook Islands — early drought reconstruction — Climate context is distinct from an excavated settlement or demonstrated cause of migration.
  • Southern Cook Islands — later drought reconstruction — Co-occurrence does not establish drought as the sole cause of changed land use.
  • Coastal Papua New Guinea — ancestry and interaction — Regional study of people, not universal evidence that ceramic trade carried particular genes or caused every coastal relocation.
  • Pacific paper mulberry — plant dispersal networks — Plant genetic hubs do not establish human ancestry, routes or dates of first settlement.
  • Cook Islands — sourced basalt exchange — Material provenance is not proof of direct nonstop voyages, traded volume, direction or population migration.
  • Galápagos — pre-Columbian claims reassessed — This does not rule out every unrecorded visit; no prehistoric settlement dot is invented from an absence assessment.
  • Ebon — sustained mollusc harvesting — Do not infer no impacts on every species, no terrestrial change or a particular population size.
  • Mololo, Raja Ampat — early Pacific occupation — This is Near Oceania/Island Southeast Asian context, not evidence of Pleistocene Polynesian or Micronesian settlement. Approximate island reference pending source-coordinate extraction.
