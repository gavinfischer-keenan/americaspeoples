    Geographic Locations and Settlement Networks

    Aztec urbanism was concentrated across the highland Basin of Mexico and adjoining valleys such as Morelos and Oaxaca, forming an extensive network of city-states governed through hegemonic, indirect imperial control Smith2021
    Carballo2022
    . Eight substantial urban centers ringed Lake Texcoco alongside Tenochtitlan, with major regional hubs flourishing at Cholula, Tlaxcala, and Yautepec Smith2021
    Morse1984. These urban centers integrated monumental civic plazas and temple pyramids with domestic craft workshops and local marketplace exchanges Smith2021
    Smith2008Smith2025.

    In contrast, the Inca Empire constructed a linear urban network spanning more than 1,500 kilometers north and south along the Andean cordillera through modern Peru, Ecuador, Bolivia, Chile, and Argentina Covey2025
    Marsh2017. Full-coverage regional pedestrian surveys around the capital at Cuzco identified approximately 1,500 Inca-era settlements, including major royal estates like Machu Picchu and regional centers such as Ollantaytambo, Chokepukio, and Ak'awillay Covey2025
    . Along the highland camino real (Inca road system), imperial administrative nodes were placed at strategic waypoints including Quito, Cajamarca, Jauja, Vilcas, Huánuco, and Bonbón to manage state agricultural and pastoral production Rostain2024Morse1984Lobo2019
