    The research arc reflects an empirical shift from long chronologies based on legacy radiocarbon dates to refined, shorter estimates established by chronometric hygiene and genomics Lipo2015Ioannidis2021.
    Pre-2006 Early Settlement Baseline

    Early archaeological paradigms supported an extended settlement chronology placing initial Polynesian landfall on Rapa Nui between AD 400 and 800 Mulrooney2021Hunt2008Thorsby2016
    . These estimates stemmed from legacy excavations, including a radiocarbon determination of AD 770 ± 230 from a rectangular house structure on Rano Kau Mulrooney2021, alongside early palynological evidence from lake cores and linguistic models Hunt2008Rull2019
    . Early investigations at Anakena also yielded dates accepted as evidence for first-millennium occupation Martinsson-Wallin2001Berenguer2024
    . However, these determinations frequently suffered from substantial in-built age, uncritical sample selection, and high measurement errors DiNapoli2020Wilmshurst2010
    .
    The 2006–2015 Chronometric Hygiene Turning Point

    Systematic excavations at Anakena Dune demonstrated that the earliest stratigraphic cultural layers date to approximately AD 1200 Hunt2006Lipo2015Hunt2008. Chronometric hygiene protocols applied to over 300 island-wide radiocarbon dates eliminated unreliable samples, demonstrating no secure evidence of human activity prior to the 13th century Lipo2015Mulrooney2013Hunt2008. A meta-analysis of 1,434 regional dates confirmed that East Polynesian expansion occurred rapidly in two distinct phases, with Rapa Nui settled between AD 1200 and 1253 Wilmshurst2010
    Thorsby2016
    . Sediment cores from Rano Raraku corroborated this timeline, showing that severe drought ended and human-driven deforestation began shortly before AD 1180–1290 Mann2008.
