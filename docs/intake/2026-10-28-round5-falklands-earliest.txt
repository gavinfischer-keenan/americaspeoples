what is the earliest settlement on the falkland islands
Port Louis, established in 1764, is the earliest confirmed settlement.
The earliest confirmed historical settlement on the Falkland Islands is the French colony of Port Saint-Louis (later
Port Louis), established in 1764 by Louis-Antoine de Bougainville on East Falkland (A. et al., 2025; Peoples, 2019;
Pernety, 2013). While earlier maritime sightings date to the late 16th and 17th centuries, no permanent colonization
occurred prior to the 18th century, though recent palaeoecological studies debate whether ephemeral prehistoric
indigenous landings preceded European contact (Green, 2019; Hamley et al., 2021; Zangrando & Borrero, 2022).
Earliest Historical Settlements
Historical and archaeological evidence confirms that permanent colonization began in the mid-18th century as rival
European powers sought strategic outposts near Cape Horn (Philpott, 1996; Pernety, 2013).

FIGURE 1 Chronology and characteristics of the earliest colonial settlements in the Falkland Islands
Following the French and Indian War, Louis-Antoine de Bougainville founded Port Saint-Louis in 1764 on East
Falkland to establish a base for displaced Acadians (Pernety, 2013). In 1765, British Commodore John Byron
established an outpost at Port Egmont on Saunders Island, erecting dwelling houses, storehouses, and fortifications
to secure passage around Cape Horn (Philpott, 1996; Clayton, 1776). Spain subsequently purchased the French
settlement in 1766–1767, renaming it Puerto de la Soledad and maintaining an administrative presence until the
Latin American wars of independence triggered its withdrawal around 1806–1811 (Peoples, 2019; Rubin, 2021;
Clayton, 1776).

Evidence for Prehistoric Activity
While structured town sites remain strictly colonial, palaeoecological and archaeological research investigates
whether Indigenous peoples reached the archipelago prior to European landings (Zangrando & Borrero, 2022):
Archaeologists emphasize that these prehistoric findings represent ephemeral visits rather than permanent,
sedentary settlements (Zangrando & Borrero, 2022; Hamley et al., 2021). Historical contextualizations argue that
many isolated surface artifacts, sea mammal bone piles, and charcoal layers are more economically explained by
18th-to-20th-century sealing voyages, transient marine crews, or Anglican missionary initiatives rather than
established prehistoric villages (Zangrando & Borrero, 2022). Consequently, permanent, sedentary habitation in the
Falkland Islands began with the 1764 establishment of Port Saint-Louis (Peoples, 2019; Hamley et al., 2021; A. et al.,
2025).
