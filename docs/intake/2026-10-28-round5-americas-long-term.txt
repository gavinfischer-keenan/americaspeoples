Multidisciplinary evidence confirms that, despite severe bottlenecks from European contact, contemporary
Indigenous Americans derive their primary ancestry and demographic foundations directly from late Pleistocene
founding populations (Waters, 2019; Willerslev & Meltzer, 2021; Llamas et al., 2016).

Chronological and Archaeological Dispersals
Bayesian modeling of radiocarbon records establishes that human occupation expanded significantly across North
America during the abrupt warming of Greenland Interstadial 1, roughly 14.7 to 12.9 thousand years ago (Becerra-
Valdivia & Higham, 2020). While earlier contested evidence exists—such as in situ footprints at White Sands dated
between 23 and 21 ka (Bennett et al., 2021), stratified lithics at Chiquihuite Cave (Ardelean et al., 2020), and fecal
biomarkers at Lake E5 (Vachula et al., 2019)—consensus strongly anchors the first widespread colonizing complex
to post-16 ka Paleoindian dispersals (Potter et al., 2018; Potter et al., 2021; Fiedel, 2022). Between 15.5 and 14 ka,
geographically dispersed foraging groups using biface and blade tools settled multiple regions, reaching as far
south as Monte Verde, Chile by ~14.6 ka (Waters, 2019; Llamas et al., 2016; Nichols, 2024). By ~13 ka, widespread
fluted point complexes emerged nearly synchronously across both continents, marked by Clovis traditions in North
America and Fishtail points in South America (Waters, 2019; Potter et al., 2018; Fiedel, 2022). This continent-wide
human expansion coincided directly with the final disappearance of 18 large terrestrial mammal genera (Becerra-
Valdivia & Higham, 2020; Fiedel, 2022).

Paleogenomic Branching and Lineage Trajectories
Multi-Proxy Evidence From Prehistory to Present
Following the late Pleistocene dispersal, genetic and material datasets show that regional differentiation rapidly
took hold across both continents (Willerslev & Meltzer, 2021; Sutter, 2020; Reich et al., 2012). In South America,
broad-spectrum foraging gave way to plant domestication and landscape modification by ~8000 cal BC, followed
by agricultural expansions around 5000 BC that reshaped highland demography while lowland groups maintained
long-term descent from early colonists (Sutter, 2020). Modern linguistics preserves diagnostic structural founder
traits that mirror these deglaciation corridors across more than 200 independent indigenous language families
(Nichols, 2024).
