    Archaic Hunter-Gatherer Horizons

    Recent stratigraphic and accelerator mass spectrometry (AMS) dating programs have dramatically extended the initial occupation antiquity of the southern Caribbean Kraan2024Fitzpatrick2015. Excavations at the C-1426 rockshelter at Saliña Sint Marie on Curaçao established the earliest known archaeological occupation on the island, dating to 5735–5600 cal BP (~3785–3650 BC) Kraan2024Giovas2026. Across the ABC archipelago, faunal records from 26 sites confirm that hunter-gatherer groups were exploiting local ecosystems from ca. 4000 BC onward Conlan2026. On Bonaire, the onset of Archaic settlement is radiocarbon dated to approximately 1500 BC Seferidou2024
    . These early populations relied on marine and coastal resources, including the specialized capture of green (Chelonia mydas) and hawksbill (Eretmochelys imbricata) sea turtles from as early as 2310 BC Conlan2026.

    Ceramic Age Agricultural Villages

    Around AD 400, a new migration originating from northwestern Venezuela established the Ceramic Age across the islands, introducing permanent villages and agriculture De Jong2008Seferidou2024
    . This era, traditionally linked to the Arawak-speaking Caquetío, lasted until Spanish contact in AD 1499 De Jong2008Seferidou2024
    . Ceramic styles and exotic raw materials testify to sustained, intensive interaction spheres connecting Aruba, Curaçao, and Bonaire with the Dabajuroid traditions of the Venezuelan mainland and oceanic archipelagos such as Las Aves and Los Roques Antczak2016Seferidou2024
    .

    Colonial Maritime Landscapes

    Following the arrival of Europeans in 1499, Spanish slave raids permanently interrupted traditional indigenous mobility networks connecting the mainland and the islands Antczak2016Seferidou2024
    . Archaeological investigations on Bonaire trace the development of colonial extraction sites, notably eighteenth-century fortifications and military installations Stelten2022
