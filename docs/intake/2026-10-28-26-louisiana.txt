    Earliest Paleoindian and Archaic Occupations

    Pleistocene landforms across Louisiana preserve evidence of mobile hunter-gatherers dating from roughly 11,500 BC (~13,500 cal BP) Mehta2019
    Trubitt2019. The oldest occupations are generally located inland away from the modern coast on stable Pleistocene terraces, whereas coastal features like the salt domes of Avery Island and Cote Blanche preserve the earliest human traces in the coastal zone Mehta2019
    . Diagnostic fluted projectile point surveys throughout the Southeast reflect intense regional late Pleistocene exploration along Gulf Coastal drainages contemporary with nearby pre-Clovis and early Paleoindian fauna-processing sites Anderson2015Halligan2016
    . In northwestern Louisiana, stratified campsites such as the Conly site illustrate the long transition from Paleoindian hunters to early Holocene Archaic foragers Trubitt2019Kassabaum2012.

    Middle Archaic communities in northern and coastal Louisiana engineered the earliest known earthworks in North America beginning around 3500 BC Saunders1997Mehta2019. At Watson Brake in northeastern Louisiana, an 11-mound oval complex was initiated around 3500 BC on a Pleistocene stream terrace and built across multiple stages until roughly 3000 BC Saunders2005Kassabaum2012. Basal middens reveal all-season foraging supported by fish, small mammals, and native seeds that later became the first eastern North American domesticates Saunders1997Saunders2005. Contemporaneous Middle Archaic earthen monuments include the Banana Bayou mound on Avery Island and contested early burning levels at the Monte Sano mounds dating toward 4270 BC Mehta2019
