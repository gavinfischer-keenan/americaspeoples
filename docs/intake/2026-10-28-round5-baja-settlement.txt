Archaeological and palaeogenomic records confirm human presence across Baja California extending continuously
from terminal Pleistocene coastal foragers through Middle and Late Holocene hunter-gatherer populations (Davis
et al., 2025; Erlandson et al., 2008; Davis et al., 2025).

Pleistocene and Early Holocene Horizons
On Cedros Island off the Pacific coast of Baja California, excavations demonstrate intensive human adaptation
spanning the Pleistocene-Holocene transition (Davis et al., 2025). Stratified deposits at the El Peregrino and Colina
Castor sites, constrained by Bayesian modelling of over 40 radiocarbon determinations, date the initial onset of
occupation to the Younger Dryas chronozone between 12,905 and 12,050 cal BP (Davis et al., 2025). Early foraging
groups carried marine shells and lithic artifacts as far as 15 km inland from submerged palaeocoastlines to base
camps positioned beside active freshwater springs (Davis et al., 2025). Nearby at Cerro Pedregoso, stratified
alluvial fan deposits verify repeated maritime occupations between ~12,000 and 9600 cal BP, with underlying
paleosols suggesting the possibility of even older occupations (Davis et al., 2025). In the Sea of Cortez, stratified
rockshelter deposits at Covacha Babisuri on Espíritu Santo Island confirm human activity by ~12,000 cal BP (Davis
et al., 2025), complemented by submerged paleolandscape surveys recovering inundated lithic materials (Gusick &
Davis, 2010).
Broader peninsular contexts establish that early groups entered from the north via coastal and terrestrial routes
(Fujita & Ainis, 2018; Baglay, 2021). Fluted point occurrences link early inhabitants to late Pleistocene Clovis
traditions (Fujita & Ainis, 2018; Baglay, 2021), while ancient lake margins—such as Laguna Seca Chapala—exhibit
Western Pluvial Lakes assemblages consisting of leaf-shaped projectile points, eccentric crescents, and unifacial
scrapers (Fujita & Ainis, 2018).

Palaeogenomics and Biological Continuity
Genome-wide analyses of ancient individuals demonstrate that Baja California populations maintained deep
genetic continuity rooted in early lineages (Nakatsuka et al., 2023). Ancient individuals from Baja California share
more derived alleles with Early Holocene individuals from Central California (dating to ~5200 BP) than with later
populations, supporting the existence of an early, widespread coastal genetic and linguistic substrate (Nakatsuka et
al., 2023).

Craniometric evaluations similarly identify distinct morphological traits in historic Baja California groups that show
affinities with early Holocene Palaeoamerican skeletons rather than neighboring mainland groups (González-José
et al., 2003). Prolonged environmental hyper-aridity during the Middle Holocene likely restricted gene flow from
northern continental groups, preserving ancestral lineage structures until late prehistoric population increases
began ~1500 to 1000 years ago (Ritter, 1998; González-José et al., 2003).
