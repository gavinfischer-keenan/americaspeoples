    Highland Incursions and Wetland Intensification

    In the southern and central regions of Brazil, the first millennium AD witnessed major demographic shifts as populations expanded out of the central savannas and occupied new ecological niches Iriarte2016
    . Jê-speaking groups made their initial incursions from central Brazilian savannas into the southern highlands, adapting to more humid Late Holocene climates by intensively managing Araucaria pine forests Iriarte2016
    . By AD 1000, these Proto-Jê populations were constructing elaborate ceremonial architecture and monumental earthworks, reflecting increasingly territorial sociopolitical structures Iriarte2016
    .

    Simultaneously, wetland zones in central-western and southeastern Brazil experienced pronounced population increases and heightened landscape modification Iriarte2016
    . Around the turn of the second millennium BP, archaeological sites expanded rapidly across the Pantanal, the lower Paraná River, and adjacent riverine floodplains Iriarte2016
    . These communities intensified local food production and established regional networks that linked previously isolated aquatic and terrestrial foraging groups Iriarte2016
    .
    Amazonian Complexity and Riverine Expansions

    In northern and central Brazil, the Amazon Basin was home to complex tropical forest societies that developed sophisticated material cultures and high population densities Iriarte2016
    Gomes2018McEwan2001. These Amazonian populations sustained intricate artistic traditions that included elaborately decorated ceramics, worked stone, carved wood, and featherwork bearing rich symbolic imagery McEwan2001. Rather than remaining isolated in the rainforest, Amazonian societies formed large, structured communities that influenced regions far beyond the basin's margins Iriarte2016
