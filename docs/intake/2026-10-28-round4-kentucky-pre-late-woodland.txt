    Paleoindian, Archaic, and Early/Middle Woodland Populations Predate Late Woodland Kentucky

    Before the Late Woodland period, Kentucky was inhabited by Paleoindian, Archaic, and Early to Middle Woodland populations spanning over ten millennia of cultural and technological development Spaulding1955Jefferies2005Hill2018
    .
    Chronological Predecessors Across Kentucky

    The cultural sequence preceding the Late Woodland consists of four primary archaeological horizons, moving from nomadic mobile bands to complex earthwork-building horticultural societies Spaulding1955Hill2018

    Paleoindian and Archaic Traditions

    The earliest human presence is documented by Paleoindian surface finds of fluted points and related lithic forms from non-pottery sites in Kentucky Spaulding1955Fairbanks1955. During the Early Holocene, Early Archaic Kirk Cluster hunter-gatherers established seasonal camps, often settling in near-upland settings close to lithic sources and higher-order streams Jennings2023. At sites like Butterfield on the Green River, populations aggregated in lowland valley settings, interacting across the Ohio River Jennings2023.

    As the Middle and Late Archaic periods developed between 8,000 and 3,000 BP, settlement networks shifted, reflected in dense shell-midden accumulations like Indian Knoll, Ward, and Chiggerville Spaulding1955Fairbanks1955Jefferies2005+1 more. Surveys in the Green River drainage near Cypress Creek revealed 40 previously unrecorded Archaic sites across four distinct environmental zones, demonstrating that populations altered their territorial mobility and risk-minimization strategies over millennia Jefferies2005.

    Early and Middle Woodland Societies

    Between 1000 BC and AD 500, populations transitioned from broad-spectrum foraging to early farming while establishing ceremonial earthwork complexes Spaulding1955Hill2018
    Mueller2018. Radiocarbon dates at the West Runway site (15BE391) place Early Woodland occupations between 770 BC and 390 BC, where pit features contained coarse Fayette Thick pottery and Kramer projectile points Bergman1998
    . These early residential settlements predate monumental Adena mortuary structures, implying that ceremonial mounds functioned to socially integrate dispersed household units across the Kentucky landscape Bergman1998
    .

    Paleobotanical deposits from rockshelters like Cold Oak and Cliff Palace Pond indicate that Late Archaic and Woodland groups used fire to clear forest canopies, cultivating native species of the Eastern Agricultural Complex such as squash, sumpweed, chenopod, and maygrass Delcourt1998Gremillion1993Ray2025. Middle Woodland populations associated with Adena and Hopewell networks intensified this agriculture; sub-mound contexts at the Walker-Noe site (15Gd56) preserved early domesticated erect knotweed dating to around 1 AD Spaulding1955Mueller2018. This sustained dietary transition toward softer, processed horticultural food caused measurable long-term dental reduction across Kentucky populations Hill2018

    Dental Reduction and Diet in the Prehistoric Ohio River Valley
    2018·M. K. Hill ·Dental Anthropology Journal

    Bioarchaeological analysis of 282 prehistoric individuals from the Ohio River valley shows that tooth size decreased progressively from the Late Archaic to the Woodland period, coinciding directly with the adoption of pottery and cooked grain porridge Hill2018
    . Reductions were most pronounced in maxillary molars, mirroring a steady drop in dietary abrasiveness as foraging was supplemented by garden produce Hill2018
    . By the onset of the Late Woodland period, these indigenous societies had developed the sedentary villages, pottery traditions, and horticultural economies that defined later prehistoric Kentucky Hollenbach2022Hill2018
