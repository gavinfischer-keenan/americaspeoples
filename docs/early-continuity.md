# Early Americas and Pacific continuity

The v0.17 candidate adds **37 shared observations** and three dedicated place guides with contextual movies: **Caral/Supe, Kuk and Upano**. These are observations at a smaller number of places, not 37 newly discovered towns. All use the master map; the shared endpoint remains 1934. The total is 2,075 records: 2,017 shared and 58 focused.

## What changed

| Area | Added detail | Limits retained |
|---|---|---|
| Honduras | Eleven separate El Gigante use phases across the early Holocene and later prehistory | Model boundaries are approximate. Long gaps at this shelter are not extended into regional depopulation. |
| Southern Peru | Two economic stages at Pernil Alto | Original monograph coordinates replace the tempting but misplaced secondary geographic reference. Six archaeological phases are summarized as two broad stages, not a fabricated growth curve. |
| Supe/Huaura valleys | Áspero, Lurihuasi, Chupacigarro, two Caral construction observations, later transformation, and two widely separated Vichama occupations | Single construction-reference dates are not founding dates. The environmental explanation for Caral’s transformation remains qualified. |
| New Guinea highlands | Early Kuk plant use, later field systems, renewed drainage, sweet-potato-era gardens, Kawelka departure after warfare and the 1933 observation | Cultivation phases, dated samples and oral-history reconstruction stay separate. Wetland disuse is not abandonment of the whole valley. |
| Upano | Individually located Sangay/Huapula and Kilamope, later mound reuse, and early/later Lake Cormorán land-use signals | Regional phase dates do not directly date every platform. A lake catchment is not a town or a valley-wide population count. |
| Vanuatu | Uripiv’s Lapita, post-Lapita and much later occupation samples | Mobility evidence does not identify a unique voyage route or demonstrate when all traffic stopped. |
| Southern Massim | Two Malakai/Nimowa occupation observations, with pottery-exchange context | Original abstract checked; full excavation tables remain unverified. The location is explicitly an island reference, not the excavation coordinate. |

The two existing Kuk records now use Denham and colleagues’ scientific calibrated onset windows: 6950–6440 cal BP for mounding and 4350–3980 cal BP for ditching. They share a canonical place reference with the new phases, allowing local chronology to connect them.

The historical map counter now says **foundation records**, matching what it counts. It is not a count of all inhabited places or people.

## Deep dives now have four lists

The directory reorganizes the existing collection into **Subjects, Places, Languages and Cultures**. It includes all existing independent movies, contextual stories and site/culture guides. Chaco and the three new anchors combine a guide and contextual movie on one card. The original URLs remain usable.

- **Independent movie:** only that topic, on its own clock.
- **Contextual movie:** focus and chapters within the shared historical world. Zooming out retains other contemporary evidence.
- **Read guide:** a published place/culture study, with sources and local detail.
- **Coming soon:** a planned dedicated study, with no fabricated playback link.

The initial visible queue includes the two requested Australian subjects, fifteen additional archaeological places, four language studies and four cultural studies. These are editorial research priorities, not claims that those places lack all map records. Chaco, Machu Picchu, Cahokia, Mesa Verde, Hovenweep and Canyons of the Ancients keep their available guides and are separately marked for expansion.

## Evidence, access and unresolved leads

One complete Consensus Deep dossier was requested for this campaign. Cumulative usage is **70/100 calls**, including all four submission/poll calls in this intake; 30 remain. The [research thread](https://consensus.app/search/produce-one-comprehensive-source-grounded-research/rXHxjPj4SXyP6yI7Jo4MeQ/) is linked for access. Its complete returned document and citation markers are retained privately, not copied into the public atlas.

The new records contain original factual summaries and links to the underlying research. Full texts were checked for El Gigante, the Caral construction paper, Caral’s environmental interpretation, Vichama, the Kuk chapters, Uripiv, the Upano supplement and Lake Cormorán. Pernil Alto uses its publisher’s detailed synopsis and full geographic chapter. Malakai/Nimowa uses the authors’ institutional abstract with that access limit stated. The Upano critique is identified as an e-letter; its institutional abstract is not represented as a replacement chronology.

Calibrated BP is referenced to 1950. Uncalibrated radiocarbon ages are not converted into calendar dates by subtraction. One source’s date for a sample is not silently promoted into a city’s entire lifespan. Public location references are distinct from surveyed building footprints. Sangay/Huapula and Kilamope use separate published municipal inventory coordinates checked against the archaeological setting. Lake Cormorán uses the study’s lake coordinate.

The dossier also identified promising leads at Vampiros, Aguadulce, Finca Guardiria, San Isidro, Acahualinca, Nanchoc, Quebrada Jaguay, Telarmachay, Tikopia, Pangpang, New Caledonian sites and additional Massim/Bismarck sequences. These remain candidates where date semantics, accessible source detail, overlap with existing evidence or location precision still need work. In particular, the 2005 Nanchoc paper’s headline radiocarbon ages must not be mistaken for its older calibrated ranges.

This intake improves continuity; it does not complete Central America or Melanesia. El Salvador and Nicaragua remain much weaker than the new Honduras sequence. Post-Lapita Solomons and New Caledonia still need named, individually dated places. Australian pastoral/mining growth and Atlantic Brazilian/Patagonian gaps remain queued. No new route is fabricated from the mere presence of imported material or a non-local isotope signature.
