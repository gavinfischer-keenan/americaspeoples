# Community history topic movies

Version 0.13 adds three separate topic movies: **Haudenosaunee history and lacrosse**, **Amazonian peoples and languages**, and **The Caribbean and forced African migration**. They add **31 chapters** to the seven existing movies, for **ten topics and 83 chapters**.

The atlas contains **1,860 source-linked records**. This batch adds **26 records: 24 shared historical records and two subject-only lacrosse records**. Topic clocks and cameras remain separate. Watch and Explore still show the wider world at the chosen date. Each topic chapter offers links to shared evidence at that record’s own date; that date may differ from the chapter.

## What the movies cover

| Topic | Chapters | Selected coverage |
|---|---:|---|
| Haudenosaunee | 9 | Four ancestral Mohawk village estimates; Ganondagan and its 1687 destruction; the 1779 campaign; Grand River resettlement; Canandaigua diplomacy; Onondaga lacrosse and the 1932 selection contest. |
| Amazonian peoples | 8 | Separate early and late deposits at Isla del Tesoro; Sangay / Huapula; Cotoca and Landívar; Upper Xingu networks; Belém; Putumayo violence; Rio Negro institutional and language evidence. |
| Caribbean societies and enslavement | 14 | Existing Indigenous towns; the 1492 encounter; Henrietta Marie’s forced crossing; a Maroon treaty; Haitian revolution and independence; Garifuna detention/deportation; distinct emancipation and labor transitions in Jamaica, Martinique, Curaçao, Puerto Rico and Cuba. |

These are selected histories, **not exhaustive inventories** of towns, languages, wars, voyages or communities. The topics end within the shared 1934 horizon. A later publication or present-day community explanation can interpret an earlier event without adding a later event to the timeline.

## Dates and places that need particular care

- **Mohawk villages:** [Manning, Lorentzen and Hart (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0258555) supply preferred 68.3% highest-posterior-density occupation estimates: Smith-Pagerie 1478–1498, Klock 1499–1521, Garoga 1550–1582 and Brigg’s Run 1619–1632. These are modeled windows, not exact foundations or proof of one community moving through all four sites. The locality points have approximately 5 km uncertainty. No excavated longhouse footprint is invented.
- **Ganondagan and lacrosse:** [Ganondagan’s own interpretation](https://www.ganondagan.org/about/SiteHistory.html) and [Onondaga’s explanation of lacrosse](https://www.onondaganation.org/culture/sports/lacrosse/) retain community perspectives. A tradition is not forced into a first-invention date. The 1932 marker explicitly locates the team’s home community, not the match venue. Modern reconstructed buildings are not treated as original structures.
- **Diplomacy and removal:** The [Canandaigua treaty text](https://www.onondaganation.org/government/the-canandaigua-treaty-of-1794/) records commitments, not proof of compliance. The [Grand River history](https://www.ontario.ca/page/six-nations-grand-river) identifies resettlement without implying that all Haudenosaunee left their homelands. No new reservation polygon is inferred.
- **Isla del Tesoro:** [Lombardo et al. (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0072746) report 10,604 ±126 cal BP for basal cultural material and 401 ±51 cal BP for a butchered caiman fragment in the upper cultural layer. The display preserves those reported ranges, converted from the 1950 BP reference: −8780 to −8528 and 1498–1600 in astronomical years. This is arithmetic conversion, not recalibration. The two records do not assert uninterrupted occupation between them. Figure 1B supplies an approximate 3 km locality anchor.
- **Cotoca and Landívar:** [Prümers et al. (2022)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9177426/) and [Supplementary Table 1](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-022-04780-4/MediaObjects/41586_2022_4780_MOESM1_ESM.docx) identify sites 185 and 168. Their public UTM20S points were transformed to WGS84 and rounded to 0.01 degree. The 500–1400 CE interval is the regional Casarabe phase; it is not direct dating of each building at these two sites. No language or modern ethnic identity is assigned from earthwork form.
- **Putumayo:** The [published Casement correspondence and testimony](https://centroderecursos.cultura.pe/sites/default/files/rb/pdf/Libro%20Azul%20Britanico.pdf) supports a record of coercion, torture and killings. The 1904–1910 window covers reported episodes. The public La Chorrera anchor orients a much larger station network; it does not place every atrocity at the town. Inquiry in 1910 and publication in 1912 remain distinct.
- **Garifuna deportation:** [Garifuna community history](https://gahfu.org/garifuna-history) distinguishes detention, departure, a Jamaica stop and arrival at Roatán. Conflicting counts remain unresolved. The line has only two schematic endpoints; the Jamaica stop and the separate captured transport to Trujillo are described in the evidence, not passed off as a surveyed ship track.
- **Emancipation:** [British archival guidance](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/british-transatlantic-slave-trade-records/), the [French archival dossier](https://www.culture.gouv.fr/mc/content/download/310540/file/20220511_DP-AN_Decret-abolition-esclavage.pdf?inLanguage=fre-FR&version=1), [Curaçao’s register guide](https://nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/zoekhulpen/curacao-slaven-en-emancipatieregisters-1839-1863), [Puerto Rico’s abolition history](https://guides.loc.gov/world-of-1898/abolition-slavery-puerto-rico) and the [Cuban collection guide](https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/c8v98gwv) support separate local dates. Abolition of a trade, legal enslavement and compulsory labor are not one event. No bulk personal register or restricted dataset was copied.

## Cross-references and display

All 24 new settlement, encounter, political and coercion records belong to the shared atlas. They are not duplicated into a disconnected topic dataset. Cotoca and Landívar enrich their existing canonical records with verified public locations, retaining the original site claims and Casarabe network links. Existing places and episodes—including Caguana, En Bas Saline, Belém, Upper Xingu, Vertières and Henrietta Marie—retain their canonical records.

The two lacrosse records remain subject-only. Their audit entries link relevant independently dated community history, without treating a game as a town’s foundation. All 46 subject-only records now have explicit shared-context decisions in the audit. Reciprocal related-record links let readers move in both directions.

A topic’s displayed date means the whole calendar year. A November treaty is therefore available in its 1794 chapter; it is not accidentally hidden by a January 1 cutoff. Expedition playback retains its more precise day-level clock in the shared atlas.

Old planned bookmarks such as `#topics/haudenosaunee` open the playable movie. Ten requested topics remain in the future queue. “See also” links state why another history matters and distinguish playable movies from planned research.

Tablet and computer views are the primary targets. Basic phone layout remains supported without promising the complete large-screen experience.

## Gaps to work on next

- More Haudenosaunee nations, named villages, diplomacy, women’s political roles, school histories and dated lacrosse episodes. This edition’s archaeological sequence is specifically Mohawk; it does not substitute for Wendat history or every Iroquoian-speaking society.
- Detailed Upper Xingu site phases and public coordinates; additional Amazonian settlement networks; local language attestations, multilingual practices and community-authored histories of withdrawal and contact. Current isolated communities’ sensitive locations are not published.
- Earlier documented African arrivals, more individual Caribbean voyages, diverse African origins, Indigenous resistance and continuity, additional Maroon communities, Danish and other island histories, and local life after emancipation. One documented ship is an example, not a representative census.
- Directly dated building growth and defensible land boundaries. The new sites are points at their declared precision; neither circles of influence nor broad phase ranges supply an invented building plan.

## Research and reuse

One combined Consensus **Deep** dossier supplied leads for this batch. Including dispatch and polling, cumulative usage is **38 of the user’s 100-call cap**. The complete provider report is preserved privately and is not bundled in the site or downloads. Records publish original summaries, source locators and links to the underlying research, documents and community accounts.

Some discovery metadata described book reviews as if they were the books themselves; those were not adopted as primary evidence. Dates and map membership were checked against the sources actually used. Automated validation checks consistency and links; it is not a scientific peer review.
