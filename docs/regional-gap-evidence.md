# Regional evidence and source decisions

This campaign adds **43 dated observations to the shared master map**: 11 in Central America, 17 in South America and 15 in Australia. These are observations at 33 distinct locality IDs, not 43 towns or population estimates. All are within the shared timeline ending in 1934. Stories and expeditions can show them as surrounding context; none is restricted to topic mode.

## Research and call budget

One completed **Consensus Deep** dossier supplied research leads across the three regions. Starting cumulative usage was 53 of the 100-call limit. Call 54 submitted the Deep brief; calls 55–58 checked its status. **Cumulative usage is 58/100, with 42 calls remaining.** Polls count conservatively toward the cap. No Quick or Pro searches were used. The returned report and citations are retained privately; the public atlas contains original summaries and underlying source references.

The dossier is a discovery aid, not a source of historical authority. Publication records, original excavation reports, archival documents and community/custodial histories were checked separately. A source labeled “access checked” can be an author abstract or searchable archival catalog where stated in its locator; it does not imply reading an inaccessible full paper.

## Continuity and uncertainty

Historical place markers remain available after their dated evidence, explicitly labeled as earlier evidence rather than a proven annual population. A reserve's administrative closure is not abandonment: Coranderrk residents remained after 1924. Canudos is different: archival evidence supports destruction of that settlement in 1897, while survivors and later communities must not be erased.

Dates spanning several years usually describe a possible observation or event window. They do not assert continuous activity unless the record explicitly encodes an occupation interval. The Canudos community phase is an interval; the Kurtonitj objects' date range is not.

Most new points are rounded public town/locality references with stated uncertainty, not surveyed buildings. Kumako uses a generalized settlement-area point; Kurtonitj uses the public UNESCO component reference, not a house coordinate. Old Canudos is distinguished from the modern town; Santa Rosa de Ocopa is the Andean convent, not Puerto Ocopa. The localities should be refined from public site surveys before representing building outlines.

## Leads held back

- Earlier Makassan contact models: old wood and underlying deposits complicate their interpretation. The established exchange record is retained; no earlier continuous route is inferred.
- Muldoons fish-trap rebuilding: calibrated sample intervals and their association with specific structures need direct extraction before adding construction phases.
- Cofán/Siona rubber-era movements: the dossier highlights survival and rebuilding, but named village dates and older locations require further primary-text checks. Modern isolated-community locations are not inferred from broad ethnonyms.
- Mojos rubber-era agency: a dossier citation resolves to a book review. It is not treated as the underlying archival study.
- Murutucu's supposed 1711 chapel and an early archaeological mean: disputes over structures and sample meaning prevent turning either into a secure mill-foundation date. The independently documented 1780 workers are mapped instead.
- Mission suppression does not establish that all mission towns were abandoned. No blanket 1767 disappearance is encoded.

## Remaining priorities

The largest continuing weaknesses include Amazonian communities outside the Belém cluster; Gran Chaco and Guianas local sequences; Central American village continuity after independence; and Australian northern/inland local histories between colonial encounters. Places with a single dated attestation still need additional independent observations. These are missing research and resolution, not proof of empty land.

The [coverage audit](world-coverage-audit.html) compares this campaign with an immutable pre-change snapshot. Its rectangular regions overlap, and its counts exclude route-only evidence and historical-memory markers. Do not add the region totals together or interpret a zero as population absence.
