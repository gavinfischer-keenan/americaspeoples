# One place, several pieces of history

The November 2026 display audit started from the 2,317-record v0.20 atlas. Bluefish Cave II has **one** published record. Its grey dot represented the cave evidence; a detached red asterisk meant that a claim was disputed. It was not a second cave or a second find.

The renderer now attaches the qualification to the feature: a red rim for disputed interpretation, gold for discussion, with a plain-language explanation on hover or keyboard focus. Regional shapes carry the qualification on their outline. No separate asterisk is placed on the map. The old pattern affected 385 dated record candidates across ordinary points and point-based overlays (not necessarily visible at the same year).

The coordinate audit found 270 shared anchors across points, regional areas and route endpoints. Of these, 174 groups were eligible individual point features; 56 contained a mixture of drawing layers, such as a foundation plus later occupation or a conflict at the same place. Eligible records now share a single numbered marker. Opening it preserves every source record, date and interpretation. Routes and regional areas retain their geometry; nearby buildings are not merged merely because they are close.

Two Sagitario entries had identical titles but date different charcoal samples. Their titles now identify P06 and P07. Three Northwest Coast terrace records share generalized wording and a regional reference point, but name three distinct places; deleting them would erase evidence. The scan found no justification for deleting those records or the Bluefish record.

This is a reproducible identity, coordinate and rendering audit, not a claim that every historical overlap has been resolved. Counts describe the pre-change catalog; the shared-marker rule also applies to subsequent additions and visitor corrections. Source identities and proposal identities remain distinct.

Browser checks cover Bluefish at close and overview scales, keyboard inspection, construction and dated-find phases, and a conflict at a settlement. Existing selection tests continue to check that areas do not block individual places.
