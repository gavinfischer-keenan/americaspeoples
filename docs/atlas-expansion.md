# A more connected atlas

This release adds selected histories of Pacific habitation and exchange, Indigenous and colonial settlements, missions, violence, forced movement, voyages, and transportation. It is an expanding educational collection, not a census of every town or conflict.

## Watch or explore

Watch offers narrated scene sequences and a separate dated-expedition selector. Expedition playback moves a symbol between documented stops and holds it for recorded stays. The line between stops is schematic; it is not a recovered daily ship log. Some itineraries cover only a well-documented portion of a longer expedition. The itinerary panel names those limits and sources. Norse presence is shown as dated archaeological evidence without inventing daily saga itineraries. Cabrillo's San Diego visit is distinguished from Balboa's crossing of the isthmus.

Explore supports local zoom, evidence search, and individual record selection. Dotted borders, rail references, and expedition symbols never intercept clicks on towns. Overlapping evidence can be selected through the overlap chooser. A visible date explains which boundaries are shown even when the evidence list spans all periods.

The Pacific view includes Australia, New Guinea, island Asia, and the Americas. Detailed present-day coastlines improve small-island orientation. These are geographic reference coastlines, not reconstructed ancient shorelines. Mainland connectors are schematic directions to supported origin or destination regions, not complete settlement mapping in Europe, Asia, or Africa.

## Continuity and movement

- Habitation is separate from a founding event. Ongoing communities can remain visible through the atlas endpoint of 1934 without implying extinction then.
- A documented or inferred departure has its own range and explanation. A local site ending does not mean its people vanished. Rapa Nui is not depicted as abandoned.
- Exchange lines appear within supported evidence windows. Imported stone can demonstrate material movement without proving a return voyage, a direct crossing, or continuous annual traffic.
- Forced transport of enslaved people and forced removals retain their coercive meaning. Acadian expulsion in 1755 and later Louisiana resettlement are separate stages.
- Violence records distinguish lethal evidence, mixed injuries, healed trauma, and uncertainty. Unknown perpetrators remain unknown; burning or abandonment alone is not automatically evidence of killing.
- Wrecks accumulate as loss records. A vessel's intended destination is not plotted as an actual completed arrival.

## Boundary sources and meaning

| Layer | Included coverage | Meaning and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Newberry Atlas of Historical County Boundaries | Selected U.S. states, territories and acquisitions from 1783 through 1934 | Dated administrative/claimed extents. Earlier statehood is not implied by a dataset start. Proposed Deseret is excluded. Louisiana Purchase changes into later territorial versions. |
| HGIS de las Indias | Spanish provinces, principal divisions, and foreign colonial claims, 1701–1808 | Annual scholarly reconstruction. Provincial detail appears when zoomed in. The last year is a dataset boundary, not simultaneous independence. |
| Cliopatria | Selected earlier American colonial components and Latin American polities in 1809–1885 | Coarser reconstruction. Known misleading transition dates/names are omitted or explicitly corrected with sources. It does not supply every local province. |
| CShapes 2.0 | Selected American and Pacific states/dependencies, 1886–1934 | Dated national/dependency versions. A dependency is not necessarily independent. Coverage begins in 1886, not its founding year. |
| Original reservation reconstructions | Navajo versions of 1868, 1878 and 1880; Hopi order of 1882 | Approximate legal-description geometry with dates and source limits. Navajo mapping stops before the next unsupported 1884 change. These are neither all reservations nor full ancestral homelands. |

Boundaries are outlines without filled areas. Annual playback selects the version valid on December 31, so a predecessor and successor do not both appear as current after a midyear replacement. Exact source dates remain in the source panel. A line describes an administrative record or claim, not exclusive Indigenous occupation, legitimate ownership, or effective control. Cessions are not silently converted into reservations.

## Built transportation

The railway layer uses Jeremy Atack's historical U.S. railroad reconstruction. It accumulates segments with a known opening year or first historical map attestation. It does not extrapolate construction along proposed lines or imply continuous service after opening. Source dates sometimes mean “in operation by,” not a precisely known first day. Regional records add selected built connections in the Caribbean, Mexico, Central and South America, Hawaiʻi and Alaska. Pony Express service has a limited 1860–1861 window.

## Remaining gaps

The highest priorities are more Indigenous settlement sequences, Pacific travel frequency and securely dated endings, local Caribbean towns, Brazilian and Spanish American provincial changes, reservation versions after the selected examples, and regional rail construction sequences outside the contiguous United States. Violence coverage is especially uneven; absence of a marker does not establish peace. Many voyages have only selected dated stops. No claim is made that every colonial city, settlement, mission outside Alta California, shipwreck, battle, railroad, or reservation is mapped.

All 21 Alta California mission foundations are represented, with separately sourced early sites and selected relocations. Approximate anchors are described as approximate. A surviving mission church is not automatically the original foundation site. Archaeological locations stay coarse where appropriate; the atlas is not a guide to accessing or disturbing sites.

## Sources and reuse

Source catalogs travel with the download: `boundary-sources.json`, `expanded-boundary-sources.json`, `early-national-boundary-sources.json`, `reservation-boundary-sources.json`, `voyages.json`, and `rail-network.json`. Individual evidence reviews link the underlying sources and distinguish abstracts, full text, and institutional summaries. Acquisition scripts preserve source identifiers, dates, transformations, and hashes.

Eight Consensus Deep reviews supported this research cycle; 22 total connector calls, including polling, were used against the requested limit of 100. Raw reports remain private. The public atlas contains independently checked, original evidence summaries and source links.
