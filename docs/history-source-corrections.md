# Focused historical corrections — 15 November 2026

This audit changes two existing history rows and two Heywoods proposal rows, and adds one separately sourced Rapa Nui continuation. Existing record IDs remain stable. No Consensus calls were made; the completed reports were not changed.

## Pitcairn material transfer

`history-mangareva-pitcairn-henderson` previously drew a two-way line from Mangareva straight to Henderson. The revised line runs from Pitcairn to Henderson and represents the origin and recovery of sourced stone, without asserting return vessels or an itinerary. Both endpoints are approximate island anchors with 25 km uncertainty.

[Molle and Hermann, ANU Press chapter 2](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/n4467/html/ch02.xhtml), especially the material-provenance and abandonment sections, supports this narrower interpretation. The retained 1050–1450 window is explicitly a broad archaeological context envelope. Its early bound comes from settlement context, not a dated first shipment. Its later bound marks inferred export contraction, not proof that all travel stopped. The broader network citation was removed from this narrower claim.

## Rapa Nui continuity

`history-rapa-nui-regional-occupation` retains its original 1250–1860 dates and genomic claim, now typed as habitation. A linked new row, `history-rapa-nui-historical-continuity`, covers the later regional evidence through the application's 1934 horizon. Neither record asserts abandonment. The 1860 join is an editorial division of source coverage, not a new settlement date.

The [National Library-hosted historical volume](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/archivos2/pdfs/MC0062395.pdf), edited by Cristino and Fuentes (2011), records nineteenth-century residents on printed pp. 27–29 and reproduces testimony from Métraux's 1934 visit on p. 21. Only community presence is used; its earlier collapse interpretation and inconsistent nineteenth-century population figures are excluded. The source is a historical synthesis reproducing testimony, not a newly examined original census.

The [UNESCO conservation report](https://articles.unesco.org/sites/default/files/medias/fichiers/2024/05/uns-ranoraraku_diagnostico-04.pdf), p. 15, independently documents local people confined at Hanga Roa during the company period spanning 1934. Its publication page identifies 2023, although the upload URL contains 2024. Title pages and cited pages of both PDFs were downloaded into memory and extracted for inspection after the browsing tool rejected their size. Regional continuity does not imply stable population or unchanged occupation at every site.

## Heywoods geographic placement

Both Heywoods records now use `[-59.53, 13.17]`, rounded from the island coordinates in the [WHO and Barbados Ministry of Health report](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/mental-health/who-aims-country-reports/barbados_who_aims_report.pdf), Introduction p. 10. They remain regional overlays with 30 km uncertainty and no archaeological site location. The earlier `[-60, 13]` anchor was offshore.

[Fitzpatrick's publisher-hosted paper](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/C28B75878B7BAD772ADBC91A966BCDB3/S0033822200039072a.pdf/verification-of-an-archaic-age-occupation-on-barbados-southern-lesser-antilles.pdf), p. 598, locates Heywoods north of Speightstown; this does not supply a Trench 39 coordinate. The publisher URL replaces the legacy Arizona link, which now redirects to an access-denied page. Dates remain separate sample-age envelopes. The adze abstract gives 2530–2200 cal BC, while Table 1 and Results give 2530–2220; the existing broader envelope is retained and the discrepancy disclosed. Marine calibration and cultural-association cautions remain.

A semantic before/after comparison confirmed that only the two named existing history rows and two Heywoods proposals changed; all other rows, including Florida, were unchanged by this audit.
