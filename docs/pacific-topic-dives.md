# Pacific topic movies and connected history

Version 0.12.0 adds separate movies for **sweet potatoes, boats/voyaging and dogs**, alongside horses, maize, trade and languages. Each keeps its own clock and focused map. Ordinary Watch and Explore continue to show the shared historical world.

This intake adds 16 topic evidence records and nine shared human-context records, bringing the published collection to 1,834. Seven topic movies contain 52 chapters. This is selected evidence, not a comprehensive inventory of Pacific archaeology, crop introductions, boats or dogs.

The boats movie also reuses existing Sahul, Wallacean, Arctic, Lapita, coastal trade and Hawaiian interisland evidence. A crossing inferred from island occupation does not identify a boat design. The Gulf Coast canal remains reading evidence: its one-sided construction constraint cannot support a bounded movie date. Modern experiments inform explanation rather than becoming ancient events.

## Shared context and reciprocal links

All 44 subject-only records across the seven dives have a recorded context decision in `data/topic-context-audit.json`. Existing shared evidence used by a topic remains globally eligible. New shared records cover Hawaiian provisioning of the visiting expedition in 1779, Wairau Bar, Triangle Flat, Anaho, Kohala land use, deliberate burials at Matja Kuru 2 and Stilwell II, the Ruiz expedition’s encounter with Indigenous merchants, and the Russian missionary arrival at Unalaska.

Subject records and their canonical context link in both directions. “In the shared history” displays each context record’s own date: a related earlier place is not silently made contemporary with the chapter. The explicit shared-map action selects that record’s supported date and clears expedition focus. Ordinary return preserves the previous shared time, camera and filters. A topical specimen remains outside the master map even when its associated human activity is included there.

No new settlement interval is manufactured where a regional specimen or document provides no independent habitation chronology. Existing La Isabela, Puerto Real and Xihuatoxtla topic pins now reuse their canonical public locality anchors. Other points retain explicit regional or approximate precision; none of these additions claims surveyed building geometry.

## Related and planned stories

“See also” explains why another topic matters. Available topics open their independent movies; planned topics open the clearly marked research queue. The queue includes all requested later histories, with links among missions, forced relocations, languages, purchases, maritime extraction, forced African migration and treasure shipping. See [the roadmap](roadmap.md).

Only the later Hawaiʻi statehood story, and territorial-purchase story if needed, have permission for a topic-specific endpoint beyond 1934. No endpoint is extended in this release. The master atlas and every currently playable topic still end in 1934.

Tablet and computer screens are the primary design targets. Phone support remains a basic, best-effort layout with secondary controls under disclosures; it does not drive full-feature layout decisions.

## Research and reuse

One combined Consensus **Deep** run was completed for these three topics. The cumulative ledger is **34 calls out of 100**, including 31 earlier calls, one dispatch and two polls. The verbatim research report stays outside the public repository and package. Published content uses original summaries, primary-source links and bibliographic metadata; Consensus is a discovery tool, not evidence for a historical claim.

Original educational content and copyrightable curated data remain CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; software remains PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0. Underlying scholarly works keep their own rights. No source figures, full papers or provider reports are republished. Source-specific access and evidence limits are retained per record.
