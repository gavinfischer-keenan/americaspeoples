Both issues are labelled `feature request`. Site and culture pages are now being implemented in v0.5.0; 3D model ingestion and the ancestry graph remain future work.

Owner request 2026-10-29: eventually host exceptionally detailed historic-site studies, including 3D models of Hovenweep and other places, supporting media, sources and explanations.

Keep these as site-detail resources, separate from the lightweight global catalog. Large 3D meshes, textures and media should load only when a visitor opens the relevant deep dive. Preserve stable site IDs and individual settlement granularity so one regional culture does not become a substitute for a specific building/site. Each future asset needs its own source, capture/reconstruction method, revision and appropriate credit. A reconstruction must remain distinguishable from observed geometry.

For the second feature, selecting present-day tribal geography should open a sourced predecessor graph and allow backward exploration on the map. Distinguish jurisdiction, reservation/reserve lands, traditional territories and overlaps. Keep community histories, cultural relationships, language descent/contact and genetic evidence separate. Support multiple predecessors, admixture and disputed alternatives rather than forcing an exclusive tree. Every relationship needs sources, time guidance and explicit uncertainty; identity and citizenship are not inferred from genetics. Present-day communities require a view beyond the historical animation's 1934 endpoint.

No 3D viewer, model ingestion or ancestry graph is implemented in this release. The initial site-page collection separates locations from culture/civilization summaries, with cited religion/ritual, language and rise/transformation sections. Chaco and Inca pages include schematic geographic road-network maps with explicit source/precision limits. See data/site-research-queue.json for the next regional complexes.


## Focused-topic research queue — November 2026

Sweet potatoes, boats/voyaging and dogs join horses, maize, trade and languages in the next release candidate. Requested later histories, in priority order:

1. Haudenosaunee history, lacrosse and regional influence.
2. Amazonian peoples: settlement histories, languages, connections and documented isolation.
3. Caribbean Indigenous societies, forced African migration, enslavement, resistance and resulting communities.
4. Forced Indigenous relocations across all the Americas and Pacific: removals, reservations and changed boundaries, forced boarding/residential schools, resistance and survival. Expand case by case beyond a North American focus.
5. Hawaiʻi from Queen Liliʻuokalani and the overthrow through annexation, territorial rule and statehood. User authorizes a later endpoint for THIS topic only; the shared atlas and other topics retain 1934.
6. Territorial purchases, starting with Alaska and Russia. Follow Indigenous ownership, consent or lack of consent, and subsequent dispossession as well as the governments’ transaction. If necessary, this topic alone may extend beyond 1934.
7. Russian and other Pacific-coast fur trades, coerced labor, coastal communities and marine-animal consequences.
8. East Coast whaling and worldwide voyages, crews, ports, whale populations and affected communities; linked to the Pacific fur-trade topic.
9. Spanish gold AND silver: extraction and labor, inland transport, ports, convoy logistics, security, piracy, naval attacks and wrecks. Separate documentary history from legends.
10. Missionaries throughout North, Central and South America, including Brazil, from the earliest documented institutions. Link conversion, negotiation, coercion, community formation, language work, removal and schooling. Mission names need not begin with San; a saint’s name alone proves nothing. Add missing documented operating missions and settlements to shared history with their own dates, distinguishing institution, community and later town.
11. Pottery.
12. Obsidian exchange.
13. Copper working.

The app shows this queue as not yet playable. “See also” links explain the relationship and distinguish an available movie from a planned subject. Every future intake must audit shared-map context: learned settlements, voyages, peoples and encounters become canonical shared evidence when supported, with reciprocal references to topic findings. A specimen date or a printed vocabulary does not create an invented settlement lifespan.
