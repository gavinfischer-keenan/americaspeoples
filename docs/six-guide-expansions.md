# Archaeological places in connected regional histories

The six existing guides—Chaco Canyon, Machu Picchu, Cahokia, Mesa Verde, Hovenweep and Canyons of the Ancients—now distinguish more named features and local phases. A Four Corners parent movie connects its local studies to migration, changing social relationships, colonial violence and continuing Indigenous communities. Publication status is recorded in the [release receipt](https://github.com/gavinfischer-keenan/americaspeoples/blob/main/docs/releases/v0.20.0-verification.json).

Every new ordinary historical observation belongs to the master map. Contextual movies use that same clock: zoom out to see contemporary histories elsewhere. A construction phase, a dated timber and an occupation interval are different evidence. The end of one observation does not make a people disappear. The close-up chronology names the observation as well as the place.

## Regional and local studies

Four Corners is a geographic way into these histories, not one ancient polity or a claim of exclusive descent. Its local studies include Chaco, Mesa Verde, Hovenweep, Canyons of the Ancients, Aztec, Salmon, Canyon de Chelly and communities around present-day Cortez. Pueblo Bonito is nested within Chaco; Lowry, Sand Canyon and Castle Rock sit beneath Canyons of the Ancients. All existing guide addresses remain available. The Cortez study describes ancient communities near the modern town rather than projecting that town into antiquity.

Living Pueblo, Diné and Ute accounts are attributed to their communities. The regional movie does not turn archaeological labels into a single family tree, or equate shared buildings, objects, languages and political identities. Animal DNA is an indirect line of evidence for human movement; a schematic regional arrow does not identify an exact family, village or walking route.

## What the dates and points mean

- Chaco: small-house communities, great-house additions and later reuse; the physical Great North Road corridor is distinguished from schematic regional connections. The road diagram is an overview with varying dates, not a surveyed map of a single moment. Hierarchy, inequality, household life and post-Chaco change are discussed as interpretations with their evidence and alternatives.
- Mesa Verde: pithouses, Far View buildings and reservoir phases, later villages and cliff-dwelling construction. Sample dates do not become full building lifespans.
- Hovenweep and Canyons: individual public structures, Lowry and Escalante phases, Sand Canyon, Castle Rock and other named places. Castle Rock's interpreted violent ending is mapped without inventing the attackers.
- Machu Picchu: nine named architectural features use an explicitly broad site-level occupation context where a building-specific calendar date is unavailable. Chapter viewing years are not a proposed construction order. Existing alternative site chronologies remain visible.
- Cahokia: separate mound, plaza, stockade and Woodhenge observations. Bayesian event ranges are probability windows for construction, not dates of continuous residence. The later mission anchor is corrected to the Monks Mound locality; it is not a chapel footprint.
- Aztec and Salmon: separate early construction, later modifications, communal structures and reuse. Salmon's Great Kiva roof replacement is tied to the documented 1263 timbers; earlier dates with no dendrochronology remain identified as estimates.
- Canyon de Chelly: Antelope House and Mummy Cave phases, White House context, attributed Diné art, the 1805 killing, 1864 destruction and 1931 land-rights context. The massacre location is a broad northern canyon anchor, not the modern overlook.

Public feature anchors are checked against institutional site plans and archaeological studies. Some geographic references come from OpenStreetMap and retain its attribution and database terms. A point does not claim a surveyed footprint. At very close zoom the existing terrain may be coarser than the building locations, particularly outside the local U.S. relief tiles. No copied plans or invented building outlines are included.

## Further regional movies

The same parent-and-local-study structure is promising for the Basin of Mexico, the Cusco/Sacred Valley landscape, the north coast of Peru, the Maya lowlands, and Mississippi/Illinois river communities around Cahokia. These are proposed research groupings, not newly completed regional movies or claims that each region had one culture. Existing local guides remain available while the connecting histories are researched.

## Reading and using the atlas

All map inspectors and full evidence pages lead with what happened or what was found, a recognizable place, and the date. Specialist dating, geographic precision and provenance remain available in closed evidence disclosures. This release rewrites 322 record descriptions, adds eight fuller historical accounts within that total (including Maní in 1562), and makes 213 exact editorial revisions across stories, topics and 26 guide pages. These edits improve the available evidence; they do not claim that every older record has received new primary-source research.

Every one of the 35 place guides now starts with a labeled terrain map. Linked public feature anchors identify buildings and places; modern elevation supplies shaded relief and contours. Labels open dated records. Contour intervals, geographic registration, source tiles and file hashes are recorded in the bundled terrain manifest. These maps are not reconstructed building footprints. A compact expandable key repeats every feature name and its location notes.

The two upper Watch sidebar sections collapse independently, leaving room for simultaneous-history cards. Their state survives playback updates. Deep-dive tiles have a broad native link target while secondary guide/movie actions remain separate. Hover text is shorter and positioned away from the hovered feature.

The apparent full-screen ice at 11,966 BCE was a rendering defect: four small polygon rings were inverted on the sphere. Their winding is corrected without changing any coordinates. All 147 ice snapshots are checked for accidental whole-world coverage, and future ingestion normalizes the same geometry.

## Final review and decisions

One fresh whole-branch review identified three important issues, all corrected in a bounded follow-up pass:

1. Five timber/sample-only observations now use dated-evidence semantics rather than settlement foundations. Their dates and coordinates are unchanged; documented construction sequences remain separate.
2. Regional/local study links keep the shared year. Playback seeks that year within the receiving movie; a year beyond its range remains visible on arrival, with explicit replay available. Ordinary newly opened movies still begin at their opening chapter.
3. Plain date explanations distinguish broader site/architectural periods and an attributed artist's lifetime from measured or modeled event dates. The full source qualifications remain available.

Regressions reproduced these failures before the fixes and passed afterward. The actual parent/local round trip checks 1265 and a between-chapter year of 1250. A visual inspection also caught and removed a repeated Maní archaeology paragraph. No unresolved review findings remain. No further research calls were used.
