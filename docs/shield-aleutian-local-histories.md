# Inland Shield and Aleutian village histories

Version 0.23.0 adds 51 observations to the shared atlas: 37 across Canada's inland Shield and 14 in the Aleutian Islands. Ten existing observations are revised under their original IDs, with before-and-after records retained. The catalog now contains 2,531 records: 2,458 shared and 73 focused. Four new guides and contextual movies add 33 chapters; five more chapters join the existing Canada and Aleutian overview movies. All retain the surrounding historical world and the shared endpoint of 1934.

## Canada's inland Shield

The new regional guide follows 27 named localities from Labrador to Great Slave Lake, through northern Ontario, Québec, Manitoba and Saskatchewan. Ancient hearths and worked quartz at Kamestastin, a plant-fiber wrapping on a copper object near Thunder Bay, sturgeon gathering places at the Long Rapids and food remains at Wanipigow give readers concrete evidence of inland life. Later chapters follow trading posts, Indigenous and Métis communities, treaty meetings, company-directed moves, schooling and the flooding of Lac Seul without the First Nation's consent.

The guide has 15 chapters, a labeled terrain map, original historical writing and links to community accounts, excavation reports and institutional sources. It sits under Canada, whose overview now connects eight regional guides. Existing Fort William, Mashteuiatsh and Fort Providence observations are reused. Cummins retains its original identity and unresolved calendar chronology while gaining a readable description and public Thunder Bay reference.

## Reading Aleutian villages

Three local studies sit under the Aleutian Islands guide:

- **Amaknak Bridge:** stone walls, rooms, hearths, subfloor channels, storage and rebuilding. The original explanatory diagram links its features to individual charcoal samples and the site-wide architectural observation. It describes relationships between structures; it is not a surveyed excavation plan or a geographic footprint.
- **Iliuliuk / Unalaska:** the village shore, successive churches and trade connections. A full-sheet U.S. Coast Survey chart, published in 1875 from the 1871–72 survey, shows buildings, the church, hills and harbor soundings. Readers can open the full-resolution chart and follow named features to dated atlas records.
- **Chaluka and Nikolski:** household work, a stone house, the distinct Anangula settlements, chapels, the 1930 church and the 1926 sheep ranch. Earlier tools and later houses are kept as separate observations.

The wider Aleutian movie also gains Sandy Dunes' house sequence and the cod-fishing shore at Pauloff Harbor. Anangula and Korovinski records are improved in place, and earlier Amaknak sample dates remain intact. A new west-room charcoal record is distinct from the existing main-room sample. Local terrain maps stay focused on each village while the guide reading and shared movie retain nearby context.

## Dates, locations and rights

Forty new observations have supported calendar placement. Eleven remain unplaced in calendar time and are readable in guides and Explore. Raw radiocarbon BP is not treated as calibrated calendar age. Broad phase comparisons at Black Lake are identified as tentative; source dates for samples do not silently become the full lifespan of a house. Documented operating periods remain distinct from one-time foundations and observations.

The atlas uses public locality references where building positions have not been verified. The relative Amaknak diagram does not introduce fabricated building coordinates. Published excavation figures were not copied or traced. The full Iliuliuk chart retains its source credit and public-domain status in the United States; the project's noncommercial license does not override that status. Its library source, rights statement, file dimensions and checksum are recorded in the media manifest and source register. New prose is independently written, with links to the underlying evidence and further reading.

## Research budget and next gaps

This batch used one Consensus Deep dispatch and two retrieval calls: 3 of the 63 available at the start. Across the renewed allowance, 15 of 75 calls have been used and **60 remain**. The complete generated report stays private; the public research notice links to the thread and the published records cite the underlying sources.

This is a regional expansion, not complete coverage. Additional Shield household sequences and individually documented village histories remain worthwhile. Aleutian excavation drawings still need explicit reuse permission and verified geographic control before they can become full building plans on the master map. The sources already support richer accounts of houses and local change, which this release makes available now.
