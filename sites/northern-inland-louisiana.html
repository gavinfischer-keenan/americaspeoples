Caddo places on the Red River

The Red River connects older Caddo places to later trade posts. Early Caddo communities built mounds, maintained gathering places and left a diverse material record at Mounds Plantation. Belcher preserves evidence from a later period, including pots and food remains that document events at a particular Red River site. Those places put locally dated activity between inland Louisiana’s older monumental sites and its eighteenth-century accounts. Source 1 ↗ Source 2 ↗

Two colonial posts

The French post at Natchitoches and the Spanish presidio at Los Adaes were close enough for exchange across ever-changing colonial claims. Indigenous communities traded with both. Recording the dates of the posts helps tell this political history while the Caddo evidence gives the guide a much earlier starting point. Source 1 ↗ Source 2 ↗

Evidence from different kinds of sites

A mound assembled over time, an excavated funerary context and the first known use of a trade post each support a different type of statement. Mounds Plantation data date particular archaeological material, with a narrower age published for some tomb logs. Belcher has a broad archaeological phase whose limits derive from comparisons across excavated finds. The reference points keep sensitive contexts at an appropriate public scale. Source 1 ↗ Source 2 ↗

People and trade across the line

Los Adaes and Natchitoches sat near each other across a contested edge of colonial authority. Their buildings required neighbors and supplies. The Texas Archeological Research Laboratory’s local account describes a frontier where people could sell, buy, barter and seek practical help on both sides. The two posts feature in the atlas alongside the longer chronology of Caddo communities. Source 1 ↗ Source 2 ↗
