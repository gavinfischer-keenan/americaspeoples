Changing use of the Sigatoka dunes

Looking across the dateline reveals several histories of island building and land use. On Viti Levu, the dunes at Sigatoka preserve more than one phase of activity. The later Navatu-phase deposits show how archaeological evidence extends beyond the first Lapita communities. The continuity of site names in a research report does not show that the same place was used in every intervening year. Source 1 ↗

Mounds, paths, and new shores

On Tongatapu, flat-topped mounds, open places and roads developed at more than one locality. Work near Fua‘amotu adds a much earlier construction horizon than the better-known chiefly tombs. At Lapaha, reclamation pushed the shoreline outward around a later political center. On Savai‘i, the initial stage of Pulemelei mound provides another architectural history. Each record describes something people built on one island at one time and gives context for comparing similar shapes across the region. Source 1 ↗ Source 2 ↗ Source 3 ↗

Levuka and sovereignty

At Levuka, the original Deed of Cession places a formal political action on Ovalau in 1874. The signing links an existing port to an imperial change in sovereignty and puts the political choices of Fijian chiefs within the mapped record. The map carries those changes alongside evidence of what communities built centuries earlier. Source 1 ↗

Three different construction histories

A calendar reference for construction may represent a single sample or a set of modelled estimates. The ranges for an early flat-topped Tongan mound, reclaimed land at Lapaha and the first phase of Pulemelei refer to different kinds of work. They reflect independent island chronologies even where people faced similar engineering tasks. Source 1 ↗ Source 2 ↗ Source 3 ↗

Nukuleka — coral-file founder horizon

A worked coral file associated with basal Lapita material dates to 2838±8 BP; the 2015 study reports 2846–2830 calBP at 2σ for the founder horizon.

Dates and evidence notes

Samoan Islands — early settlement evidence

The NPS synthesis places Samoan settlement as early as about 1000 BCE and describes enduring fa'a Samoa across later political divisions.

Dates and evidence notes

Source dates: 1000 BCE
Dating method: Sourced foundation or arrival chronology; negative historical years converted to astronomical year numbering.
