Five centers with different evidence

Cotoca and Landívar are major centers, while Cerrito, Santa María and Salvatierra show other scales and configurations of the same broad architectural tradition. Lidar identifies elevated platforms, enclosures and connections; excavation at Salvatierra supplies stratigraphy and dated samples. Combining these approaches reveals a connected landscape without making every mapped structure contemporary. Source 1 ↗

Seasonal water and agricultural work

Water could be both an excess and a scarce resource. Canals could drain savannah soils in wet months, while reservoirs and farm ponds retained water through drier periods. Santa María’s arrangements provide a named architectural example of water management; the separately sampled agricultural ponds address a different scale of evidence. The purposes of every canal and basin should not be treated as identical. Source 1 ↗ Source 2 ↗

The 2025 study examined 178 phytolith samples from eighteen profiles. Maize dominates the sampled agricultural evidence, but the body of the paper also reports Cucurbita in two adjacent pond samples and a Manihot pollen grain. The proposed seasonal farming system and possibility of multiple harvests do not establish that all regional food consumption was maize, nor that every household followed one uniform strategy. Networks, enclosures and political uncertainty

Causeways connect some known centers and branch through an engineered landscape. Gaps and platforms near enclosure crossings have been interpreted as restricted access, while some enclosure forms may have had defensive roles. These are functional hypotheses from architecture, not documentation of named wars, armies or boundaries. Other causeways have no identified destination, leaving their daily or ceremonial uses unresolved. Source 1 ↗

The study proposes a settlement hierarchy with several tiers and describes low-density urbanism: monumental centers embedded within a broad modified landscape. That interpretation does not supply a precise population or prove one centralized state. Smaller unmounded settlements may be less visible in lidar, so the mapped hierarchy is also shaped by what the method can detect. Source 1 ↗

Radiocarbon, landforms and later activity

The regional 500–1400 CE horizon comes from dated excavations and archaeological comparisons. At Salvatierra, the selected Phase 1 and Phase 5 contexts are modeled charcoal likelihoods within a stratigraphic sequence. A charcoal age is not automatically the date when a platform was raised, and early samples beneath buildings can represent natural activity or earlier use rather than the Casarabe construction itself. Source 1 ↗

Pond 690’s approximately 1250–1550 calendar CE sequence was modeled from sediment dates. It extends later than the broad urban-system horizon and is retained as its own observation. The result cannot extend the occupation of every town to 1550 or establish the cultural identity of every later pond user. Sampled forest profiles likewise leave other woodland uses possible even where cultivation and fire were not detected. Source 1 ↗

Cerrito — platforms, enclosure and connected causeways

Cerrito has a raised base platform, a core of large platform structures, an enclosure and radiating causeways. Surveyed connections integrate it with several neighboring centers within the Casarabe settlement network.

Source date guidance: Regional Casarabe archaeological attribution, approximately 500–1400 CE.

Santa María — platforms and engineered water storage

Santa María combines platforms beside a former river channel with dams, two circular reservoirs and an enclosing earthwork. The arrangement demonstrates local water engineering within the Casarabe architectural tradition.

Source date guidance: Regional Casarabe archaeological attribution, approximately 500–1400 CE.
Dating method: Lidar/topographic association with the dated regional tradition, not independent dating of every dam, platform or reservoir.

Water flow and reservoir operation are reconstructed from landforms. The distinct farm ponds studied in 2025 must not be relocated to Santa María.

Monumental Mounds Region — Pond 690 maize and sediment chronology

Pond 690 sediments record repeated maize microfossils within an approximately 1250–1550 CE modeled sequence. Two adjacent samples contain Cucurbita phytoliths and one sample contains Manihot pollen; the authors interpret ponds and drainage as complementary seasonal cultivation systems.

Source date guidance: Approximately 1250–1550 calendar CE, published Pond 690 age-depth interpretation.
Dating method: Seven AMS measurements on sediment humin, SHCal20 calibration and OxCal 4.4.4 P_Sequence age-depth modeling; published approximate use span.

Unplaced: published regional figure inspected but no sample-specific coordinate has been transcribed and verified. Maize dominance in sampled fields is not proof that all regional diets contained only maize. Agricultural intensity and multiple harvests are interpretations.
