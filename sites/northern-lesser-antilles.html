Early ceramic sites

Montserrat’s Trants site provides a local reference for early pottery-using communities. Toolmaking evidence elsewhere on Montserrat may be older, though the surface material does not carry the same dating confidence. In Guadeloupe, excavation at Anse à la Gourde recovered building traces and domestic material in a much longer ceramic sequence. The island chain links very different sites, so ceramic similarities can support a question about contact without establishing an exact voyage. Source 1 ↗ Source 2 ↗

Forts and labor

Saint Kitts and Antigua became prominent military and naval centers during struggles over the islands. At Brimstone Hill and English Harbour, permanent works took shape through the skills and coerced labor of enslaved Africans. Their builders are part of the place history. The dated institutions belong alongside the earlier community evidence rather than defining where the history begins. Source 1 ↗ Source 2 ↗

Anse à la Gourde’s coast

The scale of Anse à la Gourde’s coastal habitation is visible through excavated traces of domestic structures. Its long sequence combines people’s activities from multiple periods. The earliest ceramic context at Trants and the later structures in Guadeloupe provide a starting point for comparing local pottery, food and house histories, with several centuries of evidence still thin between some islands. Source 1 ↗ Source 2 ↗

Working across small islands

Northern Lesser Antillean islands were close enough to enable frequent crossings, especially among neighboring groups. Similar finds across islands can suggest ties when supported by archaeology, while dated activities at ports and forts tell a later institutional history. At Brimstone Hill, the earliest known British cannon emplacement is one moment in the much longer creation of the fortress. Source 1 ↗ Source 2 ↗
