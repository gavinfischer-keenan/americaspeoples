Southeast Asia & the Spice Islands: Early Hominins, Jade Voyagers, Maritime Entrepôts & Maluku

Linking the Bay of Bengal and South China Sea through island straits and monsoon corridors, Southeast Asia hosted three distinct Pleistocene hominin lineages, indigenous bronze-drum networks, and maritime sultanates supplying global cloves, nutmeg, camphor, and pepper.

Laang Spean, Lenggong, Bubog–Callao, Ille–Batanes, Viengxay–Vilabouly, Hội An–Giong Ca Vo, Chaiya Kra Isthmus, Santubong, Temasek Singapore, Kota Batu Brunei, Makassar, and Maluku Banda–Ternate–Tidore.

Deep island hominins and Hoabinhian rainforest–maritime foragers

Pleistocene crossings deposited *Homo erectus* in Java, *Homo floresiensis*, and *Homo luzonensis* in northern Luzon (~67–50 ka), while early *Homo sapiens* adapted across Laang Spean, Tam Pà Ling, Lenggong's Toba-ash beds, Ilin Island's Bubog Strait (~33 ka), and Palawan's Ille Cave. [1] [15] [9] [5]

South China Sea bronze-drum and Fengtien jade spheres

Waterlogged timber copper shafts at Vilabouly (Laos) and Khao Wong Prachan (Thailand) fed Đông Sơn bronze-drum foundries while Austronesian outriggers moved green Taiwan nephrite *lingling-o* rings between Batanes, Palawan, An Sơn, and Sa Huỳnh ports. [7] [16] [2] [10]

Indian Ocean portages, Srivijaya, Funan and Majapahit

Overland Kra Isthmus trails (Chaiya–Takuapa), Mekong canal cities (Óc Eo–Angkor Borei), West Java's Buni–Batujaya sites, and Straits maritime polities (Srivijaya, Temasek, Melaka, Brunei Kota Batu) fused Sanskrit–Arabic–Malay statecraft with transoceanic monsoon shipping. [8] [3] [11] [17] [14]

The Maluku Spice Epicenter: Banda, Ternate, Tidore and Makassar

Until early modern transplantation, endemic nutmeg/mace on the Banda islets and cloves on Ternate–Tidore traveled west via Makassarese, Javanese, and Malay fleets to Hội An, Martaban, and Mantai—driving world maritime competition from Zheng He to the VOC. [13] [4] [12] [18]
