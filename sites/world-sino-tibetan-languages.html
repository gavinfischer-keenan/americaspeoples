Sino-Tibetan Languages: Yellow River Millet Farmers to Over 400 Tongues

Spoken by more people today than any other family on Earth except Indo-European, Sino-Tibetan encompasses Chinese (Mandarin, Yue, Wu, Min, Hakka), Classical and vernacular Tibetan, Burmese, Karen, and hundreds of highland mountain tongues curving from Gansu across the Himalayas into Yunnan.

Connects Neolithic Yangshao–Majiayao millet archaeology and Oracle Bone epigraphy with recent Bayesian computational language phylogenies.

Dated phylogenies meet Yellow River Neolithic farms

Comparative lexical analyses and Bayesian divergence clocks published in PNAS and Scientific Reports converge on a Neolithic Yellow River homeland around ~5,900–7,200 years BP among foxtail- and broomcorn-millet farmers whose split sent one vast radiation southwestward along Himalayan-river gorges and another across northern China. [1] [2]

Scripts adapted from oracle bones to Tibetan blockprint

Whereas Chinese logographic characters stabilized literary comprehension across mutually distinct Sinitic varieties from Yue to Mandarin, Tibetan scholars adapted Gupta Indic alphabetic principles in the 7th century CE into a phonemic script that still preserves ancient Old Tibetan consonant clusters. [1] [2]

Tone genesis and upland linguistic mosaic

Linguists reconstruct how losses of voiced initials and final consonant clusters sparked tonal contrasts independently across both Chinese varieties and highland Tibeto-Burman tongues stretching down Yunnan–Myanmar river gorges. [1] [3]
