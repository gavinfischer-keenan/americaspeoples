Settlement, meals and movement across the Pacific

At Kamgot, shoreline reconstruction and food remains place settlement within a working coastal landscape. Evidence of plants and marine resources brings daily tasks into a history often centered on ocean voyages. At Uripiv, isotope research addresses diets and childhood residence, opening a different window into how people lived.

Evidence: press-files.anu.edu.au [1] ↗ · press-files.anu.edu.au [2] ↗ · journals.plos.org [3] ↗

Fishing, exchanged materials and related practices reveal maritime connections without supplying an itinerary for every voyage. Decorated pottery helps archaeologists compare sites, but it cannot name each speaker or traveler. The strongest history connects the material traces while allowing island communities to retain their different sequences and the uncertainty of particular journeys to remain visible.

Evidence: press-files.anu.edu.au [1] ↗ · journals.plos.org [2] ↗ · press-files.anu.edu.au [3] ↗

At Kamgot on Babase Island, the relationship between village and lagoon shaped everyday possibilities. Archaeologists study the former shoreline as well as pottery and deposits to work out where houses stood in relation to water. Lapita settlements elsewhere occupied raised coastal ground or included houses on posts over wet places. These were practical choices for communities whose movement and food supply were closely tied to the sea. Changing shorelines now mean that the position of a site on today’s land does not reproduce its original coastal setting.

Evidence: press-files.anu.edu.au [1] ↗

The Kamgot food remains show a mixed economy. People collected shellfish, caught fish and turtles, and used terrestrial resources. Identified fish include reef-associated groups and fish from a broader range of marine habitats. Pig and dog remains add evidence for introduced animals, while starch residues on pottery include granules consistent with taro. These discoveries connect seafaring with food preparation and cultivation. Pots were part of meals and household work, as well as objects whose decoration helps archaeologists recognize connections between distant communities.

Evidence: press-files.anu.edu.au [1] ↗

Voyages eventually brought Lapita settlement to Tonga. At Nukuleka, coral artifacts and radiocarbon samples provide closely studied evidence for an early settlement on Tongatapu. Later sites trace occupation through the central and northern island groups. The 2015 chronological study combines short-lived plant samples and coral dates to follow this expansion at a finer scale than broad ceramic periods allow. Its results describe a relatively brief initial Lapita phase, followed by a transition toward the Polynesian Plainware tradition as communities continued living on these islands.

Evidence: journals.plos.org [1] ↗

The distribution of decorated pottery thus records both travel and the establishment of homes. A settlement required more than a successful landfall: people needed food, freshwater, material for houses and continuing relationships with others. Coastal site positions at Kamgot and successive occupations in Tonga give different views of that process. As local histories lengthened, pottery and other practices changed. Following those changes makes Lapita a history of moving and settling communities rather than a single unchanging package carried across an empty ocean.

Evidence: press-files.anu.edu.au [1] ↗ · journals.plos.org [2] ↗

What the name Lapita can and cannot do

Lapita is an archaeological category built around material patterns, especially decorated pottery. It is useful for comparing sites, but it is not a single ethnic identity, a biological ancestry or a language automatically readable from a potsherd. Source 1 ↗

Settlement and everyday food

Kamgot’s shoreline reconstruction and plant and marine remains bring local work into a history often dominated by voyaging. Uripiv’s isotope study adds evidence about diet and childhood residence. These methods answer different questions and must retain their own limits. Source 1 ↗ Source 2 ↗ Source 3 ↗

Seafaring without invented routes

Fishing assemblages, exchanged materials and related material practices demonstrate maritime connections at different scales. They do not authorize drawing a particular family’s voyage between every sampled site. Chronological models can compare island sequences while leaving uncertain routes uncertain. Source 1 ↗ Source 2 ↗ Source 3 ↗

After decorated pottery, and among living communities

Later occupation at Uripiv shows why the end of a pottery category is not an automatic departure. Vanuatu Cultural Centre authors also describe the contemporary significance of Lapita research. That living engagement is acknowledged here without adding post-1934 events to the historical map. Source 1 ↗ Source 2 ↗
