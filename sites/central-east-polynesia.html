Society, Cook and Marquesas island histories

Preservation on Huahine

At Fa‘ahia, waterlogged deposits on Huahine preserved wood and fiber alongside harder artifacts. That unusual preservation opens questions that stone tools alone cannot answer. It also gives a reason to check what each radiocarbon sample actually dates. The deposit records changes in a coastal place used by people with skills in making, sailing and fishing. Source 1 ↗

Marquesan valleys

On Nuku Hiva, Hatiheu Valley shows the local use of fertile ground by the late thirteenth to late fourteenth century. This evidence extends the existing Nuku Hiva entries into a separate valley. Regionally comparable evidence is available for a small number of intensively excavated sites. It gives starting points for comparing household life without implying a uniform settlement date across the archipelago. Source 1 ↗

Ceremonial and missionary connections

On Raiātea, the shore and valleys at Taputapuātea include different forms of marae, places with ceremonial and political roles. Relations among sacred places, upland cultivation and maritime access fit into a long local sequence. Later, two Polynesian teachers linked the Society and Cook Islands through missionary travel. Their arrival on Aitutaki in 1821 belongs in a history of ocean crossings and local decisions, as much as in an account of an overseas church. Source 1 ↗ Source 2 ↗ Source 3 ↗

How the chronology fits

Samples from the lower cultural layer at Fa‘ahia place early local activity between 1050 and 1160. At Pahumanoo-te-tai the authors instead describe an estimated window for the first dated use of a valley locale. Taputapuātea’s heritage chronology spans different monuments and parts of the site. Comparing their dates shows how maritime connections could persist while local communities changed at their own pace. Source 1 ↗ Source 2 ↗ Source 3 ↗

Anaho — a coastal plant-processing community

Anaho’s worked shell tools and plant residues document food processing in an inhabited Marquesan valley. Several introduced plants were part of this local economy.

Dates and evidence notes

Source dates: Fourteenth-century tool context selected from a longer sequence
Dating method: Archaeological shell-tool assemblage and context chronology.

No house plan or continuous settlement lifespan is inferred from the tool assemblage. The selected century is one dated part of a longer valley history.
