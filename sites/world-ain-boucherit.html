Aïn Boucherit

Two artifact-bearing horizons near Aïn Hanech in Algeria broaden the known geographic distribution of early Oldowan technology. Their animal bones preserve evidence of cutting and breaking carcasses in the Sétif region.

Two episodes at one locality

Both levels yielded flaked artifacts associated with animal remains. The published age estimates place the lower level at about 2.4 million years and the upper one at about 1.9 million years, well separated within the sedimentary sequence. [1]

How the ages were estimated

The team combined the sedimentary sequence with magnetic polarity, faunal comparisons and electron spin resonance information. The resulting ages place toolmakers in North Africa early in the known Oldowan record. The oldest level’s estimated age depends on placing it between dated stratigraphic markers. [1] [2]

Processing animal tissue

Marks on bones indicate removal of flesh and the breakage of bone for marrow. Researchers distinguished cut and percussion marks from damage made by carnivores and other processes. The assemblages preserve behavior without identifying the species of toolmaker. [2]
