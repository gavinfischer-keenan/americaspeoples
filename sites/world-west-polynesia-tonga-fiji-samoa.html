Fiji, Tonga & Samoa: Lapita Foundations, Fortified Chiefdoms & the Tuʻi Tonga Network

Around the International Date Line, Fiji, Tonga, Samoa, and Futuna formed the crucible where Remote Oceanic Lapita voyagers transformed into classic Polynesian maritime chiefdoms.

Bourewa, Vatia & Bua Fijian hillforts, Talasiu/Nukuleka & Lapaha Tongan capital, Samoan Pulemelei star mound, Tataga-Matau adzes, and Futuna.

Monumentality, warfare & prestige exchange

During the 12th–17th centuries CE, the Tuʻi Tonga state consolidated Tongatapu through monumental coral-faced *langi* royal tombs, canoe canals at Lapaha, and LiDAR-mapped earth-ditch forts forged amidst dynastic expansion and civil conflict. [6] [16] [7] [8]

High-precision LA-ICP-MS geochemistry on stone tools from Lapaha reveals long-distance elite adze imports from Tutuila (Tataga-Matau, Samoa), Fiji, and Central Polynesia—integrating fortified Fijian hillfort provinces and Samoan *tiʻa*/Pulemelei chiefly hubs. [15] [14] [1] [17]

From Nukuleka Lapita villages to Ancestral Polynesian transformation

Early Lapita shell-midden layers at Nukuleka, Talasiu, and Futuna evolved into plainware horticultural villages across West Polynesia, setting the linguistic and social foundations of Polynesian chiefdoms. [9] [10] [4]

Hillforts (*koro ni valu*) & Little Ice Age warfare

Archaeological GIS visibility modeling and LiDAR scans across Bua/Vatia (Fiji) and Tongatapu show widespread earth-ditch ramparts, watch ridges, and fortified enclosures protecting chiefly lineages amid regional competition. [1] [8] [7]
