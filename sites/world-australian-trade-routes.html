Continental Trade Routes of Aboriginal Australia: Ochre, Pituri, Greenstone & Pearl Shell

Far from isolated bands, Aboriginal Australia was crisscrossed by thousands of kilometres of ceremonial Songline trade corridors moving prized ochres, stone blades, baler shells, and narcotics across arid deserts and river basins.

Geochemically sourced ochre (Wilgie Mia, Parachilna), Mulligan *pituri*, Mount William greenstone axes, Kimberley *riji* shell, Torres Strait, and Macassan maritime trade.

Sourced materials across desert & river networks

Scientific provenance methods (LA-ICP-MS, portable XRF, XRD, and direct AMS radiocarbon on shells and wooden artifacts) now trace Aboriginal Australian exchange objects far beyond 19th-century ethnographic maps. Shimmering specular ochre from Wilgie Mia (WA) and Pukardu (SA) moved along structured Dreaming tracks carrying pituri (*Duboisia*) south from the Georgina–Mulligan basin. [1] [2] [6] [9]

Across southeastern waterways, greenstone blanks from Mount William (*Wil-im-ee Moor-ring*) traveled >700 km along Murray–Darling kinship ties, mirrored in Northern Australia's *leilira* blades and maritime exchange across Torres Strait and Arnhem Land's trepang coasts. [11] [18] [19] [20]

Kimberley pearl shell, desert balers & sandstone grindstones

Directly radiocarbon-dated marine baler shells (*Melo*) and incised *riji* pearl-shell pendants found over 1,500 km inland across the Great Sandy and Gibson Deserts demonstrate continental social webs tying northwestern reef harvesters to Central Australian groups. [4] [5] [7]

Arnhem Land–Makassar *trepang* fleets & Torres Strait mariners

Across Australia's northern seas, Yolŋu communities harvested sea cucumbers alongside monsoon fleets from Makassar (leaving rock-art paintings, tamarinds, and loanwords), while Torres Strait islanders mediated Papuan outriggers with Cape York ochre. [14] [15] [16]
