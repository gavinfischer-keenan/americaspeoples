A closer reading

Households within a large village

Sand Canyon Pueblo’s residential suites, refuse and shared buildings reveal a lived village. Households worked within a settlement that also included a great kiva and other communal spaces. The connection between domestic rooms and collective architecture makes ordinary activity part of the same history as gathering and ceremonial life.

Evidence: crowcanyon.org [1] ↗

Most building belongs to the middle and later thirteenth century, from the late 1240s or early 1250s into the late 1270s. Some beams were older, perhaps salvaged from other structures. Their dates preserve histories of wood as well as building. Excavated context helps distinguish those earlier materials from the main construction sequence of the village itself.

Evidence: crowcanyon.org [1] ↗

Construction and reused wood

Crow Canyon’s synthesis dates the main building span to the late 1240s or early 1250s through the late 1270s. Older timbers may be salvaged. The latest sample, 1277vv, is not an exact cutting date, and the movie’s round years do not improve its precision. Source 1 ↗

A lived village

Residential suites, refuse deposits, and shared structures reveal domestic and ceremonial activity within Sand Canyon Pueblo. Its great kiva was one communal space within an inhabited village, alongside rooms where households lived and worked. Source 1 ↗

The terminal evidence

Violent deaths and the conditions of final deposits affect how excavators interpret the village’s ending. Neither attacker identity nor a single regional cause follows from that evidence. Places, finds and events on the map

Sand Canyon Pueblo — public settlement

Sand Canyon Pueblo was occupied from the late 1240s or early 1250s until about 1280 CE. Published estimates place its population at roughly 400–600 people.

Dates and evidence notes

Source dates: Occupied between late AD 1240s or early 1250s and approximately AD 1280
Dating method: Primary excavation database synthesis of archaeological dating

Map point represents a named public place or access location, not settlement extent. Approximate dating does not establish uninterrupted residence. Population estimates, when present, have no annual growth series.

Sand Canyon — lethal violence around 1280

The 2002 analysis identifies evidence for at least eight violent deaths at Sand Canyon around 1280 CE. The date is approximate and refers to the violent episode, not the entire village’s occupation span.

Dates and evidence notes

Source dates: AroundAD 1280, approximate event.
Dating method: Original source CE chronology as stated; editorial decade envelopes are identified. No new radiocarbon calibration.

A minimum identified death count is neither a total death toll nor village population. This study does not independently prove drought caused the attack. No resident count or annual population curve is inferred. Archaeological architecture, sampled individuals and fatalities are different quantities.

Sand Canyon Pueblo — village construction sequence

Tree-ring evidence places village construction from the late 1240s or early 1250s through the late 1270s. Earlier timbers are interpreted largely as wood reused from older farmsteads.

Dates and evidence notes

Source dates: Late 1240s or early 1250s to late 1270s CE; early timbers probably reused.
Dating method: Published archaeological and institutional chronology, with its original level of precision retained.

1240 is the coarse beginning of the named 1240s decade and 1279 the end of the named 1270s decade; neither is an exact construction date. This is a construction envelope, not continuous building in every year. Existing Sand Canyon Place is reused and remains an access-side anchor. Official public place/access point, rounded to six decimals; representative of named settlement, not a footprint or coordinate accuracy claim.
