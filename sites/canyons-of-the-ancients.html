Canyons of the Ancients

These public sites represent different moments and forms of ancestral Pueblo settlement in southwestern Colorado. Lowry's longer growth contrasts with the concentrated thirteenth-century histories of Sand Canyon Pueblo and Castle Rock Pueblo.

Three communities, three archaeological records

Canyons of the Ancients protects a broad cultural landscape northwest of Mesa Verde and Cortez. Its thousands of recorded places belong to histories extending well beyond the three pueblos shown here. The visitor center and museum provides context before a visit; in the Sand Canyon/Rock Creek management area, travel is restricted to designated routes. Source 1 ↗

Major additions occurred about 1085–1170.Grew from a small settlement to about 40 rooms, eight kivas, and a great kiva. Sand Canyon PuebloConstruction began in the late 1240s or early 1250s and continued into the late 1270s.An estimated 420 rooms, 90 kivas, fourteen towers, a plaza, a great kiva, and a D-shaped bi-wall building. A substantial masonry wall enclosed much of the village around a canyon-head setting.Counts are estimates for a complex archaeological site. Excavated structures and archived study units are not the same as all structures in the pueblo. A wall had defensive potential, but its presence does not explain every aspect of community life.

Castle Rock PuebloOccupied from the 1260s into the 1280s; occupation ended abruptly around the mid-1280s.A village constructed at the base and on top of a prominent sandstone butte. Crow Canyon interprets the position as probably selected in part for defensibility.Excavation evidence indicates a violent ending. That event should not be expanded into an unsupported identity for the attackers or a single explanation for all regional migration. Crow Canyon estimates 75–150 residents; this is an estimate, not a census.

What excavation can and cannot establish

Sand Canyon's excavated rooms, kivas, refuse, and tree-ring samples support a reconstruction of a lived-in village that grew through additions and collective building projects. Some reused or older timbers predate the main construction sequence; individual early wood dates should not automatically be treated as the date the village was founded. Source 1 ↗

Castle Rock's abrupt violent ending is a site-specific finding. Lowry's different chronology and Sand Canyon's much larger architectural footprint caution against treating all places in the monument as one synchronous settlement or one uniform community. Dates, room totals, and excavation coverage need to remain attached to the individual site. Source 1 ↗ Source 2 ↗ Source 3 ↗
