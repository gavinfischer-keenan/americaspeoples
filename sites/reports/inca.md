# **Machu Picchu** and the Inca Heartland

Machu Picchu and the Inca heartland preserve a changing landscape of royal estates, settlements, shrines, agricultural works, and roads—not a single construction episode or uniformly controlled imperial network. The atlas treatment below separates dated occupation, observed architecture, reconstructed sequences, and historical interpretation through approximately 1600 CE.

**Source limitation:** Some supplied records contain substantial primary-text excerpts, others only abstracts or book reviews. Architectural details absent from those records remain unresolved rather than reconstructed from tourist descriptions.

### Antecedents Before 1400 CE

Pikillaqta supplies a Wari-period antecedent, conventionally dated approximately 600–1000 CE; independently investigated earthquake and debris-flow deposits fall within approximately 776–986 CE, although their role in abandonment remains an interpretation [[16.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/prehistoric-evidence-of-crustal-earthquakes-and-debris-garcía-benavente/714eb9a4d6ce513fade83ef2a1ea8551/) [[16.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/prehistoric-evidence-of-crustal-earthquakes-and-debris-garcía-benavente/714eb9a4d6ce513fade83ef2a1ea8551/). Yunkaray, on the Maras Plain northwest of Cusco, was occupied approximately 1050–1450 CE and identified through survey and excavation as an Ayarmaca center outside strong Inca cultural influence [[7.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/archaeological-investigations-at-yunkaray-cuzco-peru-quave-covey/726f3a5076c5535ca7f7f9fe42dc50bf/) [[7.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/archaeological-investigations-at-yunkaray-cuzco-peru-quave-covey/726f3a5076c5535ca7f7f9fe42dc50bf/).

Heartland incorporation was uneven: excavations at Pukara Pantillijlla indicate limited intervention in an existing horticultural and herding village, contrasting with later valley-bottom royal estates [[3.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/kinship-and-the-inca-imperial-core-multiscalar-covey/2d0823163cf35fdeb5bb2adcace7344e/) [[3.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/kinship-and-the-inca-imperial-core-multiscalar-covey/2d0823163cf35fdeb5bb2adcace7344e/). Salapunku preserves pre-Inca, Inca, and colonial occupation, but its supplied abstract does not disclose numerical radiocarbon results [[17.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/human-presence-in-the-salapunku-area-machu-picchu-cusco-sieczkowska-jacyna-bastante/bfb564d3901d55afbddcbddfa32f2670/).

### Imperial Development, Approximately 1400–1533

The traditional 1438 accession of Pachacuti and approximately 1450–1460 construction of Machu Picchu derive from historical chronology rather than direct building dates [[8.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/when-did-the-incas-build-machu-picchu-and-its-satellite-ziółkowski-abuhadba/d12f347952b45bf4a0615de9d061ea9a/). Colonial conquest narratives also risk converting geographically organized records into chronological sequences [[2.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/reconceiving-the-chronology-of-inca-imperial-expansion-ogburn/e1e0808b442359ef91cc783b81c8ff5d/) [[2.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/reconceiving-the-chronology-of-inca-imperial-expansion-ogburn/e1e0808b442359ef91cc783b81c8ff5d/).

Provincial radiometric models challenge late-fifteenth-century expansion dates: Chamical’s initial occupation was modeled at 1410–1480 cal CE, while a Mendoza model placed onset at 1350–1440 cal CE, both at 95% probability [[6.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/dating-the-expansion-of-the-inca-empire-bayesian-models-marsh-kidd/63b7b12ac0f258abb70ea4b4a3d476b4/) [[6.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/dating-the-expansion-of-the-inca-empire-bayesian-models-marsh-kidd/63b7b12ac0f258abb70ea4b4a3d476b4/). These are contested regional estimates: a contextual reassessment instead favored early-fifteenth-century entry into northern Argentina and arrival in Mendoza approximately fifty years later [[9.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/radiocarbon-chronology-of-the-inca-expansion-in-argentina-garcía-moralejo/68508c7cb9625484b2d5b36cbc453ae8/) [[9.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/radiocarbon-chronology-of-the-inca-expansion-in-argentina-garcía-moralejo/68508c7cb9625484b2d5b36cbc453ae8/).

### Colonial Changes, 1533–1600

Cusco’s Spanish foundation in 1534, the 1536–1537 siege, and subsequent rebuilding transformed rather than instantly erased its Inca fabric [[12.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/cuzco-incas-spaniards-and-the-making-of-a-colonial-city-morgado/502005996fc8547ebd1d05762930fcd3/) [[12.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/cuzco-incas-spaniards-and-the-making-of-a-colonial-city-morgado/502005996fc8547ebd1d05762930fcd3/). The House of the Sun became a Dominican convent during substantial interventions in the 1540s–1550s [[12.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/cuzco-incas-spaniards-and-the-making-of-a-colonial-city-morgado/502005996fc8547ebd1d05762930fcd3/).

Vitcos and Espíritu Pampa belong to the approximately 1536–1572 independent Inca resistance landscape [[4.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/vilcabamba-and-the-archaeology-of-inca-resistance-bauer-cruz/d2891edb4a815768814b57576881d8b3/). After 1572, colonial settlement and attempted territorial expansion continued into the early seventeenth century, without implying immediate comprehensive Spanish control [[13.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/voices-from-vilcabamba-accounts-chronicling-the-fall-of-chase/db65a4f0b5d55851a5ed3ab0a642099b/).

## Machu Picchu Site Narrative

### Occupation and Competing Chronologies

Burial-based AMS dating initially supported occupation approximately **1420–1532 CE**, earlier than the traditional foundation chronology [[21.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/new-ams-dates-for-machu-picchu-results-and-implications-burger-salazar/ba4728f060345b8ca7d0381479ad9652/) [[21.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/new-ams-dates-for-machu-picchu-results-and-implications-burger-salazar/ba4728f060345b8ca7d0381479ad9652/). A subsequent Bayesian reinterpretation incorporated tissue-formation ages, age at death, family relationships, grave goods, and a localized calibration mixture, producing boundaries around 1435 CE—1410–1455 at 95%—and 1495 CE—1480–1515 at 95% [[22.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/absolute-chronology-revisited-integrating-precise-lane-marsh/89513b1819765957a3b708f0b58d181e/) [[22.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/absolute-chronology-revisited-integrating-precise-lane-marsh/89513b1819765957a3b708f0b58d181e/).

**These alternatives must remain visible:** the later model permits abandonment before Spanish invasion, whereas the earlier interpretation extends occupation to conquest. Neither supplies construction dates for every surviving structure.

Charcoal-based modeling at Machu Picchu, Chachabamba, and Choqesuysuy remains data-poor, with unidentified wood, possible inbuilt age, and limited replication [[8.8]](https://consensus.app/papers/when-did-the-incas-build-machu-picchu-and-its-satellite-ziółkowski-abuhadba/d12f347952b45bf4a0615de9d061ea9a/) [[8.9]](https://consensus.app/papers/when-did-the-incas-build-machu-picchu-and-its-satellite-ziółkowski-abuhadba/d12f347952b45bf4a0615de9d061ea9a/). Allowing for charcoal age widened first-event estimates to 1421–1476, 1406–1480, and 1336–1470 CE respectively, at 68.2% probability [[8.10]](https://consensus.app/papers/when-did-the-incas-build-machu-picchu-and-its-satellite-ziółkowski-abuhadba/d12f347952b45bf4a0615de9d061ea9a/).

### Construction and Engineering

Geophysics integrated with trenches supports a sequence from natural drainage basin to quarry, engineered fill, and enlarged plaza [[14.10]](https://consensus.app/papers/non-invasive-subsurface-imaging-to-investigate-the-site-masini-romano/9026635033755a1db364104d81c20bd8/). Bedrock lies approximately 2–3.5 meters beneath the present plaza; a proposed earlier sunken plaza remains an interpretation of subsurface geometry and archaeological layers [[14.11]](https://consensus.app/papers/non-invasive-subsurface-imaging-to-investigate-the-site-masini-romano/9026635033755a1db364104d81c20bd8/) [[14.12]](https://consensus.app/papers/non-invasive-subsurface-imaging-to-investigate-the-site-masini-romano/9026635033755a1db364104d81c20bd8/).

Terraces combined cultivation, slope stabilization, runoff management, and support for ceremonial architecture [[14.13]](https://consensus.app/papers/non-invasive-subsurface-imaging-to-investigate-the-site-masini-romano/9026635033755a1db364104d81c20bd8/) [[14.14]](https://consensus.app/papers/non-invasive-subsurface-imaging-to-investigate-the-site-masini-romano/9026635033755a1db364104d81c20bd8/) [[14.15]](https://consensus.app/papers/non-invasive-subsurface-imaging-to-investigate-the-site-masini-romano/9026635033755a1db364104d81c20bd8/). A spring-fed aqueduct supplied the settlement, while engineered fills and buried drainage addressed heavy rainfall and infiltration [[23.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/at-the-other-end-of-the-sun’s-path-a-new-interpretation-of-magli/5d65f503ac7b546fb4d0d3999a9b0184/) [[14.16]](https://consensus.app/papers/non-invasive-subsurface-imaging-to-investigate-the-site-masini-romano/9026635033755a1db364104d81c20bd8/).

Approximately fifty prepared stone elements suggest an intended replacement of an earlier urban canal, demonstrating modification rather than an immutable master plan [[14.17]](https://consensus.app/papers/non-invasive-subsurface-imaging-to-investigate-the-site-masini-romano/9026635033755a1db364104d81c20bd8/) [[14.18]](https://consensus.app/papers/non-invasive-subsurface-imaging-to-investigate-the-site-masini-romano/9026635033755a1db364104d81c20bd8/). Masonry includes rubble walls with mud joints, two-leaf construction with infill, and finely worked ceremonial ashlar [[18.5]](https://consensus.app/papers/a-taxonomy-for-prehispanic-stone-walls-in-southern-peru-lipa-tarque/5e9085a6a0e0529db8facac21eb52ba2/) [[18.6]](https://consensus.app/papers/a-taxonomy-for-prehispanic-stone-walls-in-southern-peru-lipa-tarque/5e9085a6a0e0529db8facac21eb52ba2/).

### Function and Restoration

The royal-estate interpretation integrates architecture, material culture, and ethnohistory, including feasting, diplomacy, and religious activity [[24.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/machu-picchu-unveiling-the-mystery-of-the-incas-alconini/e7e7619c86945c26b3ead00795cef5da/) [[24.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/machu-picchu-unveiling-the-mystery-of-the-incas-alconini/e7e7619c86945c26b3ead00795cef5da/). A competing pilgrimage-center interpretation emphasizes access, landscape relationships, and celestial cycles; it does not negate the observed ceremonial architecture [[23.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/at-the-other-end-of-the-sun’s-path-a-new-interpretation-of-magli/5d65f503ac7b546fb4d0d3999a9b0184/) [[23.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/at-the-other-end-of-the-sun’s-path-a-new-interpretation-of-magli/5d65f503ac7b546fb4d0d3999a9b0184/).

Measured architectural patterns suggest modules near 42 and 54 centimeters, but measurement height, battered walls, and uncertain original setting-out procedures complicate interpretation [[25.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/delineation-of-buildings-at-machu-picchu-by-inca-builders-–-kubicka-sowińska/ed7b0bfe07a852b6b0529cb000b3fc3a/) [[25.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/delineation-of-buildings-at-machu-picchu-by-inca-builders-–-kubicka-sowińska/ed7b0bfe07a852b6b0529cb000b3fc3a/). Reconstruction history must also be checked before treating every visible dimension as untouched Inca work [[25.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/delineation-of-buildings-at-machu-picchu-by-inca-builders-–-kubicka-sowińska/ed7b0bfe07a852b6b0529cb000b3fc3a/).

## Heartland and Vilcabamba Complexes

### Cusco and Sacsayhuaman

Cusco’s surviving walls cannot alone reconstruct the preconquest city: early visual evidence is limited, and plans must distinguish extant from hypothetical fabric [[12.4]](https://consensus.app/papers/cuzco-incas-spaniards-and-the-making-of-a-colonial-city-morgado/502005996fc8547ebd1d05762930fcd3/) [[12.5]](https://consensus.app/papers/cuzco-incas-spaniards-and-the-making-of-a-colonial-city-morgado/502005996fc8547ebd1d05762930fcd3/). Indigenous builders continued Inca-style construction after conquest, sometimes incorporating European tools and forms [[26.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/witnessing-the-invisibility-of-inca-architecture-in-nair/71d5a0225d835c1aa141f7dcb3c43b4f/) [[26.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/witnessing-the-invisibility-of-inca-architecture-in-nair/71d5a0225d835c1aa141f7dcb3c43b4f/).

Sacsayhuaman’s excavation history documents walls, buildings, and conduits, but the supplied contemporary material does not securely inventory or date individual towers and sectors [[27.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/archæological-studies-in-peru/eec778e59d9d56fba5e5b5b926a65a34/). Regional archaeoseismology recorded almost 3,000 earthquake effects across nine Inca sites and inferred an otherwise unreported event within approximately 1400–1533 CE—not a precisely dated destructive episode at every complex [[28.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/reassessing-the-seismic-hazard-in-the-cusco-area-peru-new-combey-audin/46177be742065435a80a2cb61d04f93d/) [[28.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/reassessing-the-seismic-hazard-in-the-cusco-area-peru-new-combey-audin/46177be742065435a80a2cb61d04f93d/).

### Pisac, Ollantaytambo, and Chinchero

Pisac’s terrace study reports mapped units ranging from 4.89 to 110.20 hectares, not a single independently established cultivated total or population capacity [[15.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/biophysical-spatial-functional-and-constructive-huaman-esenarro/dac7e45aed215a608e547418e648d7e9/). Comparative research warns that agricultural works differ in administrative and productive context despite shared engineering principles [[29.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/style-and-function-in-inca-agricultural-works-near-cuzco-niles/c0f14ba10a6e526dbdf57093e70c5889/).

Ollantaytambo’s strongest architectural foundation is Protzen’s measured, aboveground documentation and experimental investigation of quarrying, transport, dressing, fitting, and construction episodes [[1.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/inca-architecture-and-construction-at-ollantaytambo-by-peters/dc4a29559fff5c5e864aa726a6818d88/) [[1.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/inca-architecture-and-construction-at-ollantaytambo-by-peters/dc4a29559fff5c5e864aa726a6818d88/) [[1.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/inca-architecture-and-construction-at-ollantaytambo-by-peters/dc4a29559fff5c5e864aa726a6818d88/). The supplied excerpts do not justify detailed Six Monoliths dimensions, building-specific absolute dates, or a securely dated cause for interrupted work.

Chinchero’s estate attribution to Topa Inca rests on architecture and historical interpretation [[30.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/at-home-with-the-sapa-inca-architecture-space-and-legacy-at-hyland/3deaa40c904552e68845afc6e4a4c8fa/). Restricted approaches to royal apartments and ceremonial use of monumental thresholds complicate simple residential-versus-public classifications [[30.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/at-home-with-the-sapa-inca-architecture-space-and-legacy-at-hyland/3deaa40c904552e68845afc6e4a4c8fa/) [[30.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/at-home-with-the-sapa-inca-architecture-space-and-legacy-at-hyland/3deaa40c904552e68845afc6e4a4c8fa/).

The present plaza and church atrium incorporate colonial remodeling, reused stones, and Hispanic-period engravings [[31.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/los-grabados-de-chinchero-cusco-franch/5957675648115466abc8378342eb5c86/) [[31.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/los-grabados-de-chinchero-cusco-franch/5957675648115466abc8378342eb5c86/).

### Choquequirao

A recent architectural study distinguishes upper political–ceremonial, lower mixed-use, southern ushnu, and eastern/western agricultural-residential sectors [[19.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/ancestral-inca-construction-systems-and-worldview-at-the-esenarro-bacalla/9e5528c8ad8a57908d582f851a553469/). Its ten-sector, 1,800-hectare study extent should not be confused with continuously built settlement or simultaneous occupation [[19.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/ancestral-inca-construction-systems-and-worldview-at-the-esenarro-bacalla/9e5528c8ad8a57908d582f851a553469/).

The supplied study supports spatial description, not an absolute construction sequence, quantified population, or demonstrated role as the final capital.

### Vitcos, Yurak Rumi, and Espíritu Pampa

Vitcos-region survey identified pre-Inca settlement and increasing elite control before 1533 [[5.5]](https://consensus.app/papers/vilcabamba-and-the-archaeology-of-inca-resistance-covey/62a6282668d65e8eab296aba27fa8951/). At Yurak Rumi, carved rock, masonry sequences, ritual assemblages, and burned construction materials provide archaeological context for the historically reported shrine destruction in 1570 [[5.6]](https://consensus.app/papers/vilcabamba-and-the-archaeology-of-inca-resistance-covey/62a6282668d65e8eab296aba27fa8951/) [[5.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/vilcabamba-and-the-archaeology-of-inca-resistance-covey/62a6282668d65e8eab296aba27fa8951/).

Espíritu Pampa excavations included eleven Tendi Pampa buildings and structures in four additional sectors [[5.4]](https://consensus.app/papers/vilcabamba-and-the-archaeology-of-inca-resistance-covey/62a6282668d65e8eab296aba27fa8951/). Inca architectural and ceramic continuity coexisted with roof tiles, iron tools, glass beads, and other introduced materials [[5.7]](https://consensus.app/papers/vilcabamba-and-the-archaeology-of-inca-resistance-covey/62a6282668d65e8eab296aba27fa8951/).

### Political Control Was Variable

Copiapó’s radiocarbon-supported sequence distinguishes road and mountain-shrine installation, direct village intervention, and later isolated administrative buildings interpreted as diplomatic facilities [[49.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/a-chronological-model-for-inca-provincial-expansion-the-garrido/edf441c5544f5bee9d0406b59d2612ee/) [[49.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/a-chronological-model-for-inca-provincial-expansion-the-garrido/edf441c5544f5bee9d0406b59d2612ee/). Full-coverage Atacama research also identifies local mining communities appropriating imperial infrastructure for production and exchange [[50.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/rethinking-imperial-infrastructure-a-bottomup-garrido/4bad6ce1705b597fbdd5d385ad4ee552/) [[50.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/rethinking-imperial-infrastructure-a-bottomup-garrido/4bad6ce1705b597fbdd5d385ad4ee552/).

Humahuaca presents a contrasting interpretation of relatively direct intervention associated with Tilcara production and transverse routes [[51.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/usos-productivos-y-rituales-de-las-rutas-incaicas-del-ochoa-otero/78be87a04f4d5664b498339c7c03ef88/) [[51.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/usos-productivos-y-rituales-de-las-rutas-incaicas-del-ochoa-otero/78be87a04f4d5664b498339c7c03ef88/). Neither roads nor “Andean” material traits alone settle contested imperial incorporation at the Colombia–Ecuador frontier [[52.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/los-límites-septentrionales-del-imperio-inca-y-el-qhapaq-vélez/cc52ae54c0eb5af89fbfc9ac637c0221/) [[52.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/los-límites-septentrionales-del-imperio-inca-y-el-qhapaq-vélez/cc52ae54c0eb5af89fbfc9ac637c0221/).

### Burial Samples and Affiliation

Genome-wide data from 34 Machu Picchu individuals, compared with 34 from Cusco, support heterogeneous ancestry among people interpreted as royal attendants or retainers [[55.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/insights-into-the-genetic-histories-and-lifeways-of-machu-salazar-burger/3e812c421ea85e3facbfdb3813775667/) [[55.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/insights-into-the-genetic-histories-and-lifeways-of-machu-salazar-burger/3e812c421ea85e3facbfdb3813775667/). Oxygen, strontium, and lead isotope analysis of 74 individuals independently supports diverse childhood origins, while geographic assignments remain tentative [[56.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/insights-into-immigration-and-social-class-at-machu-picchu-turner-kamenov/5286130288a25f428b57a5c5a450dc2a/) [[56.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/insights-into-immigration-and-social-class-at-machu-picchu-turner-kamenov/5286130288a25f428b57a5c5a450dc2a/).

These are **sampled cemetery populations**, not a census of builders, visiting royalty, seasonal workers, or everyone who occupied the estate. Earlier reanalysis likewise interpreted the burials as retainers rather than royal-family members and guests [[24.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/machu-picchu-unveiling-the-mystery-of-the-incas-alconini/e7e7619c86945c26b3ead00795cef5da/).

### Language Limits

Imperial society was multilingual; modern Quechua predominance cannot simply be projected backward onto each settlement [[57.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/the-language-of-the-inka-since-the-european-invasion-bright-mannheim/f6321cde57b950c7ba5d41f833b35ae9/). The proposed court-language connection with Puquina rests on sparse onomastic evidence alongside Quechua and Aymara explanations, not direct identification of Machu Picchu residents’ speech [[58.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/unravelling-the-enigma-of-the-‘particular-language’-of-the-cerrón-palomino/f1e2bb4b17635dfdb92e166dc98a36ce/) [[58.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/unravelling-the-enigma-of-the-‘particular-language’-of-the-cerrón-palomino/f1e2bb4b17635dfdb92e166dc98a36ce/).

Resettlement policies continued after conquest and complicate reconstruction of Quechua’s sixteenth-century Cusco-region boundaries [[59.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/language-and-society-in-early-colonial-peru-ramos/cfdaeee7af9d5d14bf74f0a233b6d297/). Genetic ancestry, ceramic style, political affiliation, and spoken language therefore require separate atlas fields.

## Dating, Hazards, and Source Gaps

Radiocarbon ages require calibration rather than direct conversion into calendar years [[60.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/the-intcal20-northern-hemisphere-radiocarbon-age-reimer-austin/348143424ec155abb268e005fe70d092/). SHCal20 addresses the variable hemispheric offset, while tropical South American records can require explicitly justified mixed curves [[61.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/shcal20-southern-hemisphere-calibration-0–55000-years-hogg-heaton/8ff00213b3935f52b1c49700ae8d610e/) [[62.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/intcal-shcal-or-a-mixed-curve-choosing-a-14c-calibration-marsh-bruno/135e59911b5d5d61b9092811fd314660/). Atmospheric modeling estimated approximately 51% northern and 49% southern contributions at Machu Picchu, but this is methodological evidence—not a building date [[63.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/influence-of-air-parcels-from-northern-and-southern-ancapichún-pawlyta/c13bd38d0dcf50718c6d8899b36dc1aa/).

No supplied site-specific dendrochronological sequence dates the requested buildings; the chronology study instead recommends short-lived samples and possible ordered wood sequences or wiggle matching [[8.12]](https://consensus.app/papers/when-did-the-incas-build-machu-picchu-and-its-satellite-ziółkowski-abuhadba/d12f347952b45bf4a0615de9d061ea9a/) [[8.13]](https://consensus.app/papers/when-did-the-incas-build-machu-picchu-and-its-satellite-ziółkowski-abuhadba/d12f347952b45bf4a0615de9d061ea9a/). Across Argentine dates, poor contextual reporting is substantial: 114 measurements, approximately 64%, lacked strong contextual attribution [[9.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/radiocarbon-chronology-of-the-inca-expansion-in-argentina-garcía-moralejo/68508c7cb9625484b2d5b36cbc453ae8/).

Earthquake damage, settlement, infiltration, and slope processes must remain competing explanations where dating is weak [[18.7]](https://consensus.app/papers/a-taxonomy-for-prehispanic-stone-walls-in-southern-peru-lipa-tarque/5e9085a6a0e0529db8facac21eb52ba2/). The supplied evidence does **not establish earthquake-driven abandonment of Machu Picchu**, or a causal relationship with Huaynaputina’s eruption or a particular drought.
