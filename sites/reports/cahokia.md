# Cahokia and **Mississippian Urban Networks**

Cahokia and the American Bottom supported a changing constellation of settlements, monuments, farming communities, and ceremonial connections between roughly 500 and 1600 CE—not one homogeneous people or demonstrably unified Mississippian empire. The review below separates physical remains, chronological models, and interpretations, while identifying where the supplied evidence cannot support atlas-level precision.

**Access notation:** **FT** means substantive full-text excerpts are supplied; **A** means findings are available only through an abstract; **R** means a review or secondary summary. These distinctions concern the supplied text, not whether a publication is obtainable elsewhere. No numerical coordinates are supplied because none can be verified from the indexed passages.

## Regional Chronology

The standard Cahokia phase framework is Lohmann 1050–1100, Stirling 1100–1200, Moorehead 1200–1300, and Sand Prairie 1300–1400 [[16.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/shell-bead-crafting-at-greater-cahokia-kozuch/53fe716f5f7b56568a6a685d5e93e375/). Earlier Grand Plaza investigations placed initial earthmoving before approximately 1000, whereas later corridor excavations emphasized its monumental mid-eleventh-century configuration; these concern different stages of landscape formation [[17.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/investigations-in-the-cahokia-site-grand-plaza-holley-dalan/15c65886482c5d9895543537034cec3d/) [[11.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/the-construction-and-use-of-cahokia’s-grand-plaza-alt-kruchten/ab2dc369b6095b45ab31d49586d5b1af/).

**Planning and labor.** Building one-quarter to one-third of the Grand Plaza potentially required more than 10,000 person-days; this is a labor estimate, not a population count [[11.4]](https://consensus.app/papers/the-construction-and-use-of-cahokia’s-grand-plaza-alt-kruchten/ab2dc369b6095b45ab31d49586d5b1af/). Regional clustering and intervisibility support coordinated placement, but the authors present shared decision-making as an interpretation rather than proof of a particular government [[28.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/constructing-greater-cahokia-site-concentrations-and-vilbig-sagan/14c0361f9c6d5b5a9812ce738b3812b8/) [[28.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/constructing-greater-cahokia-site-concentrations-and-vilbig-sagan/14c0361f9c6d5b5a9812ce738b3812b8/).

**Neighborhoods and storage.** Architectural diversity cautions against treating every building concentration as residential: some zones contained specialized craft or religious architecture [[29.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/elements-of-cahokian-neighborhoods-betzenhauser-pauketat/fb798149192052e5943d3c20dfc76332/). Household research explicitly includes storage/processing pits, debris distributions, and domestic–ceremonial arrangements, but the supplied passages cannot establish capacities, citywide granary totals, or centralized surplus administration [[30.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/cahokias-countryside-household-archaeology-settlement-mehrer/ce3e83a33b9a515eac5f95a2fe91f96a/) [[30.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/cahokias-countryside-household-archaeology-settlement-mehrer/ce3e83a33b9a515eac5f95a2fe91f96a/).

**Figure 3:** American Bottom communities and documented spatial relationships

**East St. Louis.** The compound burned in one conflagration; ritual commemoration, aggression, and accident remain alternatives, with circumstantial evidence favoring ritual commemoration [[31.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/a-mississippian-conflagration-at-east-st-louis-and-its-pauketat-fortier/451302866c655f74b131d9a7426dae51/). Subsequent reductions in construction place the event within a wider transformation, not an independently demonstrated military destruction of Greater Cahokia [[31.4]](https://consensus.app/papers/a-mississippian-conflagration-at-east-st-louis-and-its-pauketat-fortier/451302866c655f74b131d9a7426dae51/).

**St. Louis.** Surviving plats, lithographs, and drawings permit probable mound-location reconstruction, but celestial interpretations remain a separate analytical step [[32.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/in-search-of-the-st-louis-mound-group-archaeoastronomic-and-romain/224836158322530a8d6094daf31eaaeb/). Big Mound’s copper long-nosed mask supplies an artifact connection rather than a political boundary [[37.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/cahokia-interaction-with-contemporary-southeastern-and-griffin/d888edb484d55fbfa45a14839b3595cc/).

**Mitchell.** The preserved post base lay in an extraction ramp within a four-meter-deep pit [[9.4]](https://consensus.app/papers/age-and-origin-of-a-cahokian-wooden-monument-at-the-mitchell-kessler-benson/11f2b085b7845b8793645afb9f4f3230/). A 1124 cutting date fits best, but 1122–1126 and 1129–1132 cannot be statistically excluded; neither cutting nor inferred later removal directly dates mound construction [[9.5]](https://consensus.app/papers/age-and-origin-of-a-cahokian-wooden-monument-at-the-mitchell-kessler-benson/11f2b085b7845b8793645afb9f4f3230/) [[9.6]](https://consensus.app/papers/age-and-origin-of-a-cahokian-wooden-monument-at-the-mitchell-kessler-benson/11f2b085b7845b8793645afb9f4f3230/).

**Emerald.** Ceramic and architectural evidence identifies at least five short occupations around 1020–1200, including rebuilding associated with approximately 1050 [[38.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/pilgrimage-and-the-construction-of-cahokia-a-view-from-the-skousen/01fd4a85b7c95c6aba5b6189a73e4873/) [[38.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/pilgrimage-and-the-construction-of-cahokia-a-view-from-the-skousen/01fd4a85b7c95c6aba5b6189a73e4873/). Short-term domiciles, shrines, feasting, and renewed monuments underpin the pilgrimage interpretation; lunar-standstill scheduling is a further hypothesis [[38.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/pilgrimage-and-the-construction-of-cahokia-a-view-from-the-skousen/01fd4a85b7c95c6aba5b6189a73e4873/) [[38.4]](https://consensus.app/papers/pilgrimage-and-the-construction-of-cahokia-a-view-from-the-skousen/01fd4a85b7c95c6aba5b6189a73e4873/).

**Washausen.** Bayesian modeling suggests major occupation lasted potentially thirty years or less, with rapid departure beginning around 1100 or shortly afterward [[39.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/town-aggregation-and-abandonment-during-the-era-of-urban-barrier/30ffa0e711e657cb998615bf76010352/) [[39.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/town-aggregation-and-abandonment-during-the-era-of-urban-barrier/30ffa0e711e657cb998615bf76010352/). Its contraction while larger centers approached population maxima argues against mapping all regional sites as simultaneously flourishing [[39.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/town-aggregation-and-abandonment-during-the-era-of-urban-barrier/30ffa0e711e657cb998615bf76010352/).

**Kane and rural communities.** Legacy analyses resolve occupation and mortuary histories extending from approximately 500 BCE into the fourteenth century, including local people and immigrants [[40.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/employing-biomolecular-analyses-and-radiocarbon-ams-fort-hedman/89298601d1aa51e59bbe6dd1a77ee216/). Rural planning has been interpreted as comparatively logistical and autonomous, illustrating disagreement with strongly centralized regional models [[30.5]](https://consensus.app/papers/cahokias-countryside-household-archaeology-settlement-mehrer/ce3e83a33b9a515eac5f95a2fe91f96a/) [[30.6]](https://consensus.app/papers/cahokias-countryside-household-archaeology-settlement-mehrer/ce3e83a33b9a515eac5f95a2fe91f96a/).

## Religion, Language, and Persistence

Ritual interpretations include pilgrimage, public posts, mortuary transformation, and celestial landscapes, but specific deity identifications require qualification [[38.5]](https://consensus.app/papers/pilgrimage-and-the-construction-of-cahokia-a-view-from-the-skousen/01fd4a85b7c95c6aba5b6189a73e4873/) [[9.10]](https://consensus.app/papers/age-and-origin-of-a-cahokian-wooden-monument-at-the-mitchell-kessler-benson/11f2b085b7845b8793645afb9f4f3230/). Milky Way and solar/lunar alignment arguments combine landscape measurement with ethnographic analogy; they are interpretations, not surviving Cahokian doctrinal texts [[24.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/following-the-milky-way-path-of-souls-romain/54097d69239c5e15bea02286f5a7b81b/) [[66.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/archaeoastronomy-and-landscape-archaeology-at-cahokia-romain/1a67c66a837a53a088c22355869dc637/).

Picture Cave research incorporates conversations with Dhegiha-speaking elders, including Osage, Quapaw, and Kansa, and interprets imagery through southern Siouan traditions [[67.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/ancient-ritual-behavior-as-reflected-in-the-imagery-at-diaz-granádos-duncan/23a40feef8ce54b2b2398553cf605f36/) [[67.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/ancient-ritual-behavior-as-reflected-in-the-imagery-at-diaz-granádos-duncan/23a40feef8ce54b2b2398553cf605f36/). This does **not** supply a site-wide linguistic identification for Cahokia; the available evidence supports neither one ancient language nor one homogeneous ethnicity.

One-third of sampled individuals in the strontium study were nonlocal, with multiple apparent origins; that is a sampled mortuary result, not an exact immigrant proportion for the entire city [[68.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/immigrants-at-the-mississippian-polity-of-cahokia-slater-hedman/97dcaf89d5465366bef8cea2d66b230d/). Isotopic provenance is not genomics, and neither should be presented as Indigenous political identity. Descendant-community collaboration and sovereignty should govern heritage interpretation rather than archaeological classification alone [[69.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/archaeology-and-social-justice-in-native-america-laluk-montgomery/7976a3d34b1d5f40a5e65573f6193ab3/).
