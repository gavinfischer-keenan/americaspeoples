Windward Islands and Barbados

Ceramic-age settlements and changes in fishing connect Grenada, Carriacou and Saint Lucia. Records from Barbados and Saint Lucia trace resistance and emancipation in the plantation era.

Coasts and ceramic-age communities

On Grenada, a dated shell at Pearls places Indigenous activity centuries before the contact-era observations that dominate this portion of the atlas. Two coastal communities on Carriacou add a different kind of evidence. The deposits at Grand Bay and Sabazan contain pottery and remains of meals. Study of fish bones at Sabazan shows the breadth and changing composition of local catches. People worked these shores over many generations, with the archaeological contexts capturing some visits and activities more clearly than others. Source 1 ↗ Source 2 ↗ Source 3 ↗

Saint Lucian villages and plantations

Lavoutte in northern Saint Lucia preserves part of a Late Ceramic Age village and burial landscape. In a later era, Saint Lucian plantations drew on enslaved labor. A government heritage account for the Soufrière district gives the scale of the slave plantation economy at emancipation. Local records from each island clarify how people used different landscapes within a seafaring region. Source 1 ↗ Source 2 ↗

Barbados resistance

On Barbados, people enslaved on plantations organized the 1816 rebellion that took Bussa’s name. The account links Bayley’s Plantation to other places, including Golden Grove. The district-level pin introduces a history distributed across much more of the island than one plantation. It also gives the emancipation-era record from Saint Lucia a contemporary regional context of political action and resistance. Source 1 ↗ Source 2 ↗

Dating the islands’ evidence

Shell and charcoal dates require different checks. The shell from Pearls has a wide calibrated interval and limited contextual detail. At Sabazan and Grand Bay, several excavations and analyses establish broad periods of use, within which separate layers can register change. Ranges for those middens are much less precise than the year recorded in colonial documents. Source 1 ↗ Source 2 ↗ Source 3 ↗

Martinique — revolt and local emancipation

An uprising at Saint-Pierre on 22 May 1848 was followed by the governor’s emancipation proclamation on 23 May, before the official bearer of the French decree arrived.
