Mesa Verde

The cliff dwellings are one part of Mesa Verde's settlement history. Mesa-top houses, farming communities, reservoirs, and ceremonial buildings reveal a landscape inhabited and repeatedly transformed over centuries.

Individual cliff dwellings

NPS dates most cliff-dwelling construction to about 1180–1300 CE. Nearby seep springs helped make alcoves attractive as water supplies became less dependable. These were varied villages with living rooms, granaries, communal spaces, and kivas, fitted to particular rock faces and available space. Source 1 ↗

Mesa-top places deserve equal attention

Far View shows that the move into alcoves was not universal. Some families continued living on the mesa top while nearby cliff villages were occupied. Fields, houses, water storage, and paths connected these places; a simple progression from mesa-top settlement to complete cliff occupation obscures that coexistence. Source 1 ↗

Individually reviewed atlas evidence

Mesa Verde — Ancestral Pueblo settlement landscape

Mesa Verde preserves Ancestral Pueblo pithouses, masonry settlements, farming features and cliff dwellings. NPS summarizes occupation of this landscape at about 550–1300 CE. The long interval covers changing settlement forms; it does not mean cliff dwellings existed throughout. UNESCO recognizes a continuing relationship between this heritage and Pueblo peoples today.

Source date guidance: NPS Places page: c. 550–1300 CE. UNESCO Outstanding Universal Value gives a broader c. 450–1300 landscape chronology.
Dating method: Official synthesis of archaeological settlement, architecture and associated material culture; these are approximate phase limits, not a newly calculated age model.

The park-wide range is not the chronology of any one structure. NPS and UNESCO differ by about a century on the beginning of the broad sequence. The point is a property locator. A final regional occupation date cannot be translated into the extinction of a people.

Central Mesa Verde — modeled late-thirteenth-century decline

Kemp and colleagues summarize regional models showing central Mesa Verde population peaking in the mid-1200s, declining by 1260 and reaching near-complete local farmer departure around 1280.

Source date guidance: Mid-1200s peak; decline by 1260 CE; model approaches zero about 1280 CE.
Dating method: Reviewed approximate source chronology; occurrence/context envelope, not continuous occupation

Secondary summary of demographic models, not a fresh census or exact date of every last visit. Scope is central Mesa Verde farmers, not all Indigenous people.
