Island Melanesia & Lapita: Obsidian Corridors, Villages & Chiefdoms

Inhabitants of New Ireland and Manus crossed open waters 45,000–21,000 years ago; millennia later, Lapita outrigger fleets fused local obsidian networks with Austronesian pottery to reach Vanuatu and New Caledonia in a historical blink.

Bismarcks (Talasea, Watom, Lou, Anir), Kilu/Solomons (Roviana hillforts), Teouma (Vanuatu), and Koné (New Caledonia).

Obsidian fingerprints & Remote Oceanic leapfrogs

Pleistocene explorers first carried Talasea and Lou obsidian between New Ireland and Manus more than 20,000 years ago. From ~1200–1000 BCE, Lapita canoes pushed these standardized volcanic-glass blades over 3,000 km southeast into Vanuatu (Teouma), New Caledonia (Koné), and Fiji. [17] [1] [18] [19]

Subsequent genetic and archaeological turnovers in Vanuatu and the Solomon Islands generated culturally rich, defended Melanesian polities—exemplified by Roviana Lagoon coral hillforts and long-distance war-canoe networks. [11] [13] [7] [20]

Bismarck origins: from 40 ka reefs to Makekur & Watom

Deep limestone caves on New Ireland and Manus establish over 40,000 years of open-water adaptation before Lapita stilt villages crystallized at Makekur (Arawe) and Watom Island around 3,300–2,700 BP. [26] [2] [4]

Post-Lapita transformations & Solomon hillfort chiefdoms

Genetic sampling at Teouma combined with chemical pottery sourcing charts rapid post-Lapita Papuan admixture across Vanuatu, culminating millennia later in fortified New Georgia lagoons and *tomoko* war-canoe chiefdoms in the Solomons. [11] [27] [7]
