Korea: Paleolithic Valleys, Three Kingdoms Gold, Metal Type & Joseon Mountain Citadels

Positioned on a mountainous peninsula jutting between Northeast Asia's steppe frontier and the Japanese archipelago, Korean societies paired granite mountain-ridge engineering and unmatched East Asian movable metal-type printing with agile coastal navigation.

Seokjang-ni & Jeongok-ri, Bibong-ri dugout & Bangudae cliffs, Gochang dolmens, Pyongyang Anhak & Goguryeo forts, Gongju–Iksan Baekje, Gyeongju Bulguksa–Seokguram, Byokrando–Kaesong, Cheongju *Jikji*, and Hansando–Namhansanseong–Hwaseong.

Pleistocene river terraces and the world's early maritime whalers

Multi-horizon quartz assemblages at Jeongok-ri and Seokjang-ni along the Hantan and Geum rivers establish deep Pleistocene settlement across Korea's western lowlands prior to Yellow Sea post-glacial flooding. [1] [5] [6]

By 6000 BCE at Bibong-ri, wetland dwellers paddled carved pine dugouts while coastal communities at Bangudae chipped >300 scenes of whales, net-fishing craft, and harpoons—later raising the planet's densest concentration of Bronze Age megalithic dolmens. [7] [8] [15]

Goguryeo mountain forts, Baekje granite craft and Silla gold

Rather than enclosing sprawling flat plains, Goguryeo (Ji'an and Pyongyang Taesong) and Baekje (Gongsanseong, Buyeo, Iksan) crowned rugged ridges with dry-stone and rammed-earth fortresses while shipping silk, gilt bronze, and Buddhism overseas to Japan. [9] [10] [2] [16] [12]

Bio-anthropological and ancient genomic analyses of 6th-century Silla Gyeongju tombs reveal elite kinship cohesion alongside vibrant Eurasian Silk Road imports reflected in Roman glass, gold filigree crowns, and Seokguram's stone dome. [13] [3] [11]

Goryeo Byokrando celadon ports and 1377 Jikji movable metal type

Under Goryeo (918–1392), Byokrando harbor near Kaesong welcomed Song Chinese and Middle Eastern ships while state artisans perfected jade-green celadon, the 81,258 woodblocks of *Tripitaka Koreana*, and movable bronze-alloy type attested in 1234 and surviving 1377 *Jikji*. [4] [15]

Joseon Hangeul, Imjin naval defense and Hwaseong

From King Sejong's phonetic Hangeul script and Andong clan academies to Admiral Yi Sun-sin's coastal Imjin War victories (1592–1598), Namhansanseong's 12-km mountain bastion, and Suwon Hwaseong's 1794 *Silhak* brick-and-stone ramparts, late Joseon fused Confucian scholarship with military engineering. [4] [14]
