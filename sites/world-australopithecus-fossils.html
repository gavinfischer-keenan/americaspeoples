Australopithecus: Every Published Fossil Locality Across Africa

Between roughly 4.3 million and 1.98 million years ago, hominins of the genus Australopithecus radiated across eastern, central, and southern Africa. Here every published fossil-bearing formation, footprint bed, and cave chamber is catalogued with specimen numbers, radiometric/cosmogenic dating methods, and active taxonomic and chronological debates.

All 39 mapped Australopithecus localities and stratigraphic horizons across Ethiopia, Kenya, Tanzania, Chad, and South Africa (~5.0–1.98 Ma).

Origins & Australopithecus anamensis (5.0–3.8 Ma)

Fragmentary cf. anamensis jaws at Lothagam (~5.0–4.2 Ma) and Tabarin (~4.45 Ma) precede the first rich radiation of Australopithecus anamensis across the East African Rift between ~4.3 Ma (Ileret mandible, Lewis et al. 2024) and ~3.8 Ma (Woranso-Mille MRD cranium). Along the Awash and Turkana rivers—at Kanapoi (type site, 4.17–4.07 Ma), Asa Issie (4.2–4.1 Ma directly above Ardipithecus ramidus), Fejej, Galili, and Allia Bay—thickened molar enamel and human-like hindlimb tibiae confirm habitual bipedalism in woodland-bushland corridors. [22] [23] [26] [29]

Mid-Pliocene Radiation: A. afarensis, A. deyiremeda, A. bahrelghazali & Late Survivors (3.85–2.5 Ma)

Australopithecus afarensis endured for nearly a million years from Belohdelie (~3.89 Ma) and Laetoli (3.85–3.46 Ma) through Woranso-Mille KSD-VP-1/1 ('Kadanuumuu'), Maka (3.4 Ma), Dikika ('Selam'), Hadar (AL 129, Lucy AL 288-1, AL 333 'First Family'), Omo Usno/Shungura, Koobi Fora Tulu Bor, Chemeron, and Kantis near Nairobi (~1,740 m upland Rift shoulder). Yet Woranso-Mille proves mid-Pliocene diversity: between 3.5 and 3.3 Ma, A. afarensis lived side-by-side with A. deyiremeda, whose climbing-adapted Burtele foot was firmly attributed in 2025. [30] [37] [34]

Far west in Chad, the Koro Toro sites (KT 12 'Abel', KT 13, KT 40; ~3.58 Ma) reveal an australopith population (A. bahrelghazali) flourishing along Lake Chad woodland-sedgelands 2,500 km from the Rift. Later still, after 2.8 Ma, Bouri yields megadont A. garhi (~2.5 Ma) while Villmoare et al. (2025) documented a late non-afarensis Australopithecus overlapping chronologically with earliest Homo at Ledi-Geraru (~2.78–2.59 Ma). [35] [40]

Southern African Karst Caves & Dating Controversies (3.76–1.98 Ma)

Southern Africa's Australopithecus fossils accumulated inside dolomitic limestone sinkholes at Taung, Makapansgat, Sterkfontein (Jacovec Cavern, Member 2 Silberberg 'Little Foot', Member 4 Sts 5 'Mrs. Ples', Member 5), Gladysvale, and Malapa (1.977 Ma A. sediba). Because caves lack volcanic tuffs, chronology hinges on uranium-lead flowstone dating, mammalian biochronology (~3.0–2.0 Ma for Taung/Makapansgat/Member 4), and 26Al/10Be cosmogenic nuclide burial ages pushing Member 2 'Little Foot' (~3.67 Ma), Jacovec (~3.76 Ma), and Member 4 (~3.4 Ma) back to the Mid-Pliocene. [42] [47] [46] [51] [52]

Alongside age disputes, Ronald Clarke and colleagues distinguish two southern species—A. prometheus (StW 573 'Little Foot' and Makapansgat) and A. africanus—whereas Beaudet (2023) and others interpret the Sterkfontein variation as a single variable species. Newly verified 2025 enamel palaeoproteomic peptides from Member 4 open new biomolecular windows into South African australopiths. [48] [49]
