New Guinea & Papua: Highland Agriculture, Bird's Head Pioneers, Hiri & the Kula Ring

Joined to Australia throughout most of the Ice Age as northern Sahul, New Guinea became an independent center of highland agriculture and later anchored some of the Pacific's largest maritime exchange rings.

Ivane Valley, Kuk Swamp, Walufeni/Simbai Highlands, Lachitu, Auwim East Sepik, Caution Bay/Motupore Hiri, and Massim Kula ring.

Highland agriculture & coastal maritime hubs

In the Wahgi Valley (Kuk Swamp) and Southern Highlands, Papuan societies independently cleared montane forests, hunted, and dug drainage ditches by 4,500 BP while exchanging marine shells far inland over misty passes. [4] [5] [19] [20]

Along coastal New Guinea, Lapita mariners reached Caution Bay and Giligilina ~2,900 BP before local specialization generated three legendary maritime systems: the Hiri *lagatoi* sago-pottery trade in the Papuan Gulf, Bel–Siassi Voyagers across Vitiaz Strait, and the Massim *Kula* Ring. [21] [22] [12] [23]

Upper Karawari caves & highland-to-coast links

Rock-art shelters at Auwim along the Upper Karawari River and interior caves such as Walufeni preserve marine shell wealth and shared highland pottery motifs connecting remote mountain ridges to coastal estuaries. [2] [19] [7]

Post-Lapita pottery towns & Massim sociopolitics

From Caution Bay and Emo along southern Papua to Milne Bay island ossuaries, ceramic sourcing and genome-wide surveys trace centuries of evolving maritime kinship underpinning the Hiri and Kula networks. [22] [14] [26]
