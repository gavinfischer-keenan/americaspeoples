Wallacea to Sahul: Early Hominins, Sea Crossings & Deep-Time Pioneers

Even at glacial low sea stands, deep oceanic trenches separated Southeast Asia (Sunda) from Australia and New Guinea (Sahul). Yet hominins reached Flores over one million years ago and Homo sapiens sailed east to Raja Ampat and Arnhem Land before 50,000 years ago.

Archaic Wallacean hominins (Wolo Sege, Mata Menge, Liang Bua), Sulawesi karst art, pelagic fisheries in Timor and Alor, Denisovan genomic admixture, and the Northern Route gateway at Mololo Cave.

The whole area in view

Basin and karst cave localities bridging Wallacea and northern Sahul; coastlines show modern sea levels rather than exposed Pleistocene continental shelves.

  1. Wolo Sege & Mata Menge (Flores)

    c. 1.02 Ma–700 ka

    Stone tools at Wolo Sege and miniature early Middle Pleistocene mandibles/humerus at Mata Menge reveal diminutive hominins settled Flores well before Homo sapiens. [1] [2] [3] [4]

    -8.69°, 121.10°

  2. Liang Bua Cave (Flores)

    c. 190–50 ka

    Limestone cavern yielding Homo floresiensis skeletons (~100–60 ka) and stone industries ending around a 61 ka regional drying pulse. [5] [6] [7] [8] [9]

    -8.53°, 120.44°

  3. Walanae Basin & Maros Karst (Sulawesi)

    >1.04 Ma tools; ≥67.8 ka art

    Early Pleistocene river-terrace artifacts (>1 Ma) plus the world's oldest dated cave hand stencils and figurative scenes in Maros–Pangkep karsts. [10] [11] [12] [13]

    -4.95°, 119.65°

  4. Asitau Kuru (Timor) & Tron Bon Lei (Alor)

    c. 44–7 ka

    Deep maritime caves documenting ~44 ka pelagic tuna capture, rotating shell fishhooks, and Pleistocene obsidian transfers among Lesser Sunda islands. [14] [15] [16] [17]

    -8.35°, 125.00°

  5. Mololo Cave (Waigeo, Raja Ampat)

    c. 55–50 ka

    Equatorial karst shelter recording the earliest radiocarbon-dated human plant-resin processing and foraging along the Northern Route into Sahul. [18] [19] [20]

    -0.30°, 130.73°

Archaic island hominins across Wallace's Line

Archaeological horizons in the So'a Basin of Flores (Wolo Sege, 1.02 Ma) and Sulawesi's Walanae Valley prove that early hominins repeatedly crossed open deep-water channels without terrestrial bridges. By 700,000 years ago at Mata Menge, isolated selective pressures had already produced tiny hominins ancestral to Homo floresiensis. [1] [2] [10] [21]

Revised stratigraphy at Liang Bua shows that Homo floresiensis disappeared shortly after 60–50 ka amid monsoon aridification—right around the window when modern human voyagers and ancient Denisovan-introgressed lineages crossed Wallacea toward Sahul. [7] [8] [22] [23]

Oceanographic corridors into Sahul

Agent-based drift and least-cost voyaging models show both a Northern Route (Kalimantan → Sulawesi → Moluccas → Bird's Head New Guinea) and a Southern Route (Bali → Flores → Timor → NW Australia Shelf), substantiated directly by 55–50 ka tree-resin finds at Mololo Cave and 67.8–44 ka sites in Sulawesi, Timor, and Alor. [19] [18] [11] [14]

Denisovan admixture & island paleogenomics

Whole-genome studies across Wallacea, Papua New Guinea, and Australia show that Sahul ancestors received multiple pulses of archaic Denisovan DNA along island bottlenecks, alongside middle-Holocene hunter-gatherer genetic links excavated in Sulawesi. [23] [22] [30]
