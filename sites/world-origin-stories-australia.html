Indigenous Australian Origin Stories: The Dreaming (Tjukurpa, Wangarr, Bugarrigarra, Nyitting & Lutruwita)

This guide belongs to the cultural, sacred and literary record — answering how diverse Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander nations understand where land, law, ancestors and human life came from, distinct from the archaeological map elsewhere in this atlas. Rather than a single uniform story, Australia holds hundreds of interconnected Creation systems known locally as Tjukurpa (Western Desert), Alcheringa / Altjira (Arrernte), Wangarr (Yolŋu Arnhem Land), Ungud / Wunan (Kimberley), Bugarrigarra (West Kimberley), Nyitting (Noongar Southwest), and Palawa/Zenadth Kes traditions.

Publicly documented creation narratives spanning northern Arnhem Land and Tiwi Islands, Central and Western Deserts, Kimberley and Pilbara, Murray–Darling and Victoria, Southwest Australia, Tasmania (Lutruwita), and the Torres Strait. Custodianship and restricted ceremonial tiers remain solely with Traditional Owners.

1. What 'The Dreaming' means: the Everywhen, Country & Songlines

Across Australia, 'The Dreaming' is an English approximation for complex Indigenous terms—including Tjukurpa (Anangu/Western Desert), Altjira or Alcheringa (Arrernte), Wangarr (Yolŋu), Ungud or Lalai (Ngarinyin/Worrorra), Bugarrigarra (Karajarri/Yawuru), and Nyitting (Noongar)—that describe not an unreal dream or a closed past chapter, but what anthropologist W. E. H. Stanner called the 'Everywhen': the foundational epoch in which Ancestral Beings emerged from subterranean earth, ocean horizons, or sky realms to travel, fight, sing, camp, give birth, and leave living Law inside every rock outcrop, spring, tree, and star cluster. [7] [6]

People in these traditions did not descend from distant foreign continents; rather, their spiritual essence, clan moieties, totems, and languages were deposited directly into particular stretches of Country by Creator Ancestors whose physical bodies transformed into landmarks, red ochre seams, freshwater billabongs, or constellations. Singing a Songline across thousands of kilometers recalls those exact creative footsteps—keeping human beings, landforms, and ancestors continuously linked. [6] [1]

2. Northern Coasts & Top End: Djanggawul, Wawalag, Rainbow Serpent & Tiwi Mudungkala

In Yolŋu Wangarr accounts of northeastern Arnhem Land, two sacred Djanggawul (Djang'kawu) Sisters arrived from the spirit-island of Baralku beyond the eastern sunrise, paddling their bark canoe onto the white sands of Yalangbara at Port Bradshaw. Carrying sacred conical mats (nganmarra) and carved mawa digging sticks, they pierced the barren coastal dunes to bring up permanent freshwater springs—before giving birth to the first Yolŋu human clans across successive sacred camps and entrusting named land, songs, and languages to each generation. [7]

A companion Top End cycle follows the Wawalag (Wagilak) Sisters, who traveled north toward Arnhem coasts naming plants, animals, and clan law until pitching camp near the Mirarrmina waterhole inhabited by Wititj / Yurlunggur, the Giant Olive Python / Rainbow Serpent. Enraged when blood and taboo disturbance entered his deep spring, Yurlunggur coiled skyward into towering monsoon thunderheads, deluged the bush, and swallowed the sisters and children—regurgitating them in the rhythmic cycle of dry burning season and torrential wet monsoon that still structures ceremony across Arnhem Land. [7]

Further west across Kunwinjku (Gunwinggu) and Iwaidja Country, Creator Mother Yingarna / Warramurrungundji crossed the sea carrying woven dillybags stuffed with yams, digging sticks, and spirit-children (nganjuk/wird), planting distinct Aboriginal languages clan by clan while Ngalyod the Rainbow Serpent excavated deep sandstone gorges. Out on Bathurst and Melville Islands (Tiwi Islands), Creation began in unbroken blackness when blind elder woman Mudungkala crawled on knees out of dark mud, her trailing fluid cutting the Clarence and Apsley Straits as she bore three children (Wuriupranala the sun-woman, Murupiangkala, and Tipaklipa)—creating the sunlit islands before Purukapali's grief over his son Jinani brought death for the first time into Tiwi life. [7]

3. Central & Western Deserts: Numbakulla, Arrernte Inapatua, Seven Sisters & Uluru

Documented extensively across Arrernte (Central Australian) traditions, the world first stretched under shallow salt waters from which rugged MacDonnell ridge peaks (*Tjoritja*) jutted upward. Creator ancestor Numbakulla emerged with two sky companions and saw incomplete, huddled, silent bundles of half-formed beings (*Inapatua* / *Rela manerinja*) curled beside salt lagoons—their eyes sealed, mouths unopened, and limbs joined like uncarved stone. Descending with curved *tula* flint knives, the ancestors slit open eyelids and ears, separated individual fingers and toes, breathed fire, bestowed circum-rite names, and sent clans outward as fully formed Arrernte peoples along Totemic tracts (*altyerrenge*). [1] [7]

Spanning more than 2,000 kilometers from the Pilbara and Martu salt lakes across Ngaanyatjarra, Pitjantjatjara, and Yankunytjatjara lands (highlighted by the National Museum of Australia's Anangu-led Songlines exhibition), the Kungkarangkalpa (Seven Sisters) fled north, east, and south from the relentless shape-shifting pursuer Wati Nyiru / Yula. Everywhere the sisters camped, dug soakages, danced, or concealed themselves inside sandstone caves—such as Cave Hill in APY Lands and Kumpupirntily (Lake Disappointment)—the desert received waterholes, edible berries, and rocky hills before the sisters hurled themselves into the night sky as the Pleiades star cluster, with Orion still trailing eternally behind. [6]

At Uluru itself, every cleft, stain, and cave encodes competing Anangu Tjukurpa ancestors: the peaceful Woma Python Woman **Kuniya**, whose eggs and digging stick fought the ruthless Poison-Snake warrior **Liru** at Mutitjulu Waterhole (staining black streaks down the southern rock face), balanced along Uluru's northern walls by **Mala** (Rufous Hare-Wallaby) ancestors holding ceremony until attacked by the vengeance-monster **Kurpany** sent because of broken ritual protocol, and **Lungkata** (Blue-Tongued Lizard Man), whose greed left blackened emu meat slabs jutting from rock overhangs. [6]

4. Kimberley, Pilbara & Southwest Noongar: Wanjina Clouds, Bugarrigarra & Waugal

In the northern Kimberley (Worrorra, Wunambal, and Ngarinyin Lalai Law), primordial freshwater snake **Ungud** partnered with cloud-and-rain **Wanjina** beings descending from sky storms. Having shaped rocky gorges, baobab trees, rivers, and wet-season cloudbursts—and imparted marriage moieties, stone-point law, and sharing ethics—the Wanjina walked into sandstone rock overhangs and dissolved their bodies into permanent painted white-ochre outlines with striking dark haloed eyes and deliberately zero mouths (for if a Wanjina ever spoke in anger, unceasing floods would swallow Earth). Under the shelter pools, Ungud stores unborn baby-spirits found by fathers in dreams. [7]

Southward along the Karajarri, Yawuru, and Pilbara *Bugarrigarra*, the twin brothers **Bagadjimbiri** rose from earth in the gray pre-dawn, calling all creatures by name—from kangaroo to echidna and wild plum—while their long hair formed stratus clouds and their bullroarer ceremonies traveled across coastal dunes. Along Australia's opposite southwest corner, **Noongar *Nyitting*** ('Ice-Cold Dreaming Time') teaches how the monumental hairy Rainbow Serpent **Waugal (Waakal)** crawled out of deep limestone caverns and tore winding river channels down the Darling Scarp—including *Derbarl Yerrigan* (the Swan River), Murray, and Blackwood rivers—leaving granite domes where it rested and giving Noongar elders the sacred charge to guard freshwater aquifers. [7] [5]

5. Southeast Australia (Bunjil, Baiame, Ngurunderi & Budj Bim), Lutruwita (Tasmania) & Torres Strait

Across the Kulin nations of Victoria, Creator Spirit **Bunjil** (Wedge-Tailed Eagle) crafted the mountains and streams around Port Phillip before molding two human forms from dark clay mixed along creek banks while his water-spirit brother **Pallyan** churned riverbank mud until two women emerged. Breathing life into nostrils, mouths, and navels (*Karween* and *Kulin* first ancestors), Bunjil gave the people Kulin sharing Law, fire from **Kararkarook** and **Bimbeal**'s star-sticks, and ascended to **Altair** on a mighty whirlwind with **Waa** (Crow) and his families; west in Gunditjmara Country, ancestor **Budj Bim** turned himself into Mount Eccles volcanic cone whose teeth and eyebrows forged channels guiding abundant eels (*kooyang*). [3] [8]

Further north across Wiradjuri, Kamilaroi (Gamilaraay), and Murray–Darling basins, **Baiame** and his son **Daramulum** walked Earth creating stone fish traps at **Ngunnhu (Brewarrina)** and **Bora** initiation rings before ascending to the sky beside the Emu-in-the-Sky dark dust constellation; meanwhile along South Australia's Murray River and Coorong, **Ngarrindjeri** hero **Ngurunderi** pursued the colossal Murray Cod **Ponde** down a tiny creek whose frantic thrashing widened the whole Murray gorge and Lake Alexandrina—spearing Ponde at the Murray Mouth, cutting his flesh into all freshwater and sea fish species, and summoning tidal floods that cut off **Karta** (Kangaroo Island) before rising skyward into **Waierri**. [2] [5]

On **Lutruwita (Tasmania)**, Nuenonne elder Woorraddy of Bruny Island narrated to 1830s chronicler George Augustus Robinson the striking Palawa creation of humans at **Louisa Bay / Cox Bight**: great sky-spirit **Moinee**, defeated in heaven by brother **Droemerdeenne**, crashed down onto southern Tasmania's rocky coast shaping valleys and hills. First molding human people (*Parlevar*) out of earth, Moinee inadvertently shaped them standing stiffly upright with kangaroo tails and no knee joints—making it painful to sit or rest—until **Droemerdeenne** heard their misery, descended, chipped off the rigid tails with sharp flint, smeared healing fat behind their legs to loosen human knees, and rose afterward into **Canopus** while **Moinee** hardened into towering coastal rock. Out across the coral sea in **Zenadth Kes (Torres Strait)**, **Tagai** guides sea calendars across a giant constellation canoe, **Gelam**'s transformed dugong body shaped volcanic soil on Mer, and **Malo–Bomai** swam east across eight island reefs to anchor island Law (*'Keep your hands off another man's garden'*). [4] [7]
