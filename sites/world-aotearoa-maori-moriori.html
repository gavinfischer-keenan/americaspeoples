Aotearoa & Rēkohu: Polynesian Settlement, Pounamu Networks, Pā Fortifications & Wars

Aotearoa New Zealand and Rēkohu (Chatham Islands) were Earth's last major habitable landmasses reached by humans, settling c. 1280–1450 CE through skilled two-way double-hulled canoe voyages.

Early moa-hunting coastal towns (Wairau Bar, Shag River Mouth), Raoul Island Kermadecs, Mayor Island & Northland obsidian, Westland *pounamu*, *pā* fortifications, 19th-century wars, and Rēkohu.

Rapid colonization, exchange & fortification

Archaeology at Wairau Bar, Ahuahu, Rakaia, and Raoul Island records an extraordinarily interconnected early phase (c. 1280–1450 CE): Mayor Island obsidian and South Island argillite/pounamu traveled thousands of kilometres across coasts and alpine passes. [4] [20] [21] [8]

By c. 1450–1500 CE, loss of megafauna and sweet-potato intensification coincided with widespread earthwork *pā* hillfort construction, later transformed into anti-cannon trench complexes during the Musket Wars and British-imperial Waikato/Taranaki Wars. [22] [23] [12] [24]

Tūhua obsidian & West Coast *pounamu* exchange

Social-network XRF analyses track rapid maritime distribution of Mayor Island (*Tūhua*) obsidian alongside Northland Kaeo glass and South Island *pounamu* (nephrite jade) across both islands from 1280 CE onward. [4] [8] [21]

Musket Wars, imperial *pā* engineering & Rēkohu

Early 19th-century NSW firearms imports transformed inter-*iwi* warfare before Māori engineers re-designed *pā* trenches to defeat British artillery during the 1845–1872 New Zealand Wars, while isolated Moriori endured the 1835 invasion of Rēkohu. [11] [12] [17]
