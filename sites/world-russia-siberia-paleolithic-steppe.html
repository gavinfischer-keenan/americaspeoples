Siberia, the Urals & Altai: Ice Age Mammoth Hunters to Scythian Frozen Barrows

Far from a peripheral Ice Age wasteland, Siberia and the Ural corridor nourished Denisovans, easternmost Neanderthals, Ancient North Eurasians at Mal'ta, and iron-forging Scythian–Saka horse confederations.

Urals to Beringia: Kapova Cave ochre art, Altai caves, Irkutsk Angara–Baikal terraces, Lower Amur early ceramics, and Altai–Tuva royal kurgans.

Three lineages across the Altai–Ural corridor

Multi-layered caves across the northwestern Altai foothills preserve rare genetic overlap between Denisovans, late-arriving Neanderthals at Okladnikov and Chagyrskaya, and early Upper Paleolithic Homo sapiens. [6] [7]

Farther north by 32,000 years ago along the Yana River inside the Arctic Circle and later around Mal'ta west of Lake Baikal, mobile mammoth-steppe groups carved bone needles, tailored fur parkas, and bird-and-Venus ivory figurines belonging to an Ancient North Eurasian lineage. [1] [8] [4]

Holocene Uralic–Yeniseian taiga worlds and early ceramics

Ancient genomics across West Siberia and Lake Baikal now maps how Early Holocene hunter-gatherers—already crafting sculpted larch shrines like the Shigir Idol in the Urals and coarse salmon-cooking pots along the Amur—diversified into Uralic- and Yeniseian-speaking groups. [2] [10] [14]

Along the Angara and Vitim gorges, multi-horizon camps such as Ust-Yodarma II and Kovrizhka IV show long seasonal fishing and hematite rituals that fed Cis-Baikal nephrite networks. [15] [16] [17]

Chariots, Minusinsk bronzes and frozen Pazyryk barrows

Starting around 2100 BCE in the southern Urals (Sintashta–Arkaim), fortified circular settlements pioneered spoked-wheel horse chariots before Seima–Turbino tin-bronze smiths spread socketed spearheads across the Irtysh and Ob. [2] [5]

Between 800 and 300 BCE, royal kurgans in Tuva (Arzhan), Khakassia (Salbyk), and Altai mountain permafrost (Pazyryk, Bashadar) fused Scythian animal-style goldwork with Iranian textiles and Chinese lacquer. [3] [18]

Arctic sled-dogs, Beringia and subarctic resilience

At 76°N on Zhokhov Island (~9,000 BP), islanders selectively bred harnessed sled-dogs and hauled Anadyr–Chukotka obsidian 1,500 km across sea ice, laying technological foundations for Ekven–Uelen and North Pacific maritime peoples. [11] [12] [1]

Subarctic permafrost sites such as Zeleny Yar and Ust-Polui near the Ob mouth show northern Samoyedic and Ugric traders exchanging Siberian furs south for Iranian silver dishes centuries before Muscovite expansion. [14] [2]
