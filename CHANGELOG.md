Release notes, newest first. ## 0.32.0 and earlier


The world catalogue adds a playable branching human-family framework, a genetics/interbreeding guide, four dated studies (Genghis Khan, Marco Polo, Silk Roads and Hannibal), and 130 new African evidence observations through 15,000 BCE. The focused Africa map retains the calendar cutoff, dated site phases, source access and uncertainty. Shared map navigation keeps settings compact and facts visible. This is a substantially expanded, source-backed first inventory, not an exhaustive census of sites or peoples.

The world overview and shared **Region** menu open Americas, Africa & human origins, Europe & Mediterranean, and Asia & Pacific. All four regions have playable maps with separate clocks and saved camera state. Americas and northern Europe retain their detailed historical players; Africa and Asia & Pacific begin with the sourced human-family framework and linked deep dives. Ordinary map timelines end in **1900 inclusive**; Hawaiʻi statehood alone retains 1959. Later historical records remain in the research archive.

Northern Europe begins with **396 dated entries across 14 regions**, **362 sourced political geometry versions**, and **25 connected canonical voyages**. Trade, roads, railways, settlement observations and warfare have distinct evidence and display rules. The 75 new warfare entries cover Norse raids, battles, sieges and internal conflicts, including opposing Scottish factions. Seventeen population observations support town sizes; interpolation is explicit and limited to comparable observations. These are selected histories with documented gaps, not a complete inventory of every event or boundary.

**Nineteen new deep dives** cover the northern regions, the Picts and the emergence of Alba, Ötzi, other cold-preserved people and Andean summit children. Each has a full-area terrain map; six licensed photographs support the cold-preservation studies. The combined world-guide collection now contains 60 studies. [Northern coverage and next research priorities](docs/northern-europe-next-pass.md).

The preceding world expansion added **40 regional guides and one shared writing guide**, with **87 historical originals paired with published English readings**. The writing collection includes 13 examples from the Americas, from Classic Maya cacao vessels to colonial wills, accounts, and a petition. The three regional voyage guides reuse **251 documented stops across 80 existing itineraries**. [World deep dives and sources](docs/world-deep-dives.md).

The shared movie uses clearer movement and trade paths, quieter overview terrain and expandable groups where places crowd together. During playback, graphics emphasize connections and a few dated changes. Pausing reveals counts and detail while preserving the date, camera and selection. Symbol and arrow size improve readability; they do not encode population or traffic volume.

The previous maritime release added **38 new trade observations, an enriched Lake Huron connection, 14 itineraries and 145 dated voyage records**, with a Norse North Atlantic story, separate Greenland settlement endings and Iceland continuity through 1934. The shipwreck topic contains **7,512 loss records** from public catalogues and reviewed archaeology. Six maritime graveyard guides join Northwest Passage, southern-ocean route and L’Anse aux Meadows guides, with large maps, photographs and qualified seasonal/weather context. Place previews describe what is there; the ice layer uses explicitly labeled interpolation. [Sources and limits](docs/maritime-trade-expansion.md).

The earlier v0.28 release added 87 dated journeys and phases. The current catalogue contains **175 itineraries and 126 dated activity periods**, with 58 reviewed inventory entries and remaining questions retained. [Coverage and route uncertainty](docs/voyage-coverage-review.md) remain explicit.

The preceding release filled **15 previously unreviewed regional gaps**, adding 70 shared evidence records, 15 connected guides and 15 contextual stories. [The ranked review](docs/coverage-15-review.md) gives the places, selection limits and before/after locality counts.

The combined guide collection provides 187 labeled topographic frames, with 3,600-pixel terrain images and 4,800-pixel images for Chaco and Machu Picchu. Whole-area frames precede local details where needed. Six Chaco road-survey sheets, the historic Machu Picchu relief survey and other original maps form 26 additional credited map assets. Zoom, full-screen viewing and original-source links preserve useful detail. Source sampling and survey accuracy are stated separately from image size.

The Americas atlas contains **11,201 records (11,128 shared, 73 focused)**, 28 independent movies, 84 contextual stories and 90 guides. There are 39 existing individually credited guide media items alongside the maps, plus six new world-guide photographs. This campaign used no Consensus calls. [Full coverage audit](docs/world-coverage-audit.md) · [Sources, attributions and licenses](sites/sources-attributions.html).

A gap between dated discoveries retains earlier place evidence. It does not establish abandonment or automatically prove continuous occupation. Source-supported enduring communities remain inhabited across their documented history. Explicit departures receive a separate cue, such as Kiritimati’s reported 1905–1912 occupation gap. Trade and migration still follow their own supported activity windows.

The preceding eastern Brazil campaign added 30 records across 28 archaeological localities, 12 contextual chapters and two trade chapters. [Its evidence and coverage](docs/eastern-brazil.md) remain available alongside the new regional history.

The earlier history collection added 25 foundation/arrival milestones, 16 migration/exchange records, eight maritime loss records, and five regional habitation intervals. Earlier occupation markers remain as explicitly labeled history; active routes respect separate activity windows, and losses accumulate only after their dates. Colonial colors identify founding attribution, not ongoing sovereignty. The v0.9 expansion adds Pacific and Australian evidence, Caribbean and Latin American settlements, all 21 Alta California mission foundations, violence, forced Atlantic transport, Acadian migration stages, dated voyages, built railways, and dotted historical borders. See [the expansion guide](docs/atlas-expansion.md) and [the history update](docs/history-update.md) for source and display limits.

The v0.17 release adds 37 shared observations across early Americas and Pacific continuity, plus detailed Caral/Supe, Kuk and Upano guides with contextual movies. Existing movies and guides are reorganized under Deep dives: Subjects, Places, Languages and Cultures, with Coming soon lists. There are 2,017 shared records and 58 focused records. At completion of v0.17, cumulative Consensus Deep usage was **70/100 calls**, including polls. See [evidence and navigation changes](docs/early-continuity.md).

The v0.15 update adds 43 shared observations at 33 locality IDs: Central American communities, resistance and refugee settlement; South American towns, labor and conflicts; and Australian local histories and working landscapes. The master atlas contains 1,945 shared records, with 58 focused topic records unchanged. At completion of v0.15, Consensus Deep usage was 58/100 calls, including polls. See [regional evidence and source decisions](docs/regional-gap-evidence.md).

The v0.14 update completed all ten then-requested dives: forced relocations, Hawaiʻi and statehood, territorial purchases, Pacific fur trades, whaling, Spanish treasure, missionaries, pottery, obsidian and copper. Twenty independent movies now contain 184 chapters. One hundred new observations include 88 shared records and 12 focused records; four later Hawaiian political events stay outside the shared 1934 endpoint. Eight Australia/Aotearoa archaeological observations and sixteen targeted gap observations improve local coverage. That completed the previous topic queue, not its historical research. The subsequent Australian wool/pastoral and mining movies are now included; their finer coverage remains part of [continuing research](docs/roadmap.md). See [coverage audit](docs/world-coverage-audit.md), [land and sovereignty](docs/land-sovereignty-dives.md), and [trade, missions and materials](docs/remaining-topic-dives.md).

The v0.13 update adds Haudenosaunee history and lacrosse, Amazonian peoples and languages, and Caribbean societies and forced African migration. These three movies add 31 chapters and 26 records, including 24 shared historical records. Ten independent topics contain 83 chapters. See [community history evidence and coverage](docs/community-topic-dives.md).

The v0.12 update adds sweet-potato, boats/voyaging and dog movies, 25 sourced records, reciprocal links to shared history, and a linked queue of later dives. Seven independent topics now contain 52 chapters. Tablet and computer screens are the primary targets. See [Pacific topic evidence and coverage](docs/pacific-topic-dives.md).

The v0.11 update adds 473 source-linked records: 41 city and local-phase records, 401 mapped encounters, three northern evidence records and 28 topic-only evidence records. Four independent Topic Dives follow horses, maize, trade networks and languages; the ordinary atlas keeps its shared world and saved position. The Peoples of the North story and expanded chapter explanations remain contextual. See [the topic update](docs/cities-conflicts-topic-dives.md) and [coverage ledger](docs/cities-conflicts-coverage.md).

The v0.10 update restores an automatically playing, shared-date Americas movie. Every story and expedition retains the surrounding historical world. Modern topographic relief, deeper local zoom, individual archaeological places, local chronology, and separate date/location precision accompany 70 new sourced records. See [the shared history update](docs/shared-history-update.md) for Hawaiian conflicts, Pacific connections, local settlement coverage and remaining gaps.

The previous collection holds 962 records. Ten regional Consensus DEEP reviews were run sequentially, followed by original-source checks and geographic reconciliation:68 new findings and7 explicit revisions, including corrections to site classification and chronology.64 additions have supported calendar windows and36 have named-locality anchors; these are records, not68 new settlements.442 bibliographic leads retain their discovery trail, with held claims and source-access limitations explicit. Full generated reports and quotations remain private; published records cite the underlying sources. The separate author-supplied colonial registry remains visibly marked **AI generated facts**, with conflicting population columns excluded. See docs/round12-synthesis.html and docs/coverage-audit.html.

The new places/cultures collection adds twelve populated pages and five research-source access notices. The Chaco and Inca culture pages include schematic geographic road-network maps, and culture pages introduce ritual, languages and historical transformation. Raw third-party research reports are retained privately; public pages link to independently checked sources. The earlier archaeological collection is retained; selected location and interpretation corrections are documented in [the source-correction guide](docs/history-source-corrections.md). NOAA/NCEI contributes 1,580 historical earthquake entries, alongside existing eruptions and tsunamis; a master Natural events control preserves individual selections and keeps ENSO independent.

The browser renders a compact map catalog and handles smooth timeline playback. SQLite handles authoritative evidence/source storage and indexed queries. Full reviews and large climate snapshots are loaded on demand, rather than compiled into JavaScript. The separately sourced physical catalog contains8896 hazard/ENSO entries and147 ice-scenario snapshots. Geographic/time coverage, uncertainties and source-access limits remain explicit.
